# Desk Scene Explainer Playbook (v2, "Text Behind the Head, Elevated" edition, for Claude Opus 5.5)

> **In this plugin:** this file is the whole playbook: every measured value and recipe from the 5 reference reels is in it, and there is no other playbook file to open. Frame counts are measured at the references' **25 fps**: convert with t = f / 25 when you draw (`frame(t)` runs at the render fps). Positions are on **1080×1920** (ts1–ts3 were 720×1280 sources, scaled ×1.5; ts4–ts5 were native 1080×1920). The per-reel notes this file cites (`ts1…ts5`) are not shipped. Sounds are picked by id from SOUNDS.md; commands are in the edit skill's REFERENCE.md. The page gives you `FACE(n)` and `FACE0` (with `head_top` and `chin`) for every speaker frame.

**Purpose:** you (Claude) receive a talking-head video plus its script. Use this document to **ideate and build** every layout, scene, behind-the-head title, caption, transition and sound, then edit the Reel so it looks and sounds like a high-energy, scene-cut creator explainer: **the speaker talks fast at his desk, the big claim stands behind his head, and every step, number or story beat cuts to its own picture**, built with such craft that the viewer can't scroll: the words behind him land like slams, the numbers roll and hit, the steps pile up in front of their eyes, and he keeps moving through the frame.

**What v2 is:** a frame-by-frame analysis of 5 finished reels by an Indian creator-educator who teaches students to earn with skills and AI (58–89 s each): ts1 "0 to 5L/month as a student" (a 5-step freelancing method), ts2 "5 crazy things to build in free time" (a 5-level build roadmap), ts3 "Best skills to learn before 2026 ends" (3 skills, dark cinematic), ts4 "The best purchase of my life" (an office proof montage), ts5 "What are you doing this summer" (a money claim proved with real students), **elevated ten times** by the best motion designer and creative director in the world: the behind-the-head grammar kept whole, with the poster living in real depth behind him, every number rolling and slamming on its word, one anchor scene that grows into the reel's payoff, layout moves that glide instead of jumping, every key word punched, and a fully scored sound design with no music. The analysis covered cut lists, framing and layout measurements, caption timing, motion-graphic frame recipes and audio, plus contact sheets of every reel. All measured layouts, recipes and rules are kept. Where a reference reel and a direction disagree, **the directions win**.
- Every rule below is either **observed** (seen in the reels, the most consistent value chosen as the house standard) or a **fix**: something a reel got wrong that this playbook forbids.
- **This style is not one layout.** One creator, one desk set, one caption system and one temperament carry **four formats** (§3): a step-scene method, a dark chapter map with a divided screen, a proof montage, and a proof-story. Pick the format from the script.
- **The speaker moves.** He is full frame at frame 0 and for every opinion and ending; he **disappears** for full-screen scenes, **glides into the bottom box** of a split while a table or document needs reading, **shrinks into a photo card** for a branching choice, and **becomes a chat bubble** for a share ask, then comes back full screen (§3.0a). He is never parked in one box for the reel.
- **The whole face is always visible** at every zoom, punch-in, crop, card and bubble (hair to chin). How far a zoom goes is decided by the face, not the frame. Captions may cross the face (§2.1 M3). **The text behind him never hides his face:** it sits behind the head, the hair and the shoulders, never in front of them.

**Measured sizes are floors, not targets.** The references set table rows, node labels and UI text at 15–35 px, parked a 370 px photo card and a 320 px chat bubble on big pages, and left a small folder and a small QR on empty canvases. On a phone that reads as empty or unreadable. So:
- **Full screen or a box, never any footage or picture over its own blur** (Naman, 13 Oct): the creator's video, a screen recording, a landscape clip, a photo or a diagram either fills the whole frame edge to edge (cover-crop to the part that matters, or a push-in), or sits in a designed box (rounded, rimmed or softly shadowed, on this style's own ground, colour, gradient, texture, grid or drawn set) that it fills completely. Never shrink anything inside the frame over a blurred or darkened copy of itself, never pad a box, band or panel with blurred side bars, never set a card on the creator's own blurred frame. A tight selfie stays full screen as shot: move the text, not the video (eyes to chin always in; the hair may touch the top edge). When the whole head must show, use a box sized so the footage fills it with the head inside. (A matte cut-out over a plate is fine: it has no box edge.)
- **Captions:** the base caption **100–110 px** (never below 90), the two-tier key word **160–180 px**, the lead-in tier **56–60 px**; captions sit **just under the chin** on full frame (§5.2).
- **The hero of a beat** (the anchor scene's object, the card, the page, the disc, the folder, the table, the QR) is **≥ 760 px wide** (x ≈ 160–920) and owns the middle of the frame; a group (choice cards, a stat grid, icon tiles, the disc row) fills **x ≈ 80–1000**.
- **Labels:** anything that carries meaning **≥ 48 px**; table rows ≥ 52 px; node labels ≥ 64 px; titles on scene sheets ≥ 72 px.
- **No empty frame:** if more than half of y 200–1500 is plain background for more than ~0.7 s, push in, bring the next element, or bring him back.
- **The face stays big:** a tight selfie stays full screen as shot (eyes to chin always in; the hair may touch the top edge): move the poster and the text, never shrink the video over its own blur; in a split box, card or bubble the footage fills the box, cropped on the face; never a narrow pillar. The photo card is ≥ 560 px wide and the bubble ≥ 600 px wide (§3.6).

**Never pale.** The creator's footage keeps its warm, contrasty colour with deep blacks: never desaturated, faded or lifted. B/W is only for the **past** (other people's then-shots, ts5) and the one 0.6–1 s own-face reaction beat (P-22); everything about *now* is in full, rich colour.

**How to use this file:**
- Follow §1 (procedure) in order.
- Look up visuals by ID: formats `FMT-…`, layouts `L-…`, hook formulas `HF-…`, hook visuals `H-…`, structure devices `SD-…`, graphic families `G-…`, patterns `P-…`, elevated devices `EV-…`, transitions `T-…`, endings `E-…`.
- §2's craft never bends. Everything else is a default you may adapt, with a one-line reason.

### The feel (read this twice)
This is **an older brother at his desk who has already done it, telling you exactly how, at full speed**. The claim is huge behind his head the second he opens his mouth: "₹5,00,000", "18 YO", "3 SKILLS". Every time he says "step two" the screen snaps to the same spotlight desk and another sheet drops onto the pile, so you *see* how far you've come and how much is left. Numbers roll like a slot machine and slam to a stop on the syllable. The key word of every line punches up huge under his chin. When there's something to read, he glides down into the bottom of the frame and the table drops in above him. It feels **fast, ambitious and doable**: high energy, but clear; the voice never shouts, **the edit does the shouting**. Every slam has a hit, every scene arrives with a whoosh, every number rolls and lands. If it looks like a talking head with captions, it's wrong; if it looks like a hype montage with no method, it's wrong too.

**Who the reference creator is:** a young, fast, animated Indian creator in his mid-twenties who teaches students how to earn with skills, freelancing and AI tools, and runs his own agency and courses. He talks to the lens from his desk like an older brother who has done it: wide eyes, big hand gestures, props in his hands (glasses of coloured water, poker chips, a polaroid, paper cards he throws away, a laptop). English with an Indian delivery, ₹ amounts in lakh and crore. Energy is high but the voice is clear; he never shouts. The edit does the shouting.

**Audience (ICP):** Indian students and early earners aged roughly 16–24: college kids, freelancers, first-job seekers. Phone-first, impatient, ambitious, money-curious. They want **a specific number, a specific method and proof that people like them did it**, in under 90 seconds, with a clear next step.

**Style lineage:** a premium "creator explainer" edit built from four layers:
1. **Speaker layer:** one tripod setup at a desk (home studio, or an office), wide-ish lens, slight high angle, re-framed digitally with punch-ins and punch-outs between phrases. Breaths and pauses removed (3.5–4.0 words/s). He is full frame, absent, or moved into a box/card/bubble on a beat.
2. **Poster layer:** giant condensed type or a neon number **behind his head** (he is matted in front of it), built word by word as he says it: "18 YO", "3 SKILLS", "5 YEARS", "₹1,20,000", "99.9%".
3. **Scene layer:** full-screen, faceless motion scenes that carry the structure: one recurring **anchor scene** per list item (a spotlight desk where each step's sheet drops on the pile, a 3D coin per level, a nebula map that pans to the next node), plus glass UI cards, diagrams, light "product pages", filmed B-roll of real people, and a QR card.
4. **Caption layer:** white bold sans, 1–3 words at a time, appending on each spoken word, with a **two-tier emphasis** (a small lead-in over one huge key word), and the occasional gradient-wiped word.

## §1 Procedure (follow in order)

1. **Inventory.** Probe the footage. Identify the setup (§12.1) and measure the head (head-top y, chin y, eye line, face x, head height): **this decides every crop and punch-in limit** (M3).
2. **Transcribe** with word timestamps. Captions are every spoken word as said (English, or romanised Hinglish as spoken), spelled from the script. Check the first words.
3. **Find the numbers, the list and the people:** every number with its onset (M5), the list items and their ordinal words ("Step one", "Number two", "here's a…"), every person or thing that can be shown.
4. **Choose the format** (§3.0): numbered method → `FMT-STEPS`; a few skills/ideas with something to read → `FMT-MAP`; proof by many examples → `FMT-MONTAGE`; proof by people's stories → `FMT-STORY`. One line on why. The poster and the two-tier caption are shared; the anchor scene and palette stay one per reel.
5. **Plan the speaker map** (§3.0a): every beat full / absent / split-bottom / card / bubble / B-roll, and how he gets there (a glide, EV-5, or a cut on a hard onset). Full at frame 0 and for the last line; no off-camera run > ~6 s; no TH run > ~5 s without a punch, poster word or cut.
6. **Segment:** `HOOK` (0–5 s), `SETUP` (stakes/open loop), `STEP-n` / `SKILL-n` / `ITEM-n` / `STORY-n`, `TURN`, `RECAP` (optional), `CTA`, `END`.
7. **Classify every clause** (§8.4): its trigger word and its caption chunks (1–3 words; which word is big).
8. **Ideate 3 hooks** (§6.0): formula, frame 0 (speaker mid-word with the poster already landing, never empty), the first 3 s of events (≥ 6), the claim number's slam, the first captions, the loop as a picture. Recommend one.
9. **Fill the beat sheet** (§13): one beat per visual change (every 1.4–2.2 s).
10. **Plan transitions** (§9.3) and check the budget (§9.4).
11. **Produce the assets** (§8.6): drawn scenes and cards; the user's own footage and screens; credited photos for a named subject; never a third-party plug.
12. **The 10× pass and the score** (§8.0, §8.7, §11): for every beat, its hero and how it anticipates, lands and follows through; where the living poster, the number slams, the carry-throughs, the growing anchor, the glides, the key-word punches, the throw-throughs and the item rail go; then score every event and log the sound ledger. **Attack the plan:** where is he gone for more than ~5 s? Where is a frame half empty, a label small, a card a postage stamp? Where does a number appear without its roll and slam, or a poster word without its hit? Where does the picture sit still? Fix it in the plan.
13. **Show the creator** the plan and stills (§13.3).
14. **Build section by section,** look at stills of every landing frame and every punch-in (§15), score, render (§16).

## §2 Craft that never bends
- **The whole face is always visible:** hair to chin at every zoom, punch-in, split box, card and bubble (M3); a tight selfie stays full screen as shot (eyes to chin in; the hair may touch the top edge), never shrunk over its own blur, never a pillar; every box is filled by the footage. The poster sits **behind** the head and hair, never in front of the face. Captions may cross the face.
- **Text never sits on text:** no caption over a poster digit, a table row, a label or the QR; no graphic or sticker over the face (N1).
- **Sound in sync:** every SFX on the frame its picture lands (a poster word on its onset, a roll's hit on the last syllable ±2 f), never on a consonant; nothing louder than the voice.
- **True and exact:** shown numbers equal spoken ones, names spelled one way, no invented testimonial, headline or comment (M11, N9); the CTA is the user's own (M12).
- **Readable at phone size:** captions ≥ 90 px (house 100–110), key words ≥ 160 px, labels ≥ 48 px, table rows ≥ 52 px, the hero ≥ 760 px wide.
- **Loudness:** −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1.0 dBTP; end clean (M14).

Everything else is direction. You decide; write your reasons into the plan.

### 2.1 The house grammar (what makes it this style)
Not checks: this is what the five reference reels do every time, and why it works. Break one only with a reason in the plan. (The IDs are kept because other sections cite them.)
- M1. **One format per reel** (§3.0) with a written reason, **and the speaker moves** (§3.0a): full frame at frame 0, absent for scenes, gliding into the split's bottom box / a photo card / a chat bubble when the format calls for it (EV-5), back full frame for opinions, turns and the last line. Never one box for the whole reel.
- M2. **Frame 0 is never empty.** The speaker (mid-word, mid-gesture) or the real thing (a prop, a photo) is sharp at f0, the first caption by f2, the first poster/prop event by ~1 s. Fixes: ts4 opened on a defocused blur (sharp by f5); ts5 opened mid-whip with the hair out of frame; ts1's first caption came at f5. This edition: the poster's first glyph or kicker is already landing on f0 (§6.0).
- M3. **The whole face is always visible: hair to chin, at every zoom, punch-in, crop, card, split box and bubble.** How far a punch-in goes is decided by the face: stop where the hair top keeps ≥ 40 px of air under the frame (or box) edge and the chin stays inside. **When the footage is tighter than the face allows** (a handheld selfie close-up, a wide-lens close), **keep it full screen as shot and skip the punch-ins** (eyes to chin always in; the hair may touch the top edge), and size any split box, card or bubble so the footage fills it with the whole head inside (§12.4); never shrink it over a blurred copy of itself, never a narrow pillar. Captions may cross the face; graphics, stickers and props drawn by the edit may not. Fixes: ts4 punch-ins (8.0, 10.5–11.3 s) put the hair on the top edge and 39.5–41.2 s cut the forehead; ts2 32–33 s and ts5 18.0 s cut the hair; ts5's handheld-feel close at 3.0–4.5 s cut the head at the top and left; ts1's hook push (1.0–3.0 s) left only a mouth and chin.
- M4. **Pauses and breaths are cut** (3.5–4.0 words/s, no gap > 0.4 s), except **one** deliberate 0.3–0.7 s beat before a punchline or the FOMO turn (ts2 20.0 s, ts5 38.5 s). `FMT-MONTAGE` may keep up to three 0.5–1.0 s pauses as beats (ts4 filled them with music; we don't: SFX only).
- M5. **Every spoken number lands as a picture on its word:** a count-up or slot-roll behind the head or on a prop, a giant numeral, a slider or ruler; rolls finish on the last syllable (±3 f), type-ons appear on the onset (0–2 f); this edition lands every one with the number slam (EV-2).
- M6. **Every list item gets the same anchor** (ts1 spotlight sheet ×5, ts2 level coin ×5, ts3 map pan ×4), showing progress (pile, disc row, next node); the last visit lights the whole anchor as the payoff (EV-4).
- M7. **Cuts land on word onsets, mid-sentence** (ts1 34/37, ts2 33/39 scored cuts inside words). Average shot 1.4–2.2 s; a TH stretch > ~5 s needs a punch, poster word or prop action (longest observed 5.0–5.6 s).
- M8. **Captions are every spoken word, appending word by word** (1–3 words, cleared per phrase), white bold sans, soft shadow, no box, **just under the chin** on full frame, with a **two-tier** key word (§5.3). Base ≥ 90 px (house 100–110; the references ran 60–115), key word ≥ 160 px (observed 150–175).
- M9. **Every label that carries meaning is ≥ 48 px** (table rows ≥ 52 px, node labels ≥ 64 px); smaller text is texture only and the caption carries the words. Fixes: UI/slider/table/node/curriculum text at 15–35 px in ts1, ts3, ts4, ts5.
- M10. **Poster type is matted behind the head;** the head may cover the middle of a long word but **never a digit and never a key word's first letter** (fix: ts2 "18 YO" and ts1 "24.0%" read "18 _O" / "2_ _0%"; place numbers above or beside the head). The poster is **never** composited in front of the face or the hair.
- M11. **Facts are exact and the same everywhere:** the shown number equals the spoken one (fix: ts2 said "500 crores" over an article reading "$50M"); names spelled one way (fix: ts2 ZACH/ZACK, ts4 "feilds"); everything from the script or a cited source.
- M12. **The CTA slot is the user's own:** QR card, code/offer card, "scan this" page and share ask carry the user's QR, link, keyword or offer, built in this kit (§6.7).
- M13. **Audio −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1.0 dBTP.** Fix: the masters were −9.5…−10.3 LUFS with true peaks +0.8…+4.8 dBFS.
- M14. **End clean:** hard stop ≤ 6 f after the last word, or a designed end card ≤ 3.2 s.
- M15. **₹ with Indian grouping** (₹5,00,000; ₹1.2 lakh; 500 crores), or the user's currency.
- M16. **Never pale:** the creator's footage keeps its own warm, contrasty colour with deep blacks, or a touch richer; B/W only for the past (other people's then-shots) and the ≤ 1 s own-face reaction (P-22).

**What this style never does:**
- N1. Graphics, stickers or props drawn **over the face**; in-world numbers go behind the head or below the chin.
- N2. A stray insert frame (fix: ts1's 2 f phone-UI + black frame at 46.8 s).
- N3. A film/TV clip, a film meme or someone else's footage (fix: ts5 at 40.0 s); the reaction beat is the user's own face (P-22).
- N4. A third-party product plug or platform credit (fix: ts1 and ts2 plugged a vibe-coding platform); tools and brands appear only when the script names them as the subject.
- N5. Near-empty frames: a small object on a big canvas, tiny text on a white card, letterbox bands (fixes: ts4 "I mean" card, ts5 small folder, letterboxed cards, half-empty QR page). Hero objects **≥ 760 px wide** (the references' floor was 50% of the width), groups filling x 80–1000, or keep the speaker on screen (the split).
- N6. A pause > 0.4 s inside a phrase, or a dead pause with no beat reason.
- N7. Two palettes or two caption systems in one reel.
- N8. Generic stock "tech" footage; B-roll is the user's own or the real subject.
- N9. Typos, a spoken/shown number mismatch, or a fake comment/headline.
- N10. A true peak above −1.0 dBTP or a master hotter than −14 LUFS.
- N11. Pale footage: the creator desaturated, faded, lifted or washed as a look (M16).
- N12. Music: a bed, drone, beat or music outro under the voice or the end card, ever (§11).

## §3 Formats, the speaker's movement, layouts, safe zones

### 3.0 Choosing the format
| The script… | Format | Why |
|---|---|---|

### 4.4 Meaning of colour

- **White** = what he says (captions, poster type). **Black on lime** = a label to remember.

## §6 Hook system

### 6.0 The 10× hook
- **Frame 0 is three layers deep:** the speaker full frame, sharp, mid-word and mid-gesture (whole face, rich colour), a prop in hand if the hook has one; the first caption word already on just under the chin; the **poster already landing** behind his head (the kicker or the first glyph mid-punch, never a blank wall).
- **The claim number slams by ~1 s** (EV-2): the roll or count lands on its last syllable with a hit and a glow flash, behind the head or tracked on the prop.
- **≥ 6 changes by 3 s** (the references measured 5–6): the poster building word by word, the number's slam, a prop push or a tile pop, a key-word punch, the first punch-in on an onset.
- **The loop as a picture by 6–10 s:** the item rail (EV-8) appears with the count said in the hook (3 tiles, 5 chips), the last one locked or blurred with "??"; a failure stat counts up behind the head; it stays visible until it's paid.
- **The first scene by ~3.4–5.4 s, seamlessly:** the poster word carries into it (EV-3) or the camera's push continues into the anchor scene (EV-4), so the first change of world feels like one move.

### 6.1 What the hook must do in about 3 seconds
1. **Show the person and the claim at once:** the speaker sharp at f0, the claim's number or audience call-out building behind his head by ~1 s.
2. **Make it specific:** a rupee amount, an age, a count, a deadline ("₹5 lakh a month", "18 year old", "16 to 20", "3 skills before this year ends", "₹1.2 lakh without a degree").
3. **Open a loop by ~5–10 s:** a failure stat ("99.99% will fail before step three"), a twist ("the surprising part"), a confession ("something I don't usually talk about"), "watch till the end", "and the weird part…".
4. **5–6 visual changes in 3 s** (measured 5, 5, 5, 5, 6); this edition **≥ 6** (§6.0).

### 6.2 Hook formulas (`HF`)
| ID | Formula | Spoken pattern | Seen in |
|---|---|---|---|

### 7.2 The item ritual (identical for every list item)
1. **Ordinal word** ("Step three", "Number two", "Level four", "here's one more"): hard cut or flash to the anchor scene (SD-1) on its onset, or the face's push continuing into it (EV-4); the item's name lands with its word (sheet label, coin label, node label types on ~8 f); the item rail lights the next item (EV-8).
2. **Explain (full frame):** back to the speaker, a different framing from the last TH shot (punch-in ↔ punch-out); two-tier caption on the item's key word.
3. **Show (1–3 pictures, one per clause):** cards, a diagram node, a page with a highlighter label, a split table, a B-roll shot, a behind-head number.
4. **Land (full frame):** the item's one-line takeaway on the face, often with a sub-boom on the punchline (ts1 25.0 s "no one's gonna work with you").

### 7.3 Open loops (retention)
- The **number of items** is said in the hook and shown (the 5 chips, the 3 tiles, the 5 discs, the 3-node map), and stays visible as the item rail (EV-8).
- A **failure stat** or **twist** at 5–12 s; a **"watch till the end"** before the midpoint, paid with the CTA.
- The **remaining items** stay visible in the anchor scene (pile, disc row, map).
- Every loop is paid on screen with the same picture that opened it (step three's sheet; the folder → QR); the anchor's last visit lights every item at once (EV-4).

## §8 Graphics, scene and B-roll system

### 8.0 The 10× layer (what the best motion designer adds to every beat)
**Animation principles, applied to this style:**
| Principle | How it shows up here |
|---|---|
| **Anticipation** | A poster word drops in from 108% with a 2-frame darkening of the wall behind it; a sheet tilts back 3° before it falls; a card dips to 96% before it pops; the camera eases into a punch a beat before the punchline |
| **Squash & weight** | Poster words and numbers land at 112% → 98% → 100% (EV-1, EV-2); the key word at 118% → 100% (EV-6); sheets settle on the pile with a 2-frame squash and a dust puff of light; the coin's flip lands with a 4% bounce |
| **Follow-through & overlap** | Stacked poster lines land 3 f apart, the last word biggest; the split's edge overshoots 4 f and settles; glass cards fan in with 2 f between them; the light sweep keeps travelling after the word has landed |
| **Arcs** | The shrink into the card or bubble travels on a shallow arc; thrown cards spin on an arc (EV-7); the map pan curves toward the next node; the cursor arcs to its click |
| **Slow in / slow out** | Ease-out arrivals (cubic), ease-in exits, ease-in-out glides and pans; only caption words, cuts and flashes are hard |
| **Staging** | One hero: the poster behind him dims to 70% while a two-tier key word punches; in scenes, everything but the active sheet, node or card dims to 40% |
| **Secondary action** | While held: the poster drifts slower than him (parallax), dust floats in the spotlight cone, the coin turns slowly, nebula particles drift, the last digit of a held number pulses |
| **Timing** | A new picture every 1.4–2.2 s; poster words on their onsets; glides 8–12 f; number rolls 12–40 f landing on the last syllable; never two box moves within 1 s |
| **Exaggeration** | One moment per reel goes huge: the money number fills the wall behind him, the full pile lights up, the map's last node flares |
| **Appeal** | Deep navy or nebula blacks, warm spotlight gold, crisp white condensed type, neon numbers with real glow; it looks like the most expensive career channel in India made it |

**Design hierarchy (every frame):** 1. the face, or the hero of the scene (the sheet, the coin, the node, the card, the number) · 2. the key word (two-tier) or the poster's biggest word · 3. the base caption · 4. the label or the item rail · 5. everything else, dimmed to 30–40%.

**Effects toolkit:** a real person matte with a 2–3 px feather and a rim light in the poster's colour; parallax depth between the room, the poster and the speaker; glow on neon numbers and node lines; light sweeps across poster words, key words and the QR; depth blur and drifting dust in anchor scenes; motion blur on slot-rolls, whips and thrown cards; grain only on B/W past shots; the glitch, invert and white flashes as punctuation within the budget (§9.4). Never: stock flares, film burns, shake on the speaker, desaturating the creator.

### 8.5 Density and variety
- A new picture every **1.4–2.2 s** (ts5 1.4, ts4 1.5, ts2/ts3 1.9, ts1 2.2); ~27–45 visual changes per reel.
- Talking-head shots alternate framings (wide ↔ ×1.3 ↔ ×1.4 within the M3 limit); posters and captions keep a long TH alive (≤ ~5.6 s).
- Variety comes from **new pictures of the content**, not new effects: the anchor scene repeats exactly (and grows, EV-4), the caption system never changes.
- **Scale:** the hero ≥ 760 px wide and owning the middle; groups fill x 80–1000; no frame more than half empty for over ~0.7 s (the header's floors).

### 8.6 Asset rules
- **Drawn in code:** anchor scenes (spotlight desk, coin, map: 2.5D with gradients, glow, depth blur), glass cards, diagrams, sliders, pages, tables, folders, chat UI, QR and end cards.
- **The user's own:** footage, props, B-roll, screenshots and screen recordings of what they use or made; photos of people they name (supplied).
- **Fetched (credited in the asset list):** a real photo or article of a named story subject; public-domain engravings for a flicker montage. Never film/TV clips (N3), never a logo-plug (N4).
- **Brands and tools** only when the script names them as the subject; real marks, unaltered.
- **Never invent** a number, a headline, a comment or a testimonial.

## §10 Motion tokens, camera, layers, finishing

### 10.1 Motion tokens (25 fps)
- **Trigger:** on the word onset, or 1–3 f before it. Caption words: hard pop, 0 f, no scale. Type-on numerals: one glyph per word/digit, instant.
- **Numbers:** count-up 20–40 f ease-out (last digits settle slowly, from a random lower value); slot-roll ~12 f with vertical blur, then a soft pulse while holding.
- **Entrances:** defocus-in 4 f; slide-in 2–4 f with heavy horizontal blur then a slow drift; disc from a dot ~6 f (~10% overshoot); 3D spin ~10 f; coin flip ~10 f; sheet drift ~1.5 s; pill expand ~12 f; node label type-on ~8 f; branch line ~8 f each; file into folder ~10 f; tile glow sweep ~6 f.
- **Gradient word:** 2–3 f wipe L → R, lightening over ~6 f.
- **Speaker moves:** bubble shrink ~5 f ease-out (this edition 8 f on an arc); horizontal push ~8 f.
- **This edition (EV tokens):**
| Device | Tokens |
|---|---|
| Living poster (EV-1) | Word drop 108% → 98% → 100% over 4 f; wall darken 2 f; light sweep 8 f; parallax ~60% of the speaker's drift; rim glow 6–10 px |
| Number slam (EV-2) | Roll 12–40 f ease-out-back, ~3% overshoot; land punch 108% → 100% over 2 f; glow flash 3 f; unit +2 f |
| Carry-through (EV-3) | Flight 10 f ease-in-out, motion blur; land punch 2 f |
| Glides (EV-5) | Split slide 10 f ease-in-out, edge overshoot 4 f; shrink 8–12 f on an arc; grow back 10 f |
| Key-word punch (EV-6) | 118% → 100% over 3 f; sweep within 6 f; lead-in rise 12 px over 4 f |
| Throw-through (EV-7) | Spin 25–40° on an arc, 6–8 f, the card fills the lens for 2 f |
| Item rail (EV-8) | Tile pop 96% → 106% → 100% over 5 f; light-up 4 f; slide-off 8 f |
- **Scenes:** particles converge ~1 s, disintegrate ~12 f; icon orbit ~45°/s; end-card letters lock in ~10 f.

### 10.2 Camera
- **Digital punch-ins/outs between phrases** (×1.3, ×1.4–1.5), never a slow Ken Burns on the speaker; the M3 limit decides the maximum. A punch that leads into a scene keeps its direction into the scene's dolly (EV-4).
- **Virtual moves on props** (push/whip/pull with motion blur) in the hook only.
- **Scenes have a camera:** drift in the anchor scenes, fly-through in diagrams, pans across card rows and the map.
- Photos of a story subject get a slow push (Ken Burns) for their 1–2 s.

### 10.3 Layer order (back to front)
1. Background plate (the room) **or** the scene/page
2. Poster type / behind-the-speaker proof (G-1, G-8), drifting at its own parallax (EV-1)
3. The matted speaker (or the plain footage when nothing sits behind)
4. In-world graphics below the chin (count on the chest, ruler, numbers over props)
5. Box/card/bubble chrome (split edge, card border, chat UI)
6. Captions
7. Flash layer (T-04/T-05/T-06/T-10)

### 10.4 Finishing
- Keep the footage's grade and vignette, rich and never pale (M16); scenes get depth blur, glow on neon numbers, subtle grain on B/W past shots.
- Spell every on-screen word from the script; every number matches the voice (M11).
- Look at stills of every punch-in and every box: the whole face is there (M3).

### 11.2 The SFX ledger (Naman's rule)
- **Any file at most 2× in a reel,** except the one **list cue** (the anchor's sheet, coin or node sound on every item).
- **No file on two consecutive cues;** rotate within each pool.
- **Density:** about one cue every **1.2–1.8 s** inside scenes, poster builds, cards and splits (this style is fast); on face beats only their events (a poster slam, a number's land, a rail tile, a punchline sub-boom); nothing on plain punch jump cuts or on ordinary two-tier key words.
- **Never more than two cues overlapping;** never on a consonant of the trigger word (slide ±2 f).
- The pops, ticks and paper are texture; the slams, the number lands and the sub-booms are what the viewer feels. Log every cue (id, time, role) in the ledger.

### 11.3 Silence and the mix
- **Silence** (no new SFX) for 0.3–0.7 s on the one deliberate beat before the punchline or the FOMO turn (M4, SD-9), and 0.3–0.5 s before the hook's loop line; then the punchline lands on a sub-boom.
- **The voice is close, compressed and present**; the edit adds no reverb. The voice is always the loudest thing.
- **Mix:** high-pass the voice ~80 Hz, light compression, de-ess; normalise to **−14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1.0 dBTP** (fix: all five masters were −9.5…−10.3 LUFS, true peak +0.8…+4.8 dBFS).

### 12.4 The tight-selfie fix (any layout)
When the head is too big or too close for the frame or box (hair top or chin outside, or the head crossing a side edge, as in ts5's 3.0–4.5 s handheld-feel close and ts4's 39.5 s extreme crop):
1. **Full frame: keep it full screen as shot.** No shift, no scale-down, no blurred fill. Eyes to chin always in; the hair may touch the top edge. Skip punch-ins on that footage; the poster sits behind the head anyway, so move or resize the type, never the video.
2. **In a split box, card or bubble the footage fills the box** (cover), cropped on the face; size the box so the whole head fits (a taller split bottom, a bigger card or bubble): ≥ 40 px air above the hair (≥ 30 px in a card/bubble), the chin inside; never a narrow pillar.
3. **Never** shrink the video inside its frame or box over a blurred or darkened copy of itself, pad a box with blurred side bars, or set a box on the creator's own blurred frame (Naman, 13 Oct: "either full screen or enclosed in a box").
4. If the head still can't fit a box, use the full-frame beat or another part of the take. Never crop the eyes or chin.

## §15 Look at your frames
Not a checklist; the questions the best editor asks while scrubbing:
- **The footage frame:** is every video, recording and picture full screen or in a designed box it fills, never a sharp rectangle over its own blur, never padded with blurred bars?
- **Frame 0, sound off:** is he there full frame, sharp, mid-word and mid-gesture, whole face, rich colour, the first caption under the chin and the poster already landing behind him? Are there six changes by 3 s, and does the claim number slam by ~1 s?
- **The poster:** does it live behind him (parallax, rim light, each word punching on its onset), never in front of his face or hair, no digit hidden by his head?
- **Every number:** does it roll and slam on its last syllable, big enough to read from across the room?
- **The anchor:** does every item arrive in the same space, with the earlier items still there? Does the last visit light everything as the payoff?
- **The speaker:** is he back within ~6 s every time, gliding into the split, the card and the bubble instead of jumping, whole and big in every box (card ≥ 560 px, bubble ≥ 600 px), never a pillar, never pale?
- **Scale:** shrink the still to thumbnail size. Is the hero ≥ 760 px and the first thing I see, or is the frame half empty? Can I read every caption, label and table row?
- **Captions:** is every spoken word on screen, appending, under the chin, the key word punching big?
- **Truth:** shown numbers equal spoken ones, names spelled one way, the CTA the user's own, no plug, no film clip, no invented testimonial?
- **Sound:** a slam on every big poster word, a roll and a hit on every number, a whoosh on every scene, the list cue on every item, silence before the punchline, no file overused, no music anywhere?
- **Vibe:** fast, ambitious, doable; the edit shouts, the voice doesn't. Anything that feels like a plain talking head or a hype montage with no method comes out.

## §16 Build + render

- **Pipeline:** the plugin's edit skill: plan in the edit brief (beat sheet, transition map, sound ledger), draw every frame in `reel.js` (`frame(t)`), look at stills of every landing frame, score (`plan/sounds.json`, `veos sfx`), then storyboard and render.
- **Fonts (bundled):** Inter Tight (`InterTight-VF`, `InterTight-Italic-VF`), Anton, Barlow Condensed Bold, Permanent Marker, Caveat. Pre-paint ₹ % ⚠ ✓ before the first frame.
- **Component map** (helpers written once per reel in `reel.js`):
