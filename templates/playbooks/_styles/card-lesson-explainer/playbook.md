# Card Lesson Explainer Playbook (v2, "Pro Board" edition, for Claude Opus 5.5)

> **In this plugin:** this file is the whole playbook: every measured value and recipe from the 4 reference reels, and the two-box stack, are in it; there is no other playbook file to open. Frame counts here are at **30 fps** (the references' rate). The per-reel notes it cites (`ee1…ee4`) are not shipped. Sounds are picked by id from SOUNDS.md; commands are in the edit skill's REFERENCE.md. The page gives you `FACE(n)` and `FACE0` (with `head_top` and `chin`) for framing every box.

**Purpose:** you (Claude) receive a talking-head video of the creator plus its script. Use this document to **ideate and build** every title, box, board, highlight, number, caption, move and sound, then edit the Reel as **a lesson from someone who clearly knows more than you**, on a frame so still and so precise that every change inside it lands like a point being proven.

**What v2 is:** a frame-by-frame analysis of 4 Editing Explained reels (ee1 "how your movie has subtitles but no subtitle file", ee2 "95% editors don't know this difference" (codec vs container), ee3 "social media render settings", ee4 "this mistake is ruining your video" (bit rate)), plus the creator's two-box direction of 11 Oct, elevated around the creator's standing directions. Every measured layout, recipe and rule from those reels is kept here; where a reference reel and a direction disagree (the references were voice only, with hard pops and no moves), **the directions win**.

**Measured sizes are floors, not targets.** The thing in the top box is shown as large as the box allows, cropped to what matters; a box with empty space or unreadable small UI is unfinished.
- **Full screen or a box, never any footage or picture over its own blur** (Studio Standard): the creator's video either fills the frame edge to edge, or sits in a designed box (rounded corners, a rim or soft shadow, on this style's own ground or scene) that it fills completely (cover-crop centred on the face). A tight selfie stays full screen: move the text, not the video; hair may touch the top edge, eyes to chin always in; when the whole head must show, use a box layout on the style's ground. ### The feel (read this twice)
This is the edit of **the senior on the team** explaining something to a junior over their shoulder: quick, a little cheeky ("mujhe umeed hai tum log chori nahi karte"), completely sure, never loud. The frame is a **pro's desk**: a black dot-grid, one orange glow under the promise, two clean boxes. Nothing on it ever wobbles. What moves is the *lesson*: the exact field in the real tool gets circled the instant he names it, the board fills as he teaches, the verdict slams in on the word, and his face is always right there under the proof, reacting. It should feel **exclusive** ("only pro editors know this"), **useful** (you could do it in your software right now) and **satisfying** (every click of the lesson has a sound and a place). If it ever looks like a hype reel, it's wrong; if it ever looks like a slide deck, it's wrong too.

### Who this is, and who it's for
- **The reference creator:** a calm, sharp teacher of a craft (video editing) who talks fast in **Hinglish** (3.7–4.2 words/s), peer-to-peer ("tum log"), sometimes cheeky ("mujhe umeed hai tum log chori nahi karte"), always practical. He teaches one specific thing per reel and ends on the rule.
- **The audience:** beginner and intermediate practitioners of a craft who want the one setting, term or mistake explained with the real tool on screen. They trust the reel because they **see the exact field** in the real software.
- **The lineage:** a teacher at a fixed board.
  1. **The frame:** a dark dot-grid or cream paper-grid canvas; a two-line title (bold claim + italic serif sub-line + a glowing orange underline); rounded boxes; captions under them. The furniture never wobbles.
  2. **Two boxes: the thing and the face, divided.** Whatever he wants seen gets its own box on top, his face its own box below, so the viewer sees what he means *and* who is saying it.
  3. **Box content:** the real UI with an orange box drawing around the field, a white whiteboard where he teaches, a dark card that lands a verdict, big numbers on his face.
  4. **Captions:** uppercase squared "techno" captions, 2–4 words, hard swaps, no colour.
- **Left out on purpose:** the references carry a third-party caption-tool plug ("Captions made using …") that blinks on twice per reel. It is not part of the style. Its slot under the caption may hold **the creator's own** call to action.

## §1 Procedure
0. **Know the footage and the words.** Note the framing: a medium close-up is ideal for the face box; a vertical close-up feeds the full-frame beats. Transcribe with word timestamps; the captions are the spoken words, romanised for Hinglish, UPPERCASE, 2–4 words (§5). Pick the variant (§3.0) and the canvas (dark dot grid by default; cream paper grid for a lighter tool-tutorial feel). Produce the assets: real screenshots or recordings of the tool cropped to the field, board text, numbers; no stock unless it literally shows the noun.
1. **Watch and listen to the whole cut.** Mark where he challenges the viewer, where he reveals the answer, where he states the rule, and every term, setting, number and verdict he names.
2. **Name the one lesson** in a line the viewer would repeat. It becomes the title: a claim that makes them feel left out ("Only pro editors…", "95% editors don't know…", "This mistake is ruining your…") + an italic sub-line.
3. **Build the lesson spine** (§7.1): the one thing that fills up across the reel (a board of terms, a settings panel with each field circled in turn, a five-role team, a checklist), so the top box always shows how far the lesson has come.
4. **Plan the stack beat by beat** (§3): what is in the top box, what the face box does, when the face box grows to full frame (D3).
5. **List every term with its onset** and give each a proof device (§8): the exact field circled, a board item, a number, a verdict, a literal B-roll.
6. **Write 3 hooks, second by second** (§6): the title lines, the spoken hook, the first top-box swap. Keep the one that promises most.
7. **Score it** (§11): every proof event gets its sound, logged in the sound ledger.
8. **Attack the plan** like the best editor you know: where is the top box showing nothing useful? Where does the viewer lose his face? Where is a field too small to read? Where does a change happen by cutting when it could happen to the thing itself? Where is it flashy?
9. **Draw, look at the frames (§15), fix.**

## §2 Craft that never bends
- **The whole face is always visible:** hair to chin inside its box or the full frame, with air above the hair, at every zoom, punch-in, jump-cut reframe and crop. Size the face box's crop from the face (`FACE0.head_top`, `FACE0.chin`; `FACE(n)` when he moves): if the footage is a close handheld selfie, **grow the box taller first** (down to the bottom of the stack), then let the footage **fill the box** (cover-crop centred on the face): hair may touch the box's top edge, eyes to chin always in. **Never shrink the footage inside the box over a blurred copy of itself** (Studio Standard). A caption may cross the face.
- **Text never sits on text;** nothing sits on the caption.
- **Sound in sync:** every SFX on the frame its picture lands, never on a consonant of the word it illustrates (slide ±2 f).
- **True and exact:** every setting, number and name exactly as in the real tool and the script; real screenshots; spelling exact.
- **Readable at phone size:** anything the viewer must read is ≥ 26 px; crop the UI to the field rather than shrinking the whole window.
- **Loudness:** −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP; end on the rule, never on accidental black.

Everything else is direction. You decide; write your reasons into the plan.

### 2.1 The house grammar (what makes it this style)
Not checks: this is what the four reference reels do every time, and why it works. Break one only with a reason in the plan.
- **The furniture never wobbles:** the title block, the box rects and the caption line hold still; only the boxes' content changes. They move only as whole stage moves (§3.2), on a beat.
- **The thing and the face are on screen together.** Whenever a tool, B-roll, board, number card or his own results are shown, his face is visible at the same time: in its own box under it, or as a picture-in-picture / cut-out inside the top box. Never a top box with no face for more than ~1 s (a verdict card is the exception).
- **Show every named term inside ~0.5 s:** the real UI with the orange box around the exact field, a board item typing on, or a big word or number. It lands on the keyword (−2…+2 f, never later than 8 f; ee2's "Apple Pro Res" landed ~0.5 s late) and leaves on the next sentence's first word or a jump cut.
- **Every spoken number becomes a big figure on the word; every verdict ("galat", "this is wrong") a dark card on the word.**
- **The breaths are cut:** gaps ≤ 0.2–0.25 s; one deliberate ~0.25 s beat after a verdict question is allowed ("This is wrong… why?").
- **No dead stretch:** a talking stretch longer than ~6 s gets a jump cut with a small reframe or a number/word on screen (ee4 sat 9–15 s with nothing).
- **One accent: orange.** The title underline, the field box, the key word on a card. Everything else black, white, dark grey (and cream on the light canvas).
- **Captions never wider than the boxes** (ee4 ran them to x 60–1023), uppercase, no colour.
- **Crisp, never soft:** text cards and board text are built at full resolution (ee4's up-scaled cards were soft); the UI is cropped to the field, never shrunk until it can't be read.
- **Spelling exact:** every English word and tool name ("GENTELMEN", "95% Editor", "HOTA HAY" all slipped through once).
- **Never:** flashes, whips, zoom transitions, dissolves, glitches (and never ship a decode glitch: ee2 f684); coloured or animated captions; a second accent colour.

### 3.2 Stage moves (D3)
| ID | Move | Recipe | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SM-1** | **Face up** | The face box grows from its rect to the full frame in 10–12 f (expo-out, no overshoot); the top box and the title slide up and fade out in the same move; the caption gets its black box | The hook's challenge, a turn ("Galat kiya"), the final rule |
| **SM-2** | **Back to the board** | The reverse: full frame → face box, while the top box and title come back down exactly as they were | Back to the proof |
| **SM-3** | **Top box takes over** | The top box grows to fill the upper two-thirds (to y≈1150) for a UI walkthrough, the face shrinking into a picture-in-picture inside it (≈ 280–300 px square, radius 20, bottom-left; ee3 used ≈ 280) sized from his face, or his cut-out bottom-right (ee1 inside the VLC video area, ee2/ee4 at a board) | A long screen walkthrough (> 5 s) |
| **SM-4** | **Verdict rise** | A dark verdict card rises out of the top box (scales 0.9 → 1.0, opacity 0 → 1, 8 f), sits over the top box's content, then sinks back on the next sentence | Every verdict ("Automatic hota hai", "This is wrong") |

Rhythm: the stack is home. Full frame 2–3 times per reel (hook punch, the turn, the rule), each 1.5–5 s.

### 3.3 Framing the face (D6)
- **Face box:** the head (hair to chin) ≈ 55–65% of the box height, centred, air above the hair ≈ 6–10% of the box height. Take the crop from `FACE0`; if he moves a lot, follow `FACE(n)` smoothly (6–10 f lag).
- **Handheld selfie footage** (the face fills the camera frame): the footage always **fills the box** (cover-crop centred on the face, never letterboxed, never padded with a blurred copy of itself). Make the box taller (or give the beat to full frame) until eyes to chin sit comfortably; hair may touch the box's top edge.
- **Full frame:** as shot; every settle or punch-in stops while the whole head is in frame, the head top ≥ ~80 px below the top edge (ee1's beanie touched it); eye line 25–37% of the frame height.
- **Measured face-box framing:** head top ≈ box top + 43 px, eye line ≈ 34% of the box height, face centre x ≈ 520–585. Jump cuts may alternate two crop sizes (punch ≤ 20%), both with the whole head inside.
- **Cut-outs at the board:** waist-up, bottom-right of the top box, the whole head inside the box.

### 3.4 Safe zones
Key text inside x 74–1005 (never wider than the boxes), y 90–1650; the bottom ≈ 270 px holds only the spine strip or a CTA.

## §5 Type and captions
- **Title (`TITLE-PRO`):** line 1 a bold claim, Inter Tight 800, ≈ 95 px ("Only Pro Editors", "95% Editors", "This Mistake is"); line 2 an Instrument Serif italic sub-line ("use this kind of subtitle", "don't know this difference", "Ruining your video"); a glowing orange underline (≈ 6 px core + soft glow, fading to transparent at both ends). It builds over f2–22 (§10 Title build) and stays.
- **Captions (`CAP-TECH`):** **every spoken word**, romanised as spoken, UPPERCASE, numbers as digits ("50000 KBPS PE", "H.264"). 2–4 words (≤ 5), ~0.6–0.8 s each, hard swaps (they may carry across a layout change by 1–2 f). Barlow Semi Condensed 600–700, cap height 34–38 px (≈ 46–52 px font), +6–10% tracking, `#FCFCFC`, no stroke, no shadow, no colour; the number or tool name one weight heavier. Centred under the face box (y ≈ 1530 in the stack, ≈ 1337 under the single card), never wider than the boxes. No box on the black canvas; a black box (≈ 10–12 px padding, square corners) on the cream canvas and over full-frame footage, where it sits **high, just under his chin** (top ≈ 40–80 px below the chin) so it's seen the moment his face fills the frame.
- **Board:** a condensed bold dark header word ("CODEC", Anton) ≈ 130 px with a soft drop shadow, or a casual topic word (ee4 "Bit Rate": Permanent Marker with a black→grey gradient); list items Inter Tight 400–500 ≈ 40 px, typed per character.
- **Verdict cards:** Anton condensed caps ≈ 150–170 px; line 1 orange, line 2 white ("AUTOMATIC / HOTA HAI", "LADIES / & GENTLEMEN").
- **Big words and numbers on his face:** Anton white with a dark drop shadow, ≈ 150 px for headline figures at the box bottom, ≈ 60 px beside the head for asides; they may sit **behind his hand** (matte).
- **Nothing a viewer must read under 26 px.**

## §6 Hook: the pro's challenge

### 6.1 What the first 3 seconds do
1. **Frame 0:** the stack is up: the top box already shows the thing (the two options, the setting, his result in a phone), his face below mid-sentence, the title building.
2. **The challenge in his voice:** a quiz ("honest answer karna, Google pe search na karna"), a cheeky jab ("mujhe umeed hai tum log chori nahi karte…"), or a "you've always seen this" ("chahe tum koi bhi software use karte ho…").
3. **The viewer picks a side by ~3 s:** the orange circle draws around the options or the field he's asking about.
4. **The punch:** on the challenge's last word the face box grows to full frame (SM-1) for one line, then settles back for the proof.

|---|---|---|

| **HC-2** | **The exclusive title** | The claim builds letter by letter while his face asks the question below |
| **HC-3** | **The proof phone** | The top box holds a phone playing his own finished results, cutting every ~1 s |

### 6.3 Hook sound
A soft `hint-appear-2025-08-27-02-25-12-utc-hint-appear-02` on the title's first letters, `objects-drawing-board-marker-on-paper-drawing-line-medium-length-02-vadi` as the circle draws, a single soft weight (`abstract-hit-sub-bass-07`) under the face punch.

### 6.4 Endings
The references end without a CTA: a hard stop on the practical rule ("jahan zaroorat hai wahan zyada daal…", "to aap ye embedded file de dete ho", "APPLE PRO RES 4444" held), with his face up (SM-1) and the finished spine behind. If the creator's brief has a keyword or link, it goes in the slot under the caption, in the frame's own type and orange, held for the last 2–3 s.

## §7 Structure

### 7.1 The lesson spine
One thing fills up across the reel and is visible in the top box or a thin strip at the bottom:
- **A board** whose items accumulate (terms, steps, roles), each typed on as he says it, the earlier ones staying.
- **A settings panel** where the orange circle moves from field to field (the UI glides between them), each visited field keeping a small ✓.
- **A strip** of N small labelled slots under the caption that light orange one by one (roles, steps, mistakes).
Write the spine in the plan, and for every beat, what changes in it.

### 7.2 Sections
- **Open with the challenge** (§6).
- **The wrong answer first** ("Restrict to socha na? Galat kiya.") with a verdict rise, then the face up for the turn.
- **The proof** in the stack: each term with its device (§8), the spine filling.
- **"Why?"** as a section card (a page-turn into a white question slide in the top box) to open the second half.
- **The rule** at the end: face up, the rule as the last caption, the spine complete in the top box behind it.

### 7.3 Rhythm
- Proof in the stack, punch in full frame, proof again. The face is always there.
- Something in the top box changes every ~1.5–2.5 s; the face box runs with its jump cuts.
- Escalate quietly: the last proof gets the cleanest, biggest top-box moment (the full panel with every field ticked, the full board).

### 7.4 Open loops
The quiz withheld ~3 s; the mystery ("kaise aa gaya usme?") closed by the UI reveal; "Why X is good then?" opening the second half and paid off by the rule.

**Never:** meme sounds, cartoon effects, risers that scream, glitches, crowd reactions.

### 11.2 The SFX ledger (Studio Standard Rule, unchanged)
- **Any file at most 2× in a reel,** except the one list cue (here `paper-turn-page`, once per section).
- **No file on two consecutive cues;** rotate within each pool.
- **About one cue every 1.2–1.8 s,** every one tied to a picture; the face box's jump cuts get nothing.
- **Never more than two at once,** never on a consonant of the word.
- Pen and keys are texture; the verdict and the answer are the ones the viewer should notice.

### 11.3 No music bed
No music bed, ever: this style is scored with sound effects only. The voice and the SFX carry the reel. **Go silent (no new SFX) for 0.5–1 s before the final rule.**

### 11.4 Mix
Voice chain (high-pass 80 Hz, de-ess, light compression); −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP. The voice is always the loudest thing.

## §12 Footage handling

## §15 Look at your frames
- **Frame 0, sound off:** is the thing already in the top box, his face below, the claim building?
- **The frame:** is their video full screen or in a designed box (rounded, rimmed, on the style's ground), never a sharp rectangle over its own blur?
- **Every term:** can I read the exact field, and is it circled as he says it?
- **Every frame:** is his whole head visible, hair to chin, in the face box or full frame?
- **Every change:** did it happen to the thing (circle, glide, type, roll, rise), or did it just cut?
- **Vibe:** calm, precise, one orange, no gimmicks.
- **Craft:** no text on text, sounds on their pictures.
- **The last frame:** the rule, with the finished spine behind it.
