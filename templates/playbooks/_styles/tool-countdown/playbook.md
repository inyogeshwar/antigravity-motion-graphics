# AI News Countdown Playbook (v2, "Show It While You Say It, Elevated" edition, for Claude Opus 5.5)

> **In this plugin:** this file is the whole playbook: every measured value and recipe from the 3 reference reels is in it, and there is no other playbook file to open. Frame counts are measured at the references' **30 fps** (t = f / 30). Positions are on a 1080×1920 frame (the sources were 720×1280, scaled ×1.5). The per-reel notes this file cites (`vb1…vb3`) are not shipped. Sounds are picked by id from SOUNDS.md; commands are in the edit skill's REFERENCE.md; fonts are the bundled ones. The page gives you `FACE(n)` and `FACE0` (with `head_top` and `chin`) for every speaker frame.

**Purpose:** you (Claude) receive a talking-head video of the creator plus its script. Use this document to **ideate and build** every format choice, layout move, tag, caption, B-roll beat, transition and sound, then edit the Reel so it looks and sounds like a fast, confident AI/tech news countdown: the speaker never stops talking, **the thing he names is on screen while he names it**, and every item lands with such weight, rhythm and polish that the viewer has to see the next number.

**What v2 is:** a frame-by-frame analysis of 3 finished reels by an Indian AI and tech explainer, Vaibhav (English speech): vb1 "Apple just convinced me to switch back to iPhone" (10-item feature countdown, standing studio, 2:58.7), vb2 "Midjourney just built a medical machine that will replace MRIs" (seated news explainer, two angles, 1:29.4), vb3 "MrBeast is giving away a free business" (weekly countdown of 10 free AI tools, seated desk, 2:43.0), **elevated ten times** by the best editor, motion designer and creative director in the world. The "show it on the noun" grammar, the three formats, the #NN tag and the serif punchline are kept whole; this edition gives every tag real weight and a running count, glides the speaker into his box instead of jumping, punches into the proof until it reads, unlocks the teased items on screen, carries the film-burn through the cut, and scores every show event with a crisp news-room sound. All measured layouts, recipes and rules are kept. Where a reference reel and a direction disagree, **the directions win**. Cut lists, layout and colour measurements, caption timing, motion recipes and audio were measured; every reel's contact sheets were checked by eye.
- Every rule is either **observed** (the most consistent value chosen), a **fix** (a reel got it wrong; marked **(fix)**), or this edition's **elevation** (§6.0, §8.0, §8.7).
- **This style is not one layout.** Three formats (§3): a **divided demo split** (the thing in a top box, a black divider, the speaker in his own box below), a **documentary desk** (B-roll fading into black over a desk shot, a close-up for every opinion), and a **card over the blurred self** (tag, logo behind the head, a demo card over the defocused speaker). Pick from the script.
- **And the speaker moves.** Full screen for claims, reactions, tags and turns; into his box / lower zone / backdrop / card while the thing is shown; back for the next opinion. vb1 makes that move **16 times** in 179 s, vb2 8 times in 89 s, vb3 10 times (once per item). Never one layout from frame 0 to the end (§3.0a). This edition makes the move itself a designed motion (the split glide T-14, the card dock T-15/T-16) instead of a jump.

**Measured sizes are floors, not targets.** The references' chips (46–50 px), seam captions (≈ 34 px), labels (45 px), lead-ins (≈ 35 px) and card UI were small on a phone. Here:
- **Full screen or a box, never any footage or picture over its own blur** (Naman, 13 Oct): the creator's video, a screen recording, a landscape clip, a photo or a diagram either fills the whole frame edge to edge (cover-crop to the part that matters, or a push-in), or sits in a designed box (rounded, rimmed or softly shadowed, on this style's own ground, colour, gradient, texture, grid or drawn set) that it fills completely. Never shrink anything inside the frame over a blurred or darkened copy of itself, never pad a box, band or panel with blurred side bars, never set a card on the creator's own blurred frame. A tight selfie stays full screen as shot: move the text, not the video (eyes to chin always in; the hair may touch the top edge). When the whole head must show, use a box sized so the footage fills it with the head inside. (A matte cut-out over a plate is fine: it has no box edge.)
- **Captions** `CAP-CHIP` / `CAP-SEAM` / `CAP-LIME` **64–72 px**, every spoken word. If a phrase doesn't fit, chunk it shorter; never shrink it.
- **The serif punchline** `KT-SERIF` **120–150 px**, its lead-in 56–64 px; **labels** 60–64 px; **request-card text** 52 px; **anything meant to be read ≥ 48 px**, UI inside a recording included (push in, EV-5).
- **The hero of a beat is big:** the top box is full width (1080) and ≥ 900 px tall; the demo card ≥ 1050 px wide; a boxed clip ≥ 860 px wide; logo pops ≥ 285 px; the #NN tag ≈ 245–400 px glyphs. A group (logo row, teaser icons, the recap stack) fills x ≈ 80–1000.
- **No dead frame:** a frame that is more than half black or empty canvas for longer than ~0.7 s means the thing is too small or the next element is late: push in, swap, or bring the next one.
- **The face stays big:** the speaker box and the dock card are ≥ 940 px wide; the documentary lower-zone face ≥ 320 px; a tight selfie stays full screen as shot (eyes to chin in; the hair may touch the top edge) and fills its box everywhere else, never shrunk over its own blur, never a narrow pillar.

**Never pale.** The footage keeps its own colour, or a touch richer: deep blacks, warm skin, bright screens. Never desaturate, fade or lift the blacks as a look (vb1's slightly milky studio is corrected, §4.4). His video is never blurred behind a card or used as a blurred fill (Naman, 13 Oct): it is sharp and full screen, or sharp in a box. The light leaks and film-burns are short punctuation, never a grade.

**How to use this file:**
- Follow §1 (procedure) in order.
- Look up visuals by ID: formats `FMT-…`, speaker states `SP-…`, hook formulas `HF-…`, hook visuals `H-…`, structure devices `SD-…`, graphic families `G-…`, patterns `P-…`, transitions `T-…`, endings `E-…`, elevated devices `EV-…`.
- §2 is the craft that never bends; §2.1 is the house grammar (what makes this style, with reasons). Everything else is a default you adapt like the best editor in the world, with a one-line reason.

### The feel (read this twice)
This is **a friend who tried every AI launch this week, counting down the ones worth your time, live, at full speed.** He says the name and it is already there: the real app in the top box, the logo rising from behind his head, the card sliding over him. Every number lands like a slam on the table: the lime digits roll up from the last item's number, thump, glow and settle, and the little rail at the top fills one more notch, so you always know how many are left. The punchline arrives in big white serif italic right under his chin; the proof zooms in until you can read it; the film-burn sweeps the old item away and the next number is already landing inside the light. It feels **fast, confident and generous**: news-room energy, keynote polish, zero filler. Nothing is decoration and nothing is fake: every picture is the real product. If it looks like a slideshow of screenshots, it's wrong; if it looks like a meme edit, it's wrong too.

**Who the reference creator is:** a high-energy, confident Indian AI and tech explainer talking **English** straight to camera, standing with a handheld mic or seated at a desk. Launches, features, tools, and the "what this really means" angle. A friend who already tried it: personal angle first ("I use a foldable"), then the twist, then proof, then an opinion. No filler, no pauses.

**Audience (ICP):** AI-curious Indian professionals, students and creators who follow tech news but don't read release notes. They want **what changed, what it does for them, what is free**. Prices in ₹ (and $ where priced so). They need to **see** the feature: a claim without a picture loses them.

**Style lineage:** four layers:
1. **Speaker:** one talking head, never gone for long; breaths cut; variety from punch-ins (vb1, vb3) or a second angle (vb2).
2. **Show:** the real thing named, in **a box of its own** (top box, feathered top zone, or a card below the chin over the sharp, full-screen speaker), swapping on every noun.
3. **Structure:** a **#NN tag** with lime digits on every item, a teaser label, a series ident ("Day 60", "Week 09"), a white serif-italic phrase on every punchline.
4. **Punctuation:** a warm light-leak flash at the list start or ident, a film-burn leak between items (vb3), and a full-screen glow CTA card with a cursor click (all 3).

## §1 Procedure (follow in order)

1. **Inventory.** Probe the footage (resolution, fps, duration, audio). Identify the camera setup (§12.1): standing studio with a handheld mic, seated desk, two angles or one, handheld selfie. Conform variable-frame-rate phone footage to a constant 30 fps first.
2. **Measure the face.** On sampled frames (every ~2 s and on every lean), note the head top (hair), eye line, chin and face width. This decides every crop, box, card and punch-in in the reel (§3.5). If the footage is a tight handheld selfie where the hair or chin already touches the frame edge, plan F-TIGHT (§3.5): full screen as shot, boxes sized so the footage fills them with the whole head; never scaled down over its own blur.
3. **Transcribe** with word-level timestamps in the creator's own language (English: force `en`; Hinglish: romanised, as the creator writes it). Every spoken word becomes a caption, in phrases of 2–5 (§5). Check every product, app, company and tool name against the script; the references shipped ASR spellings on screen ("zappier", "elevanlabs", "mid-journey") **(fix)**.
4. **Choose the format** (§3.0) from the shape of the script, and write one line on why:
   - a list of features/steps that must be seen working → `FMT-SPLIT`;
   - one story with an argument and a turn → `FMT-DOC`;
   - a ranked list of tools, each with a demo → `FMT-CARD`.
5. **Segment** the script into: `HOOK` (0–5 s; up to ~8.5 s with a proof clip), `SETUP` / open loop, optional `IDENT` (series ident), body `ITEM`s (one per #NN) or story `BEAT`s, the `TURN` (if any), `PAYOFF`, `CTA`.
6. **Classify every sentence** with a line type from §8.4 and mark its **trigger word**: the product, app, feature, number or verb the viewer must see. Mark **ordinal words** ("first one", "number 8", "the fourth one is") and **opinion lines** ("it's just magic", "that's not healthcare"): they drive the speaker's moves.
7. **Plan the speaker map** (§3.0a): mark every beat with a speaker state (`SP-FULL`, `SP-BOX`, `SP-CLOSE`, `SP-LOWER`, `SP-BACKDROP`, `SP-DOCK`, `SP-CUTOUT`, `SP-ABSENT`). Full screen at frame 0; into the box/zone/backdrop on the first named thing; back to full screen on every tag, reaction and turn.
8. **Ideate 3 hook variants** (§6), each with a formula, the frame-0 picture, the first 3 s of events and the first captions. Recommend one.
9. **Fill the beat sheet** (§13): one beat per trigger word, so the picture changes about **every 1.0–1.5 s**. Each beat gets the speaker state, the pattern ID, the caption, the transition and the sound.
10. **Plan the transition map** (§9.3): every layout change (glide or hard cut), every leak, every card slide and dock. Check the budget (§9.4).
11. **Produce the assets** (§8.6): screen recordings the creator captured, the product's own official media when the reel is about that product, logos, screenshots (official pages and logos may be fetched from the web; list sources). List what's missing. Never fake a UI result, a number or a post.
12. **The 10× pass and the score** (§8.0, §8.7, §11): for every beat, its hero and how it lands (tag with weight, proof punch, glide, dock, burn relay, unlock); then score every show event and log the sound ledger. **Attack the plan:** where is a proof too small to read? Where is a frame half empty? Where does the speaker sit in one layout too long? Where is the count lost? Where is a sound on a plain cut or under an opinion? Fix it in the plan.
13. **Show the creator** the plan and stills (§13.3).
14. **Build** item by item, look at your frames (§15), score, render (§16).

## §2 Craft that never bends
- **The whole face is always visible:** hair to chin, ≥ 60 px of air above the hair inside every frame, box, card, backdrop and punch-in; tight footage stays full screen as shot, or fills its box sized for the whole head; never scaled down over its own blurred copy. Captions may cross the face; graphics may not.
- **Text never sits on text:** a caption never over a tag, a label, the serif stack or a card's key line.
- **Sound in sync:** every SFX on the frame its picture lands, never on a consonant (slide ±2 f); nothing louder than the voice.
- **True and exact:** every product, feature, number and price from the script or the product's own media; names spelled as the brand spells them; no fabricated result shown as real.
- **Readable at phone size:** captions 64–72 px; anything meant to be read ≥ 48 px; the hero big.
- **Loudness:** −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP; a hard stop ≤ 6 f after the last word.

Everything else is direction. You decide; write your reasons into the plan.

### 2.1 The house grammar (what makes it this style)
Not checks: this is what the three reference reels do, and why it works. Break one only with a reason in the plan. (The IDs are kept because other sections cite them.)
- M1. **One format per reel** (§3), with a written reason, **and the speaker moves** (§3.0a): into his box / lower zone / backdrop / card when the thing is shown, back to full screen for tags, reactions and turns. Never one layout throughout. Cadence: a cycle every ~8–15 s (`FMT-SPLIT`), ~8–12 s (`FMT-DOC`), once per item (`FMT-CARD`, plus M14). This edition moves him with designed, eased motion (T-14 glide, T-15/T-16 dock); a hard cut stays for hard onsets.
- M2. **The whole face is always visible: hair to chin, at every zoom, punch-in, crop, box, card, circle or panel.** How far a punch-in goes and where a box crops is decided by the face, never by the frame or the divider. Keep ≥ 60 px of air above the hair inside any frame edge, box edge or divider, and the chin inside the frame. If the footage is tighter than the face allows (a handheld selfie close-up, a lean-in), **keep it full screen as shot (eyes to chin in; the hair may touch the top edge) and skip the punch-ins; in a box, crop it to fill the box and make the box tall enough for the whole head**; never shrink it over a blurred copy of itself; the box or card stays ≥ 940 px wide, never a narrow pillar (§3.5). Captions may cross the face; graphics may not. (fix) vb1's divider cut his hair on leans and his wrist punch-ins cut his head at the eyes; vb2's split face was ~150 px; vb3's header crossed the blurred chin. Every case and its fix: §3.5.
- M3. **Frame 0 is never empty.** The speaker or the real thing (ideally both) is on screen at f0, mid-word, and the first graphic event lands by f1–f7 (vb1 f1, vb2 f6). (fix) vb3 opened on a black canvas with ~520 px of empty black below a third-party clip; ours opens on the creator's own proof in his own layout with the speaker visible (H-3).
- M4. **Show it while you say it.** Every named product, app, feature, screen, number or company gets its real visual **on the noun** (0–0.3 s after the word starts, 3/3), even mid-phrase.
- M5. **Zero dead air.** No word gap > 0.35 s (longest measured 0.34, 0.34, 0.38 s); 3.3–3.8 words/s; jump cuts on word boundaries.
- M6. **A change about every second:** median 1.0–1.3 s (measured 1.02, 1.28, 1.07); nothing static over ~4 s except the CTA line.
- M7. **The speaker is never gone for long:** ≤ 4 s per absent block (vb1 3.7, 3.9 s), ≤ ~20% of the runtime in total (vb2 ≈ 21%).
- M8. **Every list item gets its marker, numbered correctly.** The same #NN recipe at **every** item, on the ordinal word. (fix) vb1 showed "#08" twice and no "#09": the count on screen must match the item.
- M9. **One lime per reel**, reserved for the countdown number (and, in `FMT-CARD`, the caption chip). White serif italic is reserved for spoken emphasis. Colour is never decoration (§4).
- M10. **Readable at phone size (sizes are floors):** captions 64–72 px; the serif stack 120–150 px with a 56–64 px lead-in; labels 60–64 px; card text, headers and request cards ≥ 52 px; anything meant to be read ≥ 48 px; source UI pushed in until its key words read at ≥ 48 px (EV-5) (§5.1). (fix) vb2's ~34 px seam captions, vb3's ~35 px chips, vb1's ~35 px lead-ins, vb3's unreadable card UI.
- M11. **Facts only from the script or supplied sources.** Recordings show the real product; a request card (G-5) shows what the viewer would type, never a fabricated result shown as real.
- M12. **Spelling exact**, product names included. (fix) "This not the exciting part", "bussiness", "hapened", "This my favorite", "elevanlabs", "zappier".
- M13. **Audio:** −14 LUFS, **true peak ≤ −1.0 dBTP** (fix: vb1 **+1.6 dBFS**, vb3 +0.1, vb2 −0.2). SFX at or under the voice's peak (fix: vb2's f0 hit ~17 dB over the speech median).
- M14. **No long card stints:** over ~6 s, add a 1–2 s full-screen opinion beat or a zoom into the UI. (fix) vb3's cards held 7–18 s.
- M15. **End clean:** hard stop ≤ 6 f after the last word, on the CTA card or the speaker; no silent lead-in (fix: vb3's 2 silent frames).
- M16. **The hero is big, the frame is full** (this edition): the top box full width and ≥ 900 px tall, the demo card ≥ 1050 px wide, groups filling x ≈ 80–1000; no frame more than half empty or backdrop for longer than ~0.7 s.
- M17. **Every spoken word is on screen** (the creator's direction): chips, seam captions, the lime chip, or the serif stack (its lead-in + serif lines together carry the whole phrase). Just under the chin on full screen, on the divider in the split, under the card in `FMT-CARD` (§5).
- M18. **The count is always known** (this edition): every tag rolls from the last number (EV-1) and the item rail (EV-2) shows where we are in the list; a teased item visibly unlocks when it lands (EV-8).

**Never in this style:**
- N1. A graphic over the face: overlays sit **below the chin** (≥ 10 px) or **behind the head** (matted logo tile). A PiP never flies across the face (fix: vb1 58.5 s).
- N2. Third-party creators' clips or celebrity footage/faces (fix: vb3's hook clip, vb1's famous photo). The slot becomes the creator's **own real proof** (H-3).
- N3. Fake posts or fake social UI (fix: vb3's "post" frame). A real screenshot of the creator's own real post is fine.
- N4. Third-party plugs or other people's communities. The CTA card carries **the user's own** CTA (fix: the references' card advertises the reference creator's community).
- N5. Re-using the same B-roll clip twice in one reel as filler. (fix) vb1 replayed its 12.6 s clips at 150 s.
- N6. Coloured, word-highlighted or animated chip captions. Captions hard-swap (§5.2–5.4).
- N7. Collapsing `FMT-SPLIT` into one box: the thing and the speaker live in **two divided boxes** with the black divider between them. Never put the speaker in a floating circle or bubble PiP (not seen in any reel).
- N8. A light-leak preset that shows a ghost of unrelated footage. (fix) vb2 f215 flashed a later shot inside the leak; the leak frames are pure colour over the outgoing/incoming shot.
- N9. Flash, leak or burn on ordinary cuts. Leaks are for the list start, the series ident and item changes only (§9.4).
- N10. Comedic or cartoon sounds, voice clips, game jingles, copyrighted songs. None of the 3 reels uses them.
- N11. A true peak above −1.0 dBTP, a silent lead-in, or a file whose duration doesn't match the edit.
- N12. Music, ever: no bed, track, pulse, drone or loop under the voice (the creator's direction, 12 Oct 2026). vb3's low electronic pulse is not used.
- N13. A pale picture: desaturated, faded or lifted-black footage, a blurred copy of his own frame, or a leak used as a look (§4.5).
- N14. A narrow pillar: a tight selfie shrunk into a tall thin box or card. Keep the box ≥ 940 px wide and filled by the footage (§3.5).

## §3 Formats, speaker states, layouts

### 3.0 Choosing the format
| The script… | Format | Why |
|---|---|---|
| …lists features, steps or apps the viewer has to see working ("Siri can now…", "it pulls up your ticket") | `FMT-SPLIT` | The top box can swap on every noun while the speaker keeps talking in his own box |
| …tells one story with a "why would they…?" question, a turn and a warning | `FMT-DOC` | Facts sit on cinematic B-roll; every opinion cuts to the close-up, so the argument is felt turning |
| …ranks N tools/products, each with a URL, a demo and a price | `FMT-CARD` | One identical module per item (tag → logo → card → chips) teaches the grammar in one go |
| …is a pure opinion with no list and no story | `FMT-DOC` with close-ups dominant and B-roll on each named thing | The close-up + serif phrase carries an opinion; B-roll still lands on every noun |

### 3.0a The speaker's movement (every reel, every format)
The format decides what the show layer looks like; **this decides where the speaker is, beat by beat.** All moves are **hard cuts** in the references (no animated shrink); they land on sentence or phrase starts. **This edition** keeps the same beats and makes the move a designed motion: the **split glide** (T-14) into and out of the box, the eased card slide (T-07) over his sharp frame, and the **card dock** (`SP-DOCK`, T-15/T-16) for an opinion on a demo that must stay visible. A hard cut stays the right choice on a hard onset (a slam word, a reaction peak).

| Beat | `FMT-SPLIT` | `FMT-DOC` | `FMT-CARD` |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Frame 0** | `SP-FULL`, mid-word, slam by f1–f7 | `SP-LOWER` under the real thing, hit on f0 | `SP-BOX` under the own proof (fix, H-3) |
| **Each demo / named thing** | `SP-BOX`, 1.0–7.5 s (median ≈ 4 s) | `SP-LOWER`; full-screen B-roll or a boxed clip ≤ 4 s | `SP-BACKDROP` (sharp here), 0.7–2.3 s after the tag |
| **Opinion / reaction** | Back to `SP-FULL` | `SP-CLOSE` + serif phrase (1.2–4 s) | Sharp `SP-FULL` (fix, M14) |
| **Every #NN / ident** | `SP-FULL`, tag on the chest | `SP-CLOSE` + ident after the flash | `SP-FULL`, tag + logo behind the head |
| **CTA** | CTA card (3.9 s) → `SP-FULL` + comment card | CTA card (3.2 s) | Save/follow `SP-FULL` → CTA card (3.7 s) |

### 4.3 Meaning of colour
- **Lime = position**: which item we're on. Never on anything else in `FMT-SPLIT`; in `FMT-CARD` also the caption chip (the reel's "voice" colour while the demo plays).
- **White serif italic = the line that matters**: the punchline, the shock, the turn. Never coloured.
- **White card + black text = what you'd say or do** (request card, label).
- **Red = the hook's sting and the call to comment**: one band in the hook, "Comment below" at the end. Never decorative.
- **Black = structure**: the divider, the seam, the box behind a clip. Pure black makes boxes and seam captions float.
- **Product colours come from the product**: logos, UI and footage bring their own colour (red, purple, green tiles in vb3). The edit adds none.

### 4.4 Footage look (don't fight it)
- `FMT-SPLIT` (vb1): neutral to slightly warm, soft contrast, slightly desaturated greens, lifted blacks (a black sweatshirt reads `#1E1E1E`–`#2A2A2A`), a soft-blurred studio background (grey wall, softbox, lit monitors, a purple LED strip). (fix) Keep it as shot **or a touch richer**: blacks to true black, contrast +5–10 %, colour kept; never the milky look (§4.5).
- `FMT-DOC` (vb2): warm, low-key "home office at night": amber desk lamp, deep brown shadows (`#25180E`–`#391F0C`), medium-high contrast, warm skin; the close-up has a light vignette and haze.
- `FMT-CARD` (vb3): warm amber "premium wood studio": walls `#7D420B`–`#47290B`, shadows `#100500`–`#1F130D`, gentle contrast, warm LED strips.
- Whatever the room, keep one grade per reel and match the angles/takes to each other.

### 4.5 Never pale
- **The speaker is in his own rich colour** in every state: full screen, box, lower zone, close-up, dock. Deep blacks, warm skin, contrast up a touch; no LUT, no desaturation, no fade, no lifted blacks.
- **The backdrop (`SP-BACKDROP`)** is his sharp, full-colour frame; the card's soft shadow gives the contrast. Never blurred, never a grey wash.
- **No blurred fill, ever:** his video is never shrunk over its own blurred copy (F-TIGHT keeps it full screen or fills its box).
- **Screens and B-roll** play as published: bright, saturated, their own colours; push in rather than dim to focus (the proof punch dims only the rest of a screen, ≤ 20 %, for its 1–2 s).
- **The leaks and burns** (T-03, T-04, T-09) are punctuation of 8–18 f, never a grade; the frame after them is clean, full colour.

### 5.5 `KT-SERIF` kinetic serif (all formats: vb1 hook lines, vb2 close-ups and boxes, vb3 full-frame lines)

- **Position:** starts **just under the chin** (lead-in top ≈ `FACE(n)` chin + 40 px), centre x ≈ 540, y ≈ 860–1240 (2 serif lines ≈ 330 px tall), always below the chin. In vb1 it sat left of centre at y ≈ 960–1050 at ~90 px.

- **Entry:** the lead-in types on at ~1 character per frame from the phrase onset; the serif line arrives from a **horizontally squashed, blurred** state to sharp in 1–2 f (vb2 f2510 → f2511) on the stressed word. vb1's version scales from ~110% with blur and settles in 2 f (f107 → f109).
- **Exit/replace:** the old phrase **smears out sideways** in 1 f as the new one stretches in (vb2 83.77 s, a text change with no cut). Otherwise it holds to the next cut.
- **It is the caption** on those lines: no chip/seam caption at the same time.

## §6 Hook system

### 6.0 The 10× hook (this edition)
- **Frame 0:** the speaker's whole face mid-word **and** a real thing, already moving (H-1/H-2/H-3), with the first caption on the first word; never black, never a fade-in, never an empty canvas.
- **A slam by f1–f7 with weight:** the band stack (H-1) or the proof frame (H-3) lands with a 2-f anticipation glow, overshoots 1.04 and settles; its sound lands on the same frame.
- **≥ 6 changes in the first 3 s** (the references measured 5–6): the slam, the glide into the box (T-14), the clip's own move, the first proof punch (EV-5), the serif twist, the teaser vault (EV-8).
- **The count is promised on screen by ~4 s:** the teaser vault shows the best items locked ("#02 #03 #08", blurred icons with a lock), or the item rail (EV-2) draws its empty notches; the viewer knows how long the ride is and what is waiting at the end.
- **The twist in big serif by 5 s** (H-4), right under the chin, and the warm leak (T-03) into #01 with the tag already landing inside the light.

### 6.1 What the hook must do in about 3 seconds
1. **Put the speaker and a real thing on screen at frame 0** (M3): the speaker mid-word plus the hook card (vb1), or the real thing on top with the speaker below (vb2). Never a black or empty frame.
2. **Make a news claim with a specific detail by ~3 s:** "Apple just launched one", "scans your body in just 60 seconds", "Tools 2, 3 and 8 do the whole job for ₹0".
3. **Twist it before 6 s:** a contrarian turn ("But this is not the most exciting part"), an authority shock ("Even radiologists are shocked"), or a teaser on the best items.
4. **Open a loop that the body pays off:** "the crazy AI features… nobody's talking about", "Why would a company that makes AI art suddenly want to scan a billion bodies?", "Tools #02, #03 and #08…" (paid near the end of the countdown).
5. **5–6 visual changes in the first 3 s** (measured 5, 6, 6).

## §8 Graphics and B-roll system

### 8.0 The 10× layer (what the best motion designer adds to every beat)
**Animation principles, applied to this style:**
| Principle | How it shows up here |
|---|---|
| **Anticipation** | A 2-f lime glow builds on the chest before every tag; the divider's edge appears at the bottom 2 f before the glide; the card's shadow arrives 1 f before the card; the room goes quiet 0.3 s before a reveal |
| **Follow-through & overlap** | Tags overshoot 1.06 and settle in 3 f; the label rect stretches in 3 f after the tag; logo pops overshoot ~110 % and settle; the dock card trails the face by 2 f; the serif's second line arrives 2 f after the first |
| **Squash & weight** | The tag lands with a 1-frame 4 % vertical squash; a price slam (EV-9) compresses 3 %; the recap stack's last tile thumps the grid |
| **Arcs** | The dock travels on an arc; PiPs fly in on a curve from below (never across the face); recap logos fly back on arcs to their slots |
| **Slow in / slow out** | Ease-out arrivals with a short settle; eased glides and docks (8–12 f); swaps inside the box stay hard cuts (they're the rhythm) |
| **Staging** | One hero per frame: the thing named. The proof punch dims the rest of a screen; the card's shadow separates it from his sharp frame; the serif owns the chest when it's up |
| **Secondary action** | A slow 3–6 % push inside every held shot (capped by the face); the recordings scroll; the logo tile behind the head drifts 2 % with parallax; the rail's current notch breathes |
| **Timing** | Items fast (a change every ~1.0–1.3 s); the tag holds 1.5–3 s; the punchline gets its 1–3 s; the payoff gets a beat of silence |
| **Exaggeration** | Once per reel the #01 (or the teased best item) lands bigger: the leak goes to white, the tag at 115 % with a light sweep, the deepest hit of the reel |
| **Appeal** | Real products, clean black structure, one lime, white serif; keynote polish with news-room speed; nothing cartoon |

**Design hierarchy (every frame):** 1. the thing named (the box, the card, the logo, the proof region) · 2. his face (full screen, box, dock) · 3. the #NN tag / the serif punchline · 4. the caption · 5. the rail and everything else.

**Effects toolkit:** motion blur on every arrival (tags, cards, PiPs, the serif stretch); a soft lime underglow under the tag (≤ 30 %); a specular light sweep across the hero tag and the CTA button (≤ 3 per reel); soft drop shadows under cards, labels and the dock; depth blur on the backdrop; the warm leak, film-burn and speed-line zoom only where §9 puts them. Never: RGB splits, glitches, shake, emoji, stickers, a grade, ghost frames inside a leak (N8).

### 8.5 Density and variety
- **A picture change every ~1.0–1.3 s** (median), counting box swaps, jump cuts, punch-ins, overlays, tags and layout moves. vb1: 145 events in 179 s; vb2: 57 in 89 s; vb3: 106 in 163 s.
- In `FMT-SPLIT` about a third of the events are top-box swaps (≈ 45), a quarter full-frame jump cuts (≈ 34), and the rest layout moves, punch-ins, tags and overlays.
- **Variety comes from the real thing changing**, not new effects: the same tag, chip and card recipes repeat all reel.
- **Repeat no clip** (N5). If the script returns to an idea, show a different shot of it.

### 8.6 Asset rules
- **The real thing, in this order:** (1) the creator's own screen recordings and screenshots; (2) the product's own official media (launch footage, renders, the announcement) when the reel is about that product: it is the subject, not a plug; (3) the brand's real logo. Official pages, videos and logos may be fetched from the web; list sources in the asset list.
- **Drawn in code:** tags, labels, request cards (a drawn orb, not a brand's assistant mark unless the reel is about it), serif text, chips, the CTA card, leaks.
- **Never:** a third-party creator's or celebrity's clip or photo (N2), a fake post (N3), a fabricated result shown as real (M11), a generated "screen" of a real product.
- **Readable UI:** push in on the region (EV-5) or crop tighter so key words read at ≥ 48 px. Phone recordings sit centred on black, never stretched; a narrow phone recording in the top box is scaled to the box height (≥ 900 px) and sits on the style's own dark ground (a rounded phone frame with a soft shadow, a lime underglow), never over its own blurred copy, never a thin strip in black.

## §10 Motion tokens, camera, finishing

### 10.1 Motion tokens (30 fps)
The element recipes are in §8.1 (G-2…G-17) and §5.5; the shared tokens:
| Token | Value |
|---|---|
| Trigger | **On or slightly after** the noun (0–0.3 s); tags 1 f after the jump cut; serif phrases on the stressed word (0–2 f) |
| Character | Fast arrivals (1–7 f) with weight (§8.0): overshoot and a short settle on tags, logo pops and the serif; the speaker's layout moves eased 8–12 f (T-14, T-15/T-16); swaps inside a box stay hard cuts |
| Blur | Every arrival carries motion or Gaussian blur on its first frames (slam, tag, serif, PiP, card) |
| Type-on | ~1 character per frame from the phrase onset |
| Slow push | +5–8% over 1–2 s inside a talking-head shot (vb1 f108 → f122), capped by the face |
| Holds | Tags 1.5–3 s; labels to the next cut; serif phrases 1–3 s; request cards across several jump cuts |
| Exits | On the next cut, under a leak, or a 1 f sideways blur-swap; nothing fades out slowly |

### 10.2 Camera
- **`FMT-SPLIT` (vb1):** framings are digital re-crops of one locked-off plate: wide (head top ~330), medium (default, head top 290–420), close punch-in (+20–35%, head top 180–270) alternating on jump cuts, plus a slow 5–8% push inside many shots. (fix) The ×2.5 wrist punch-ins that cut the head become P-20.
- **`FMT-DOC` (vb2):** two locked angles (a wide desk shot for the split, a close-up for punchlines); no punch-ins, no moves; every speaker edit is hidden by an angle change or B-roll. One-camera version: §12.6.
- **`FMT-CARD` (vb3):** one locked plate; medium default (head top 380–410), a few close punch-ins (head top ~260) and wide punch-outs (head top ~540) on the hook and CTA lines.
- **Every framing obeys §3.5.** The punch stops where the head + 60 px air would leave the frame.
- **The show layer moves itself:** source clips carry their own motion (scrolls, unfolds, product spins, camera moves in renders).

### 10.3 Layer order (back to front)
1. Background: the speaker plate (sharp, also in `SP-BACKDROP`), or black (seam, boxed clip, CTA card), or a replaced graphic (`SP-CUTOUT` background)
2. Logo tile behind the head (G-12)
3. The matted speaker (only when a G-12 tile or a cut-out is present)
4. Top-box B-roll / documentary zone / boxed clip / demo card
5. Divider (`FMT-SPLIT`) / feather to black (`FMT-DOC`)
6. Overlays on the torso: tags, labels, request cards, logo pops, PiPs, teaser icons
7. Captions (`CAP-CHIP`, `CAP-SEAM`, `CAP-LIME`) and `KT-SERIF`; the item rail (EV-2)
8. Leak / flash passes (T-03, T-04, T-09)
9. The CTA card (full frame, on top of everything)

### 10.4 Finishing
- **No added grain.** `FMT-DOC` keeps its light vignette and haze on the close-up; the other formats are clean.
- **Never pale** (§4.5): rich colour, true blacks, his frame sharp behind every card.
- **Captions and type:** soft shadow only on serif text; chips carry their own contrast.
- **Typos:** check every on-screen word against the script and the product's own spelling (M12).
- **Re-used clips:** none (N5).

### 11.2 The SFX ledger (Naman's rule)
- **Any file at most 2× in a reel,** except the one **list cue** (here `swish-boom` on every #NN tag).
- **No file on two consecutive cues;** rotate within each pool. A 10-item `FMT-CARD` reel needs the whole burn pool.
- **Density:** about one cue every 1.0–1.6 s on show beats (tags, glides, cards, montage, logos, proof punches), each tied to a picture event; the opinion and reaction lines on the full-screen speaker stay dry (the serif's sweep at most); box swaps are mostly dry (taps only in the montage).
- **Never more than two cues overlapping;** never on a trigger word's consonant (shift ±2 f).
- The tag slam, the price slam and the reveal hits are what the viewer feels; taps, swishes and keys are texture.

### 11.3 Silence and the mix
- **Go silent (no new SFX) for 0.3–0.6 s before the best item's tag, the turn line and the recap**, then land it. Keep the reference's 0.2–0.3 s gap before a card lands.
- Voice: high-pass 80 Hz, de-ess, firm compression (tight and present); SFX never mask a consonant (±2 f).
- Master: **−14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1.0 dBTP** (fix: the references peaked −0.2 to +1.6). No silent lead-in, no tail. The voice is always the loudest thing.

| **A: Standing studio, handheld mic** (vb1) | Locked-off tripod, eye level, mid-shot standing, ~35–50 mm feel, soft background blur; a handheld mic at the chin (covers the mouth/chin in ~1/3 of frames); hands free to hold and point at the thing | `FMT-SPLIT` |

### 12.2 Crop table per layout
The starting values are the framing table in §3.5 (`F-MED`, `F-CLOSE`, `F-PUNCH`, `F-WIDE`, `F-BOX`, `F-LOWER`, `F-BACKDROP`, `F-OBJECT`, `F-TIGHT`). Crop per shot from the measured head, not once per reel: a lean changes the crop.

### 12.3 Matte
- Needed for **G-12 logo behind the head** and **G-16 cut-out over a graphic**. Matte the person (alpha), place the tile/graphic behind, keep the hair edge clean (vb3's edge was clean in every checked frame).
- Not needed for `FMT-SPLIT` or `FMT-DOC`.

### 12.4 Frame rate
- The references are 30 fps; deliver 1080×1920 at 30 fps. Conform variable-frame-rate phone footage to constant 30 fps first.

### 12.5 Audio
One clean voice track; the chain in §11.

### 12.6 Adaptation: one person, one camera (marked adaptation)
vb2 uses **two cameras** (a wide desk shot and a close-up), and vb1 a **studio set with a handheld mic**. Most users film themselves with one camera, seated or standing, sometimes handheld. The same grammar works with one camera, using only devices seen in these reels:
- **The angle change becomes a re-crop of the one plate** (vb1's device): the "wide" in the documentary split is the plate scaled down into the lower zone (face ≥ 320 px); the "close-up" is the full plate punched in +20–35%, capped by the face. Change scale on every move between them, so the jump still reads as a new shot.
- **Speech edits inside one framing** are hidden the way vb1 and vb2 hide them: by a box/zone move, a B-roll swap, or a punch-in alternation on the jump cut.
- **No mic prop is needed**; a handheld selfie works in `FMT-SPLIT` once fitted by F-TIGHT inside the speaker box (the footage fills the full-width box, cropped on the face; the box made taller if the head needs it).
- **Hinglish or any language:** captions in the creator's own words and script (romanised Hinglish as they write it); product names spelled as the brand spells them; the serif punchline in the spoken language.
- **No studio set is needed**; keep one grade per reel (§4.4).
- **The hook proof (H-3)** is the user's own screen recording or result, with the user in their own box below.

## §15 Look at your frames
Not a checklist; the questions the best editor asks while scrubbing:
- **The footage frame:** is every video, recording and picture full screen or in a designed box it fills, never a sharp rectangle over its own blur, never padded with blurred bars?
- **Frame 0, sound off:** is his whole face there mid-word with a real thing, and the first words on screen? Does the slam land by f7?
- **Every named thing:** is it on screen on its noun, big, and can I read the line that proves it on a phone? Did it get its proof punch?
- **The count:** does every tag roll from the last number with weight, and does the rail tell me how many are left? Did the teased items unlock when they landed?
- **The speaker:** whole head in every frame, box and card; does he glide into the box on the name and back on the opinion? Any narrow pillar? Any frame where he sits in one layout too long?
- **Scale:** shrink the still to thumbnail size. Is the hero the first thing I see? Is any frame more than half black, backdrop or empty?
- **Captions:** every word on screen, 64–72 px, just under the chin on full screen, on the divider in the split? Does the serif punchline land under the chin and read in one glance?
- **Colour:** rich and deep, never pale? Is his frame sharp behind every card? Do the leaks stay punctuation?
- **Sound:** a slam on every tag, swishes on glides and cards, pops and taps on their pictures; no file overused; opinions dry; silence before the best item; no music?
- **Vibe:** fast, confident, generous, real. Anything cartoon, fake or cluttered comes out.
