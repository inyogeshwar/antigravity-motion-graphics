# Hot-Seat Q&A Playbook (v2, "Red Question, Elevated" edition, for Claude Opus 5.5)

> **In this plugin:** this file is the whole playbook: every measured value and recipe from the 3 reference reels is in it, and there is no other playbook file to open. All three references are 720×1280 at **30 fps**; every frame count is at 30 fps (t = f / 30) and every position is scaled ×1.5 to **1080×1920**. The per-reel notes it cites (`ah1…ah3`) are not shipped. Sounds are picked by id from SOUNDS.md; commands are in the edit skill's REFERENCE.md. The page gives you `FACE(n)` and `FACE0` (with `head_top` and `chin`) for every crop, box and punch.

**Purpose:** you (Claude) receive a talking-head video plus its script. Use this document to **ideate and build** every title, caption, layout change, cut, proof card and sound, then edit the Reel so it looks and sounds like a live coaching hot-seat: **a real person asks a real question, the speaker answers it plainly, and the edit makes the conversation impossible to scroll past.** The viewer always knows three things: what the question is (the red title), who is talking (the caption colour), and how the answer lands (the listener's face, or the speaker's own punch).

**What v2 is:** a frame-by-frame analysis of 3 Alex Hormozi reels (ah1 "You need to make bigger bets", ah2 "Should I sell my company?", ah3 "Why do my employees leave?"), **elevated ten times** by the best editor and creative director in the world, without losing what makes it work: it's raw, plain and human on purpose. The craft goes into **timing, cutting and type**: captions that are bigger and land every key word with weight, punch cuts on the lines that matter, a clean proof card for every named thing, a hand-written note card when the speaker lists things alone, and a quiet, punchy sound score where the references had only the room. All measured layouts, recipes and rules are kept here. Where a reference reel and a direction disagree, **the directions win**.

**Measured sizes are floors, not targets.** The references' captions were 48–50 px: too small on a phone. Here every caption is at least 56 px, and the one word that matters is bigger.

**Full screen or a box, never any footage or picture over its own blur** (Naman, 13 Oct): the creator's video either fills the whole frame edge to edge, or sits in a designed box (rounded corners, a rim or soft shadow, on this style's own ground) that it fills completely, cropped around the face. The same for every other medium: screen recordings, landscape clips, photos, receipts, diagrams and other people's footage either fill the frame (cover-cropped to the part that matters, or a push-in) or sit in a designed box on the style's own ground or scene (its colour, gradient, texture, grid or drawn set). Never shrink anything over a blurred or darkened copy of itself, never pad a band or box with blurred side bars, never set a card on the creator's blurred frame: a hard rectangle against its own blur looks cheap. A tight selfie stays full screen: move or shrink the text above the head, not the video; the hair may touch the top edge, eyes to chin are always in. When the whole head must be seen, use a box layout where it fits.

### The feel (read this twice)
This is **a room where a blunt, generous operator tells you the truth**. No theatrics, no graphics show; just a face, a question, and an answer so direct it stings a little. The edit is invisible in the best way: every cut happens exactly when your eye wants to see the other person, the caption is always under the mouth that's speaking, the number hits a beat before you've finished hearing it, and on the line that matters the frame punches in on his face like a camera operator who knows what's coming. It feels like **sitting in the front row of a hot-seat session**: tense, funny, useful, and over before you want it to be. It is never a motion-graphics reel, never a meme edit, never slow.

**Who the reference creator is:** a bearded, long-haired, plain-spoken business coach standing on a lit stage with a headset mic, answering founders who stand at an audience mic. He talks at a natural conversational pace (3.6–4.0 words/s), asks short diagnostic questions ("what's profit?", "do you have kids?", "are you married?"), mimes and jokes, then delivers one blunt principle ("you need to make bigger bets", "we always want what we don't have", "it's a feature, not a bug"). He never performs for the camera; the cameras find him.

**Audience (ICP):** business owners and founders (the guests run $1.2M, $5M and $44M a year), plus the much larger crowd who want to be one. They self-select on the **question** ("should I sell my company?", "why do my employees leave?") and stay for the **diagnosis**: watching someone like them get a straight answer. They trust real numbers ($44M, $5M, $1.2M, 30%, $70K) and real reactions.

**Style lineage:** a documentary event-clip edit, built from three layers (and one optional fourth):
1. **Conversation layer:** multicam event footage on long lenses: the host on stage, the guest at the audience mic, the room. Cuts follow the talk: on speaker turns, on phrase starts, and to the listener's face mid-sentence. While the host gives the real advice, the frame splits into **two stacked boxes** (host on top, guest below) so you watch the advice land.
2. **Voice layer:** small phrase captions on the chest or on the split's seam, **coloured by who is speaking**: host white upright, guest yellow italic. Quoted or role-played speech goes in italic with curly quotes, in the colour of the voice being imitated. Numbers are digits and bold; punch lines grow.
3. **Question layer:** a **red rounded title box** with white caps carries the question (or the payoff advice) from frame 0, then disappears for good.
4. **Teaching layer (ah3 only):** a real flip chart, drawn on live and filmed, cut into a split with the listener. There are no animated graphics anywhere in the references.
- **This style is not one box.** One recipe, one caption system and one title carry **five layouts** (full single, wide room, a stacked two-face split, a board split, a full-frame board) and **two reference formats** (the plain hot-seat and the hot-seat with a board teach). The speaker moves between them all the time: full screen → split → full screen → board → split. Never collapse the split into one box, and never keep one layout for the whole reel.
- **The references are multi-camera, two-person recordings.** The people who use this style usually film alone, on one camera. §3.8 records how the same grammar applies to **one speaker**: it is clearly marked **ADAPTATION**, and it uses only devices seen in these three reels plus this edition's restrained additions (EV-1…EV-5).

## §1 Procedure (follow in order)

1. **Inventory.** Probe the footage. Count the **cameras** and the **people**: a two-person conversation on two or more angles → `FMT-HOTSEAT` (+ `-BOARD` if a board is written on); one camera, one speaker → `FMT-SOLO` (§3.8, adaptation). Match each angle to a setup in §12.1 and note its head top and eye line.
2. **Transcribe** with word-level timestamps, then **correct the transcript against the audio**. In a live room the transcriber mis-hears trade words (ah3: "Europe Salon" = "your salon", "junior pair" = "junior hair extender lady", "shovel it on time" = "show up on time"); the reels' captions carry the corrected words.
3. **Map the voices.** For every phrase: who speaks (host / guest / solo speaker), and is it **quoted or role-played** (the host voicing the guest's thoughts, his own hypothetical self, a line someone would say)? The voice decides the caption colour and slant (§5.3–5.4), never the picture.
4. **Find the question and the payoff.** The question: the guest's ask in 4–6 words, first person ("SHOULD I SELL MY COMPANY?"). The payoff: the one line the host would put on a T-shirt ("you need to make bigger bets"). Pick one for the title (§6.2); default is the question in quotes.
5. **Segment** into the hot-seat arc (§7.1): `HOOK`, `SETUP`, `PROBE`, `TURN`, `BODY`, `PAYOFF`, `CLOSE`.
6. **Choose the format** (§3.0), one line on why.
7. **Plan the layouts beat by beat** (§3.0a–3.6): full single, split, cutaway, wide, board. The split starts on the first word of the key advice.
8. **Ideate 3 hook variants** (§6): title copy, frame 0, the first 3 s of caption swaps, the title's out frame, the first cut. Recommend one.
9. **Fill the beat sheet** (§13.1) and **plan the cuts** (§9.3): every cut on a turn or a phrase start at a word onset; cutaways mid-sentence. Check the budget (§9.4).
9b. **The 10× pass** (§8.0): mark the punch cuts (the 2–4 lines that matter most), every named thing's proof card, every number card, the note card (solo lists). Then **attack the plan**: where would a viewer scroll? Where is a caption too small or too low? Where does a cut come late? Where is a named tool invisible? Where did a punch cut the head?
9c. **Score it** (§11): every title, number, proof card, punch cut and payoff gets its sound, quietly, logged in the sound ledger.
10. **Trim** dead air; keep every laugh and comic pause whole (§7.6).
11. **Show the creator** the plan and stills (§13.3).
12. **Build** in reel order, look at stills of every layout (§15), score, render (§16).

## §2 Craft that never bends
- **The whole face is always visible:** hair to chin in every shot, split box, board shot and punch cut; the face decides how tight a crop or punch goes. Captions may cross the chin; the title box and proof cards never cover a face.
- **Text never sits on text:** the caption never touches the title (≥ 10 px clear), a proof card never overlaps the caption.
- **Sound in sync:** every SFX on the frame its picture lands, never on a consonant (slide ±2 f); laughs are never ducked under a sound.
- **True and exact:** the title paraphrases the real question; every number and name as said; real logos only.
- **Readable at phone size:** captions ≥ 56 px; nothing anyone must read under 26 px.
- **Loudness:** −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP; a hard stop on the last syllable.

Everything else is direction. You decide; write your reasons into the plan.

### 2.1 The house grammar (what makes it this style)
Not checks: this is what the three reference reels do every time, and why it works. Break one only with a reason in the plan. (The IDs are kept because other sections cite them.)
- M1. **A red title box from frame 0:** the question (in curly quotes) or the payoff, 2 lines of white caps in a red line-hugging box on the chest band (§5.2). On at f0, **hard out on its own frame** at the end of the credential or problem (3.83 / 4.73 / 11.53 s). Never returns. One per reel.
- M2. **Captions are coloured by the voice, not the picture.** Host (or solo speaker): white `#FFFFFF` upright. Guest (or asker): yellow `#F3D81F` italic. The host over the guest's face stays white; the guest off screen over the host's face stays yellow (ah2). No name labels: the colour is the label.
- M3. **Quoted and role-played speech is italic in curly quotes, in the colour of the voice being imitated:** the host playing the guest → yellow ("I can't do this" "forever"); the host playing his own hypothetical self → white ("I really wish" "I had all my people").
- M4. **Every spoken word is captioned**, 1–4 words per chunk (avg ~3), one line, hard swap on the onset, no animation. Fillers too ("okay", "mhm", "uhm..", "pffff").
- M5. **Numbers are digits with their unit, in bold:** $44M, $1.2M, $70K, 30%, 6 to 12, 12 weeks, 4-year.
- M6. **The whole face is always visible:** hair to chin, in every shot, crop, split box and board shot. The face decides how tight a crop goes. Captions may cross the face; the title box may not (§3.7). **(fix)** ah3's board close-up held half the host's head cut by the frame edge (100.9–103.2 s; in passing 67–69 s): a board shot shows no face or the whole face.
- M7. **The listener is seen while the key advice is given:** the stacked split or mid-sentence reaction cutaways. The split is 27–36% of the runtime; payoff sections are ~80% split.
- M8. **Cuts land on a speaker turn or a phrase start, at the word onset.** Layout changes land on the first word of a key sentence (ah1 54.07 s "you need to make…", ah2 22.20 s "you're gonna work…").
- M9. **Hard cuts only**, for every picture change, layout change, punch cut and the title's exit (§9).
- M10. **The speaker moves** between layouts; no split holds beyond ~24 s (longest 23.7 s, ah1), no single beyond ~16 s (15.6 s, ah1).
- M11. **Keep the breathing.** Trim dead air; keep laughs, comic pauses and writing pauses whole (3.52 s laugh in ah2, 1.67 s board pause in ah3).
- M12. **Facts are what was said.** The title paraphrases the real question and never invents a stake.
- M13. **Audio:** −14 LUFS, **true peak ≤ −1 dBTP**. **(fix)** The references peaked at −0.5, −0.7, −0.6 dBTP.
- M14. **End hard** on the last syllable: no end card, fade or tail.
- M15. **Spelling** of every caption and every word written on a board. **(fix)** ah3's flip chart read "ROLE/TIRE" for "tier": spell the board before filming.
- M16. **Censor every swear the same way** ("f\*ck", "sh\*t"). **(fix)** ah2 censored, ah1 showed "learn all this shit" in full.

**Never in this style:**
- N1. Music, ever. SFX only as the quiet score in §11; the room's laughter stays the loudest punctuation.
- N2. Decorative motion graphics, animated text effects, stock, B-roll inserts. The only graphics are the title box, the captions and this edition's restrained devices (EV-1…EV-5), each showing the real thing named.
- N3. Dissolves, flashes, whips, animated zooms, Ken Burns, speed ramps. The only camera moves are a filmed operator pan and **punch cuts** (EV-3): hard cuts to a tighter framing, capped by the face.
- N4. The title over a face, or a caption touching the title (≥ 10 px clear below it: ah1 916 → 926, ah2 886 → 908).
- N5. A divided layout collapsed into one box, a picture-in-picture, or a split with a gap, border, radius or shadow. The seam is a hard line at y 960.
- N6. Half a face at a frame edge or the seam.
- N7. A CTA, end card, watermark, logo bug or name lower-third (none in 3/3), or any third-party mention or plug.
- N8. A third caption colour or a coloured key word. Emphasis is bold, a size-up or a giant word (§5.5–5.6).
- N9. Captions in a box or pill, with a stroke, karaoke colouring or any animation.
- N10. Text in the bottom ~380 px, or a second title.

## §3 Formats, layouts and screen modes

### 3.0 Choosing the format
| The footage and script… | Format | Why |
|---|---|---|
| …is a recorded conversation (two people, two or more angles) where one asks and the other answers by talking | `FMT-HOTSEAT` | Faces and voices carry it; the split shows the advice landing |
| …is that conversation plus a stretch where the answer is drawn on a board | `FMT-HOTSEAT-BOARD` | The board needs its own layouts (`L-BOARDSPLIT`, `L-BOARD`) and the listener stays in view |
| …is one person, one camera, answering a question | `FMT-SOLO` (**adaptation**, §3.8) | The same grammar with only the devices the references use |
| …has no question at all (a monologue with no asker) | `FMT-SOLO`, with the payoff line as the title (HF-1) | The title still states what the viewer gets; the voice colours still separate the speaker from quoted voices |

Write one line on the choice in the plan. Never mix formats in one reel except that `FMT-HOTSEAT` becomes `FMT-HOTSEAT-BOARD` the moment a board appears.

### 3.0a The speaker's movement (every reel)
The format decides what is available; this decides **where the faces are, beat by beat.**
| Beat | Layout | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook (f0 → 2.7–4.4 s)** | `L-SPLIT`: host on top, guest below, red title on the top box's chest band, the guest's first words in yellow | Two faces + a question stop the thumb; you see the asker and the person who will answer |
| **Setup (the guest's pitch)** | `L-FULL` on the guest, cutting to the host's silent reaction and to the wide room | The guest's numbers and problem; the host's face is the viewer's proxy |
| **Probe (diagnostic Q&A)** | `L-FULL`, shot-reverse-shot on every turn (~1 s shots in a yes/no run) | The pace of the questions is the energy |
| **Turn (the advice starts)** | **Cut to `L-SPLIT` on the first word** of the key sentence | The advice and its effect, in one frame |
| **Body (principle, story)** | `L-FULL` on the host with listener cutaways; `L-WIDE` to breathe; back to `L-SPLIT` for the next big stretch | Variety without effects |
| **Board** | Operator pan to the board → `L-BOARD` close-up (≤ 3.5 s) → `L-BOARDSPLIT` ↔ `L-SPLIT` every 4–6 s | The diagram builds and the listener takes it in |
| **Payoff** | `L-SPLIT` or `L-FULL` on the host | The line lands on a face |
| **Close** | `L-FULL` on whoever speaks last ("thank you!") | Hard stop |

- **Budget:** 3–9 split stretches per reel (ah1 3, ah2 4, ah3 9 counting the board alternation). Each split 2.7–23.7 s; each full-single shot 0.9–15.6 s.
- The split is **not** used for rapid exchanges: return to full-single shot-reverse-shot for back-and-forth (ah2 36.6–43.9 s).

### 3.1 `HOOK` layout (f0 → first cut)
- Frame 0 is **`L-SPLIT`** in all three reels: host MCU in the top box (0–960), guest at the mic in the bottom box (960–1920), the **red title** already fully on the top box's chest band, the first caption (the guest's first words, yellow italic) on the seam at f0–f4.
- No cut until 2.7 s (ah3), 4.0 s (ah2) or 4.37 s (ah1).
- The title either leaves before the first cut (ah1: out at f115, cut at f131) or **bridges** it at the same screen position (ah2: cut at f120, out at f142; ah3: through 5 cuts, out at f346, 2 f before the cut at f348).

### 3.2 `L-FULL`: one speaker, full frame (54–64% of runtime)
- **Host:** knees-up centred (A1), side with the black curtain (A2), waist-up long lens (A3), wides (A4/A5). Head top 190–238, eye line 350–455, face x 518–594.
- **Guest:** medium 3/4 profile at the mic (G1), head top 130–190, eye line 370–432, face x 405–605.
- **Text:** caption on the torso, centre y 880–990; the title (hook only) on the chest band y 657–916, never the eyes. Blurred audience heads fill the bottom 15–25%.
- Used for: the guest's pitch, the probe, principle lines, reactions, the close.

### 3.3 `L-WIDE`: the room (4–10% of runtime)
- From behind or beside the audience; the guest small among seated people (face x ~470–520, head top 260–620), or the host full-body on stage (head top ~238–600).
- Foreground heads are out of focus and cropped by the bottom edge: they are texture, not subjects. The **subjects' faces** are small but whole.
- Caption on the subject's torso (y ≈ 860); if the subject is too small to have a torso there, at y ≈ 860–960 over the room.
- Used for 1.0–5.4 s at a time: under the guest's audio as a breather (ah1 16.6 s, 36.8 s; ah2 7.2 s, 120.4 s; ah3 33.9 s, 127.3 s, 142.7 s), never for a key line.

### 3.4 `L-SPLIT`: two faces, two stacked boxes (the advice layout, 27–36% of runtime)
```
┌─────────────────────────┐ 0
│   host head top 108–189 │
│      ( HOST face )      │ ← eye line y 270–410 (ah1 345, ah2 325, ah3 270–377)
│                         │
│ ┌─────────────────────┐ │ ← hook only: red title y 657–916
│ └─────────────────────┘ │
│  darkened lower edge    │
├──── caption on seam ────┤ 960  ← hard seam: no gap, no border, no radius
│ guest head top 1015–1130│
│     ( GUEST face )      │ ← eye line y 1166–1213
│                         │
│   torso, mic, room      │
│  darkened lower corners │
└─────────────────────────┘ 1920
```
- **Top box (0–960):** the host chest-up, face x 486–583, head top 108–189 (a raised hand may leave the top edge; the head never does).
- **Bottom box (960–1920):** the guest chest-up at the mic, head top 1015–1130 (55–170 px below the seam), eye line 1166–1213, face x 405–625.
- **Two divided boxes**, each a 1080×960 crop of its own camera, meeting at a hard line at y 960. Never one box, never rounded, no gap, no border.
- **Each box darkens toward its own lower edge;** the top box toward the seam (sampled `#59242A` / dark blue), which seats the caption, centred on the seam (block ≈ y 926–986).
- **Holds** 2.7–23.7 s uncut, both boxes live: the host gesturing, the guest nodding and laughing.
- **The host is always on top**, even when the guest speaks (ah2 52.47 s: the guest answers below, the host listens above).
- Used for: the hook, the key advice, a long guest answer, the payoff.

### 3.5 `L-BOARDSPLIT`: board on top, listener below (ah3, ~11% of runtime)
- The **board and the host share the top box**: the host full-body at the left (x ≈ 350, head top ≈ 162, face whole), the flip chart filling the right ~60%. Caption on the seam.
- The bottom box is the guest at the mic in the room, framed wider than in `L-SPLIT` (medium to wide among the audience, head top up to ≈ 1130), the face whole.
- **Alternates with `L-SPLIT`** (host close / guest close) every 4.2–6.0 s, **both boxes switching on the same frame** (ah3 74.60, 79.43, 84.77, 88.93, 94.93 s).
- Used while the host draws and explains the board: you see the diagram grow and the listener take it in.

### 3.6 `L-BOARD`: the board full frame (ah3, ~4% of runtime)
- The white flip chart fills the frame (≈ x 50–1080, y 110–1500), drawn on live with a black marker.
- **≤ 3.5 s per visit** (ah3: 66.5–70.0 s and 100.9–103.2 s).
- **The face:** none (only the arm and marker in frame), **or the whole face**. **(fix)** ah3 showed half the host's head cut by the left frame edge (67.0–69.0 s in passing, 100.9–103.2 s held). Our version frames the board so the head is either fully out or fully in.

- Used when the host names or writes the board's structure ("role and perks", "comp", "after 30 days", "120 days").

### 3.7 The face in every layout, and safe zones
| Layout | Where the face is while something is shown | Framing rule |
|---|---|---|
| `L-FULL` | One face, upper third (eye line 345–455), the caption on the chest below it | Head top ≥ ~130 px below the top edge; chin ≤ y ~640 while the title is up so the title never covers it |
| `L-WIDE` | Small faces among the room; foreground heads blurred and cropped | Subjects whole; foreground strangers may be cropped |
| `L-SPLIT` | **Two faces, two boxes**: host in the top box (eye 270–410), guest in the bottom box (eye 1166–1213); the caption on the seam between them | Top box head top ≥ ~100 px below y 0; bottom box head top ≥ ~50 px below the seam; chins inside their own box |
| `L-BOARDSPLIT` | Host small beside the board (top box), guest small in the room (bottom box) | Both faces whole; the board never covers the host |
| `L-BOARD` | **Absent** (hand and marker only) or **whole** | Never half a head at the edge (fix) |

- **Whole face, always** (M6): hair to chin inside its own box; if a crop would cut either, widen it. **Captions may cross a face** (no check needed); by default they sit on the chest or the seam.
- **The title never covers a face.** Shots under it are framed with the chin above ~y 640; if a cut would bring a face under the box, the title ends 2 f before that cut (as ah3 does, f346/f348).
- **Safe zones:** all text inside **x 120–960, y 560–1030** (title band 657–916, caption band just under the chin, ≈ 640–1025). Proof cards (EV-1) sit beside the head in the top band (y ≈ 140–520) or on the seam. Below y ≈ 1100 only the bottom box's face (or the note card); the bottom ~380 px is under the app's UI.

### 3.8 **ADAPTATION: one speaker, one camera (`FMT-SOLO`)**
> **This section is an adaptation, not an observation.** The references are two-person, multi-camera recordings. When the footage is one person on one camera, keep the grammar and use **these four devices seen in the reels**: the red title box, the captions coloured by who is speaking (including the role-play of another voice), the split with a hard seam, and the flip chart / whiteboard; **plus this edition's restrained additions**: the proof card (EV-1), the number card (EV-2), punch cuts (EV-3), the note card (EV-4) and the quiet SFX score (§11). Nothing else: no decorative graphics, no B-roll, no animated zooms, no music.

**The red title box (as observed, §5.2).** The question the speaker is answering, in curly quotes, first person, 4–6 words, 2 lines of white caps in the red line-hugging box on the chest band (y ≈ 660–916), from f0 until the question has been stated (3.8–11.5 s), hard out, never back. With no asker, the payoff line becomes the title (HF-1, ah1's choice).

**Captions coloured by voice (as observed, §5.3–5.4).**
- The speaker is the host: **white upright**, bold key words and numbers.
- **The asker's voice is yellow italic.** When the speaker reads out the question he was sent, the read-out words go yellow italic in curly quotes, exactly as a guest's would ("why do my" "employees leave?"). Then he answers in white.
- **Role-play is the solo version of the guest.** Every time the speaker plays another person (a customer, an employee, a partner, the viewer's inner voice), that line is **yellow italic in quotes**; his own hypothetical self is **white italic in quotes** (ah2's rule). A solo reel with no other voice lacks the main thing the colour does, so plan where the speaker voices someone else.

**The split with a hard seam (as observed geometry, §3.4).** The split stays two divided boxes meeting at y 960, no gap, no border, no radius, caption on the seam:
- **Speaker on top** (an MCU crop of the take, head top 110–190, whole face, eye line ≈ 300–400).
- **Bottom box, one of:**
  1. **The thing being discussed, filmed:** the board he drew on, the product, the notebook or page he refers to, filmed by the same camera (a full-bleed 1080×960 crop, no card, no rounded corners, no animation).
  2. **A second framing of the same take:** a wider crop (waist-up or full body) in the bottom box under the MCU on top. Both are the same moment; both faces whole. It reads as the room's second camera.
- **When:** exactly as the references: cut to the split on the first word of the key advice, hold it while the advice runs (up to ~24 s), cut back to full single on the next phrase start.

**The flip chart / whiteboard (as observed, §3.5–3.6).** If the speaker can draw the answer, film it: the walk to the board, the board close-up (≤ 3.5 s, no face or the whole face), and the board in the bottom box of the split under the speaker (the solo version of `L-BOARDSPLIT`). The board's column headers get the giant one-word caption (`CAP-GIANT`) as they are spoken.

**What changes from the references, and what doesn't.**
| Reference device | Solo version |
|---|---|
| Guest's yellow italic captions | The read-out question and every role-played voice in yellow italic quotes |
| Host top / guest bottom split | Speaker top / the thing discussed or a second framing bottom |
| Board split (host + board top, listener below) | Speaker top, board below (or board + speaker top, wider framing below) |
| Reaction cutaways to the listener | **Not available** (no listener): use the split's second framing or a punch cut (EV-3) instead; never fake a reaction |
| Multicam cuts on speaker turns | Cuts between `L-FULL`, the split and the board on phrase starts; where a pause is trimmed mid-shot, a plain jump cut (observed once, ah1 44.47 s, no zoom) |
| Laughs from the room | Only if the room was real; never add a laugh |

### 4.3 Meaning of colour
- **Red box** = "this is the question" (or "the answer you came for"). Only at the start.

- Match exposure and white balance between cameras; never regrade to a "look".

## §6 Hook system

### 6.1 What the hook must do in about 3 seconds
1. **Name the question** the viewer may have themselves: the red title, on at f0 (3/3).
2. **Put a real person with real stakes on screen:** two faces at f0; the guest's number within ~3–7 s ("$1.2M revenue" 3.2 s, "$5M in revenue" 5.5 s, "$44M" 7.0 s).
3. **Open the loop:** the host's answer comes 50–95 s later ("bigger bets" at 54 s; the flip chart at 63 s).

The title writes the question, the guest speaks the stakes, and the host's face is the answer about to come.

### 6.2 Hook formulas (`HF`)
| ID | Formula | Title | Spoken under it | Seen in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| HF-1 | **Payoff as title + credential** | The host's later advice, no quotes ("YOU NEED TO MAKE / BIGGER BETS") | "I sell the most effective non-invasive alternative to Botox, invented by my great-great-grandmother in 1889… we did $44M" | ah1 |
| HF-2 | **The universal question + a stranger's credential** | The guest's question in quotes ("SHOULD I SELL MY / COMPANY?") | "I sell performance upgrades to German car owners, doing about $5M in revenue last year" | ah2 |
| HF-3 | **Owner pain + a specific business + the host's dry reaction** | The pain in quotes ("WHY DO MY / EMPLOYEES LEAVE?") | "I have a hair extension salon in LA" / "perfect ✨" / "we do about $1.2M revenue" | ah3 |

Add-ons: the title bridging the first cut (ah2, ah3); a dry one-word host reaction with an emoji aside (ah3); one concrete detail (1889, LA, German car owners). Write **3 variants** with different formulas; recommend one.

### 6.3 Hook visuals (`H`)
| ID | Hook visual | Recipe |
|---|---|---|
| **H-1** | **Split cold open** (3/3) | f0 = `L-SPLIT`: host MCU on top (smiling, hand on chin, looking off), guest at the mic below; title on; first caption on the seam by f0–f4; uncut 2.7–4.4 s |
| **H-2** | **Title bridges the cut** (ah2, ah3) | Cut at 2.7–4.0 s to a single; the title stays put and leaves on its own frame (0.7 s later in ah2) |
| **H-3** | **Title through the problem** (ah3) | The title holds 11.5 s across 5 cuts, covering the whole problem statement, and leaves 2 f before a cut |

### 6.4 Hook timing template (≈ 0–12 s)
| t | Beat | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| f0 | Open | `L-SPLIT`, title on, the guest's first caption on f0 (ah2, ah3) or f4 (ah1) |
| 0–3 s | Who | 3–4 caption swaps, **no cut before 2.7 s**: 4 visual changes in 3 s (measured 4, 4, 4: the style hooks with the question, not with motion) |
| 2.7–4.4 s | First cut | To the host (ah2, ah3) or the guest (ah1); the title stays if the question isn't stated yet |
| 3–7 s | Stakes | The number in bold |
| 3.8–11.5 s | Title out | On its own frame |
| 6–17 s | The ask | The guest's actual question; the host's first probe |

### 6.5 Title writing rules
- 4–6 words, ALL CAPS, 2 lines, ≤ ~17 characters a line.
- Default: **the guest's question in their first-person voice, in curly quotes**, paraphrased to the version anyone might ask. Alternative: **the payoff as an imperative, no quotes**, when the payoff is short and surprising and the question is specific to one business.
- No emoji, no numbers (the numbers belong to the guest's voice), no promise the conversation doesn't keep. The question mark stays.

### 6.6 Hook sound
The room as recorded (the references), plus this edition's quiet score: a soft sub thump under the title at f0 (`abstract-hit-sub-bass-07`, low), a tick on the guest's first number (`money-2` or `selection-click`), nothing else. The first word lands on f0–f4.

### 6.6b The 10× hook
- Frame 0: the split (or the face), the red title, the first caption, all on at once; the question is readable in 1 s.
- The first number lands as a number card (EV-2) by ~3–7 s.
- The first punch cut (EV-3) on the first line that stings, if it comes in the first 10 s.

### 6.7 Endings (`E`; pick one per reel)
| ID | Ending | Recipe | Seen in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **E-1** | **Thank-you hard stop** | The payoff or "that's what I would do" on the host, cut to the guest's "thank you!" in yellow (or the guest off screen over the host), hard stop right after | ah1 (111.03 → 112.93 s), ah2 (150.03 → 150.83 s) |
| **E-2** | **Hard stop on the last practical line** | The host's last instruction ("and then you wanna break it up into smaller chunks") on a wide, hard stop; it loops back to the framework | ah3 (146.9 s) |

**No CTA** in 3/3: no comment keyword, follow card, end card or link.

## §7 Structure devices

### 7.1 The hot-seat arc (`SD-1`, every reel)
| Section | When | Layout | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| `HOOK` | 0–4.4 s | `L-SPLIT` + title | The guest says who they are |
| `SETUP` | to ~15–30 s | guest ↔ host reaction ↔ wide | The business, the numbers, the problem ("I train stylists up and then they take the book of business and dip") |
| `PROBE` | ~15–50 s | singles, shot-reverse-shot | Short diagnostic questions ("what's profit?" "about 30%") |
| `TURN` | 22–54 s | **cut to `L-SPLIT`** | The first sentence of the real advice ("you need to make bigger bets"; "you're gonna work again") |
| `BODY` | the middle 40–60% | splits, singles with cutaways, wides, the board | Principle → story proof → plan or framework |
| `PAYOFF` | last 10–15 s | split or host single | The one-line rule ("a guarantee from a world that will never give you one"; "a feature, not a bug") |
| `CLOSE` | last 1–2 s | single | E-1 or E-2 |

### 7.2 Rapid interrogation (`SD-2`)
For yes/no runs, **cut on every turn**, ~0.9–1.0 s a shot (ah2 36.6–40.2 s: "do you have **kids**?" / "no" / "are you **married**?" / "nope"). Each answer its own chunk. Singles only, never the split.

### 7.3 The advice split (`SD-3`)
Hard-cut to `L-SPLIT` on the first word of the real advice and hold it while the advice runs (12.1–23.7 s ah1, 13.2–16.5 s ah2, 9.7–12.5 s ah3). Back to a single on the next phrase start when it turns into an exchange or a story; cut sooner if the voice loses energy (the 23.7 s hold works only because the talk is strong).

### 7.4 Reaction cutaways (`SD-4`)
- Cut to the listener's silent face **mid-sentence** for 1.3–3.0 s (ah1 6.9 s host arms crossed / hand on chin; ah2 73.3, 79.8, 137.1, 142.6 s; ah3 45.0, 107.0, 112.0 s). The caption keeps the speaker's colour.
- A thumbs-up or a laugh may get a narrator aside. In principle and list stretches, alternate speaker / listener every 1.3–5 s (ah2 65.6–96.1 s).

### 7.5 The laugh hold (`SD-5`)
After a deadpan answer, hold the guest's face through the laugh (~2.5 s), then cut to the host laughing, no caption (ah2 113.8–116.3 s after "no."). Laughter is never captioned and never ducked.

### 7.6 Pauses and breathing (`SD-6`)
Trim dead air; keep the natural rhythm (3.62–3.97 words/s; 17–37 gaps > 0.3 s per reel). Keep comic pauses and laughs (3.52 s after "no."), writing pauses at the board (1.67 s), the beat before a payoff (1.07 s before "that's what I would do"). Trims hide under angle changes; inside one shot they are plain jump cuts with no zoom (ah1 44.47 s).

### 7.7 The board build (`SD-7`, `FMT-HOTSEAT-BOARD`)
1. "If you think about it like this…": the host walks to the chart; the operator **pans with him** (ah3 f1881–1910, ~1 s).
2. **`L-BOARD`** as he writes the structure (3.4 s), **`CAP-GIANT`** on each header he says ("role", "comp").
3. **`L-BOARDSPLIT` ↔ `L-SPLIT`** every 4.2–6.0 s, both boxes on the same frame, as long as he explains (31 s in ah3).
4. A second **`L-BOARD`** for the last value written ("maybe this is **120** days", 2.3 s).
5. Out to a wide or single for the principle the board proves.

The board is drawn live and filmed; nothing is overlaid or animated on it.

### 7.8 Story proof (`SD-8`)
One short true story with numbers inside the body (ah3: a nail-salon chain, "5 locations", "4-year retention", "$70K a year"; ah1: agencies at "$10K or $20K a month"), on host singles with listener cutaways, numbers bold.

### 7.9 Open loops (retention)
The title is the loop (paid off by the payoff line); each probe question makes the viewer guess before the host answers; an empty two-column chart asks to be filled. Every loop closes inside the reel.

## §8 Graphics and footage system

In this style the "B-roll" is **people**: the listener's face, the host's reaction, the room, and a board drawn live. The only drawn graphics are the title box, the captions, and this edition's restrained proof devices (§8.9).

### 8.0 The 10× layer (where the craft goes in a plain style)
**Animation principles, applied with restraint:**
| Principle | How it shows up here |
|---|---|
| **Anticipation** | The punch cut comes on the word *before* the punchline's key word, so the face is already close when it lands; the title holds 2 f of stillness before its hard out |
| **Weight** | The key word in a caption snaps from 500 to 800 and 1.0 → 1.15× over 2 f on its onset; a number card lands with a 1.04 → 1.00 settle |
| **Follow-through** | After a punch cut, the next cut is wider (back to the normal framing or a reaction), so the emphasis breathes out |
| **Staging** | One thing to look at: the speaking face, with its caption under the mouth; a proof card only while its name is said; the title only until the question is stated |
| **Timing** | The whole style is timing: cuts on turns and phrase starts, cutaways mid-sentence, comic pauses held whole, the punch on the line that matters |
| **Appeal** | Clean type, real faces, real room light; a white proof card that looks like a business card, not a graphic |

**Design hierarchy (every frame):** 1. the speaking face · 2. the caption under it (the key word heaviest) · 3. the title (hook) or a proof/number card (only on its word) · 4. the listener in the other box. If the eye goes anywhere before the face and the words, the frame is wrong.

**Effects (only these):** the per-box lower-edge vignette, a subtle shadow under proof and number cards, the 2-frame caption weight snap. Never: glows, light leaks, flashes, shakes, particles, stickers, emoji (except the one emoji aside).

### 8.1 Families (`G`)

### 8.2 Staging choreography (how a beat plays out)
1. **The voice decides the caption** (colour, slant, quotes) on the word onset.
2. **The talk decides the cut:** a turn → the new speaker; a key sentence → the split; a long sentence → a listener cutaway in the middle of it.
3. **Hold** while the voice carries (a split up to ~24 s, a single up to ~16 s).
4. **Hand-off** on the next phrase start; the caption keeps running across the cut.

### 8.5 Density and variety
- **A caption swap every ~0.75 s:** the steady pulse.
- **A cut every 2.0–2.8 s (median)**, mean 3.35–4.2 s: 27 cuts in 112.9 s (ah1), 44 in 150.8 s (ah2), 39 in 146.9 s (ah3) = **14–18 per 60 s**; the shortest ~0.93 s (ping-pong).
- **Long holds are fine** while the voice carries. The variety comes from faces, not effects.
- Layout mix: `L-FULL` 54–64%, `L-SPLIT` 27–36%, `L-WIDE` 4–10%, board layouts ≤ 15% when a board exists.

### 8.6 Asset rules
- **Everything is filmed,** except the proof cards (EV-1), which carry the **real logo** of the thing named (official files). No stock, screenshots, generated pictures or icons. The company letters on the stage wall are part of the set.
- **The board is real**, black marker, large enough to read in the split's top box, spelled right before filming (M15).
- **Cameras:** one on the host (tight enough for an MCU crop), one on the guest (tight enough for a chest-up crop), one wide; long lenses, tripod or operated.
- **Audio:** the host's headset or lav, the guest's mic, the room.
- `FMT-SOLO` (adaptation): one camera framed wide enough that the MCU crop and a wider crop both keep the whole head (§12.2), plus a filmed board or object if the bottom box shows one.

### 8.7 Signature moves (the ones a viewer would recognise)
1. The red question box over two stacked faces at frame 0.
2. Captions that tell you who is talking by colour, with no name labels.
3. The ventriloquist caption: the host acting out the guest in the guest's yellow italic.
4. The advice split: the real advice starts and you watch the guest hear it.
5. The listener cutaway mid-sentence, and the rapid yes/no ping-pong.
6. The board split re-cut on both boxes at once.

## §9 Transition system (`T`)

Every transition is a straight cut. What matters is **where** it sits (a turn, a phrase start, mid-sentence to a face) and **what** it cuts to.

### 9.1 Transition library (30 fps)
| ID | Transition | Frames | Recipe |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T-01** | **Turn cut** (default) | 0 | Cut to the new speaker on the first frame of their first word; the caption swaps on the same frame to the new voice's colour |
| **T-02** | **Reaction cut** | 0 | Cut to the listener **mid-sentence**; the caption keeps the speaker's colour and keeps swapping; hold 1.3–3.0 s; cut back on a phrase start |
| **T-03** | **Split in** | 0 | Full single at f, two boxes with a hard seam at f+1, on the **first word of the key sentence** (ah1 f1621/f1622; ah2 f666) |
| **T-04** | **Split out** | 0 | Back to a single on a phrase start (ah1 f1984/f1985; f3331 "that's what I would do") |
| **T-05** | **Panel re-cut** | 0 | Both boxes change camera on the same frame; the seam doesn't move (ah3 74.60, 79.43, 84.77, 88.93, 94.93 s) |
| **T-06** | **Title out** | 0 | The title leaves on its own frame while the picture runs: 16 f before a cut (ah1), 22 f after one (ah2), 2 f before one (ah3) |
| **T-07** | **Operator pan** | ~30 (filmed) | A real pan following the host to the board (ah3 f1881–1910). Never synthesised |
| **T-08** | **Jump cut** | 0 | A trimmed pause inside one shot, no zoom or reframe (ah1 f1334) |
| **T-09** | **Hard end** | 0 | The last frame is the last syllable. No fade, black or end card |

| **T-10** | **Punch cut** (EV-3) | 0 | A hard cut to a 1.12–1.2× framing of the same shot on the word before the key word, capped by the face; back on the next phrase |

Only the punch cut and the split-in carry a sound, quietly (§11).

### 9.2 Transition grammar
| Boundary | Use | Never |
|---|---|---|
| Frame 0 | Open on `L-SPLIT` with the title on | A fade-in, black, a zoom |
| Inside the hook | No cut before 2.7 s; then T-01 (the title may bridge it) | A cut on the title's exit frame |
| Speaker turn | **T-01** | Any effect |
| A long sentence from one person | **T-02** mid-sentence | Cutting a word |
| The key advice begins | **T-03** on its first word | Entering the split mid-phrase |
| Back-and-forth starts | **T-04**, then T-01 ping-pong | A split for a ping-pong |
| Board explanation | **T-07** → T-01 to `L-BOARD` → **T-05** alternation | A digital zoom onto the board |
| A trimmed pause in one shot | **T-08** | A punch-in to hide it |
| Last word | **T-09** | Any tail |

### 9.4 Transition budget (per 60 s)
- **Cuts (T-01, T-02, T-08):** 14–18; median shot 2.0–2.8 s.
- **Split stretches (T-03 … T-04):** 1–3; at least one in the advice section.
- **Reaction cutaways:** 2–6 per 60 s of host monologue.
- **T-05** only inside a board section, every 4.2–6.0 s. **T-06** once. **T-07** ≤ 1 per board. **T-09** once.
- **T-10 punch cuts:** 2–4 per reel.
- No other transition exists. The audio runs continuously under every cut.

## §10 Motion tokens, camera, finishing

### 10.1 Motion tokens (30 fps)
| Token | Value |
|---|---|
| Caption on / swap | Hard, on the onset frame of the chunk's first word (ah1 "I sell" at f4); no blank frame between chunks |
| Caption across a cut | Continues if the phrase continues |
| Size-up, giant word | No motion: drawn larger, hard on/off |
| Title | On at f0; off in 1 frame, on its own frame |
| Split in/out, panel re-cut | 0 f; both boxes on the same frame |
| Easing | None in the edit; only a filmed operator pan eases |
| Holds | Captions as long as the words; the title 3.8–11.5 s; a giant word the length of the word |
| Key-word snap | 2 f, 500 → 800 weight and 1.0 → 1.15× on the word's onset |
| Proof card | 3 f, 0.94 → 1.00, soft shadow; hard out |
| Number card | 1.04 → 1.00 settle in 3 f; holds 0.8–1.4 s |
| Punch cut | 0 f (a cut), 1.12–1.2×, capped by the face |

### 10.2 Camera
- Long lenses (85–200 mm feel), shallow depth of field, tripods or operated heads; slight operator drift is the only life in a held shot.
- **No animated zoom, Ken Burns or keyframed push** (checked ah3 f817–846: a straight cut, then a live reframe by the operator). This edition's punch cuts (EV-3) are hard cuts to a tighter framing, capped by the face.
- Split crops are **static** windows for the length of a stretch, sized by the face (§12.2).

### 10.3 Layer order (back to front)
1. Footage: one full frame or the two split boxes (0–960, 960–1920)
2. Per-box lower-edge vignette
3. Title box
4. Proof and number cards (EV-1, EV-2), the note card in the solo split
5. Captions with their shadows

Nothing else; the title, cards and captions never overlap.

### 10.4 Finishing
- Keep the event grade: warm skin, punchy contrast, no grain, LUT or bloom.
- **Vignette:** dark lower corners on wides; each split box darkens toward its own lower edge (the top box toward the seam, `seamShade`), roughly the lowest ~25% of the box to ~40–50% black (strength is a guess).
- Match cameras; spell-check every caption and board.

## §11 Sound (D4)

**No music bed, ever.** Sound is SFX only (the creator's direction, 12 Oct 2026): never a music track, bed, drone or loop under the voice.

### 11.0 What the references do (measured)
| Element | What the references do | Measured |
|---|---|---|
| **Voice** | The host's headset/lav and the guest's mic, as recorded | Speech median −17.6 / −18.5 / −17.7 dBFS (20 ms windows) |
| **Room** | Room tone between words, left as is | Gaps −34…−58 dB vs speech; 5th percentile −38 / −46 / −44 dB |
| **Laughs** | Audience laughter at full level, never ducked | ah2 26.8 s, 41.2 s, **114.7 s (+2.7 dB vs speech, ~3 s)**; ah3 76.2 s (+6 dB), 77.6 s |
| **Bed** | None | No tonal floor in any gap |
| **SFX** | None | Peaks at cuts are speech onsets or near-silence |
| **Loudness** | — | −14.6 / −14.7 / −14.5 LUFS; LRA 4.1 / 6.6 / 4.5 LU; true peak −0.5 / −0.7 / −0.6 dBTP |

This edition keeps the voices and the room as the core and adds **a quiet, punchy score**: the viewer should feel a number land or a card appear, never notice "sound design". It is sparser than the other styles because the voices and the laughs carry it.

### 11.1 The palette (quiet, punchy, dry)
| Role | When | Pool (ids from SOUNDS.md; rotate) | Level vs voice |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Title** | The red title at f0 | `abstract-hit-sub-bass-07` (low) | −18…−22 dB |
| **Money / number** | A number card (EV-2) or a bold money figure | `money-2`, `cash-machine-2025-02-19-03-33-58-utc-cash-machine` (once, for the biggest), `selection-click` | −20…−24 dB |
| **Proof card** | EV-1 lands | `pop-up-pop-up`, `game-pop-up-2025-08-27-06-39-58-utc-game-pop-up-1`, `appear-enter` | −22…−26 dB |
| **Punch / split-in** | EV-3, T-03 | `woosh-2025-08-27-02-12-31-utc-woosh-03`, `air-low-woosh` (very low) | −24…−28 dB |
| **List** | EV-4 note card items, EV-5 tally | `objects-drawing-board-marker-on-paper-drawing-line-medium-length-02-vadi` (the list cue), `0552611` → `0552621` → `0552631` | −22…−26 dB |
| **Payoff** | The one-line rule | `cinematic-hits-2025-02-19-18-27-16-utc-cinematic-hit-5` (very low) or `impactfadeinout-tfx2697` | −18…−22 dB |
| **Board** | A real marker on the flip chart | the board's own sound as recorded; no file over it | — |

**Never in this style:** meme sounds, comic stingers, risers, glitches, whooshes on plain cuts, anything over a laugh, any music.

### 11.2 The SFX ledger (Naman's rule, unchanged)
- **Any file at most 2× in a reel,** except the one **list cue** (here the marker stroke on EV-4 items).
- **No file on two consecutive cues;** rotate within each pool.
- **Density:** sparser than the other styles, about one cue every 1.5–2.5 s on average across the reel, each tied to a picture (title, number, card, punch, split, payoff); plain turn cuts and reaction cutaways get nothing.
- **Never more than two cues overlapping;** never on a consonant; **never on a laugh** (the laugh is the sound).

### 11.3 Silence and the room
- **Keep the room:** comic pauses, laughs and the beat before a payoff stay as recorded; **go silent (no new SFX) for 0.5–1 s before the payoff**, then the low payoff hit (or nothing).
- The host's "boop" and "pffff" are his own voice, captioned as asides, never replaced by a file.

### 11.4 Mix
High-pass ~80 Hz per mic, light compression, gentle de-ess; keep the room. **−14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP** (fix: all three references peaked above −1). Let laughs sit as recorded; the voice is always louder than any SFX.

## §15 Look at your frames
Not a checklist; the questions the best editor asks while scrubbing:
- **Frame 0, sound off:** can I read the question in a second, and see who's asking and who's answering?
- **Their video and every picture:** full screen, or in a designed box it fills on the style's own ground? Never a sharp rectangle (footage, recording, landscape clip, photo) over its own blur, never blurred side bars, never a card on their blurred frame?
- **Every cut:** did it come exactly when I wanted to see the other face? Is any cut late?
- **Every caption:** can I read it on a phone? Is it right under the mouth that's speaking, in that voice's colour, with the key word landing heaviest?
- **Every face:** whole, hair to chin, in every box, shot and punch?
- **Every named thing:** is its proof card there on the word, small and clean, off the face?
- **Every number:** does it land as a beat?
- **Sound:** quiet, punchy, on the picture, no file overused, nothing over a laugh, no music?
- **Vibe:** raw, plain, human. Anything that looks like a motion-graphics reel or a meme edit comes out.

## §16 Build + render

- **Pipeline:** the plugin's edit skill: plan in the edit brief (format, voice map, layout map, beat sheet, sound ledger), draw every frame in `reel.js` (`frame(t)`), look at stills of every layout, score (`plan/sounds.json`), then storyboard and render.
- **Fonts (bundled):** **Montserrat** (`Montserrat-VF.ttf`, weights 500 and 800) and **Montserrat Italic** (`Montserrat-Italic-VF.ttf`, 500 and 800 italic) for the title and every caption; **Noto Color Emoji** (`NotoColorEmoji-Regular.ttf`) for an emoji aside. Pre-paint the curly quotes “ ”, the asterisk, ✨ and $ % before the first frame.
- **Component map** (helpers you write once per reel in `reel.js`):

| Component | Role |
|---|---|
| `Shot(cam, window)` | One camera's frame, cropped to a fixed window (full frame or a 1080×960 box), from the cut list |
| `Split(top, bottom)` | Two `Shot`s stacked at 0–960 and 960–1920, hard seam, no gap/border/radius |
| `BoxVignette(rect)` | The lower-edge darkening of a box or a full frame (§10.4) |
| `TitleRed(lines, tOut)` | The tetris box: one rounded rect per line (radius 16–18), merged, red `#FB0000`, white Montserrat 800 caps; on at f0, off at `tOut` |
| `Caption(chunk)` | One line at the layout's y: voice colour, italic for the other voice and quotes, bold spans, size factor (1.0 / 1.4 / 2.0), `capShadow` |
| `GiantWord(word, t0, t1)` | `CAP-GIANT` with `giantShadow` |
| `Aside(text, emoji)` | `CAP-ASIDE` |
| `CutList` / `LayoutList` | The beat sheet as data: which shot or split is on at frame f |
| `ProofCard(logo, name)` / `NumberCard(value, unit)` | EV-1 / EV-2 |
| `PunchCut(scale)` | EV-3, capped by `FACE(n)` |
| `NoteCard(items, onsets)` | EV-4, hand-written items on a white index card |
| `Tally(n, of)` | EV-5 |

- **Face check while drawing:** for every window, confirm the head top and chin are inside the box (§12.2). Widen the window rather than cut the face.
- **Final:** 1080×1920, 30 fps, H.264, AAC; check the frame count, loudness (−14 LUFS, ≤ −1 dBTP) and a contact sheet of the final file.
