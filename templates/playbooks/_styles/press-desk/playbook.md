# Investigative Explainer Playbook (v2, "Receipts on Screen, Elevated" edition, for Claude Opus 5.5)

> **In this plugin:** this file is the whole playbook: every measured value and recipe from the 3 reference reels is in it, and there is no other playbook file to open. **Frame counts are at each reference's fps:** jh1 `f@24` (23.976), jh2 `f@25`, jh3 `f@30`; t = f / fps; durations are also given in seconds. Positions are on 1080×1920. The per-reel notes it cites (`jh1…jh3`) are not shipped. Sounds are picked by id from SOUNDS.md; commands are in the edit skill's REFERENCE.md. The page gives you `FACE(n)` and `FACE0` (with `head_top` and `chin`) for every speaker frame.

**Purpose:** you (Claude) receive a creator's video (a talking-head take, a voice-over, or both) plus its script. Use this document to **ideate and build** every format choice, receipt, map, card, caption, transition and sound, so the Reel looks and sounds like an investigative explainer in the manner of the Johnny Harris network: a brisk, clean narrator, and a screen that keeps showing **proof** (real headlines typing on with their source, press photos, archive, maps with cut-out heads, data cards, comparison cards), one picture per claim, built with such craft that the viewer feels they're watching an investigation unfold live.

**What v2 is:** a frame-by-frame analysis of 3 reels from the Johnny Harris network (jh1 "The King of FIFA just launched a new scheme": a faceless investigative explainer; jh2 "Japanese baseball looks a lot different than American baseball": a faceless archival explainer; jh3 "A place is so much more than the headlines": a two-camera podcast clip), **elevated ten times** by the best motion designer and creative director in the world: the receipts grammar kept whole, with the creator's face brought back into the story between blocks, every decode, write-on and tile animated with weight and follow-through, a visible pile of evidence that pays off at the end, readable small print, and a full newsroom sound score. All measured layouts, recipes and rules are kept. Where a reference reel and a direction disagree, **the directions win**.

**Measured sizes are floors, not targets.** The references' small print (≈ 16–22 px body, labels, tags) is texture at best; here anything a viewer is meant to read is ≥ 40 px, and the canvas camera pushes in when the line matters.

**Full screen or a box, never any footage or picture over its own blur** (Naman, 13 Oct): the creator's video either fills the whole frame edge to edge, or sits in a designed box (rounded corners, a rim or soft shadow, on this style's own ground) that it fills completely, cropped around the face. The same for every other medium: screen recordings, landscape clips, photos, receipts, diagrams and other people's footage either fill the frame (cover-cropped to the part that matters, or a push-in) or sit in a designed box on the style's own ground or scene (its colour, gradient, texture, grid or drawn set). Never shrink anything over a blurred or darkened copy of itself, never pad a band or box with blurred side bars, never set a card on the creator's blurred frame: a hard rectangle against its own blur looks cheap. A tight selfie stays full screen: move or shrink the text above the head, not the video; the hair may touch the top edge, eyes to chin are always in. When the whole head must be seen, use a box layout where it fits.

**The board is filled, not dotted.** The 12 Oct test drew a royal-blue page with small terminal boxes (≈ 340 px wide, 20 px text) and a last frame that was nearly empty blue. On a phone that reads as empty. So:
- **The hero object of a beat** (the file, the card, the terminal, the GitHub page) is **≥ 860 px wide** (x ≈ 110–970); its key line ≥ 48 px.
- **A group** (5 terminals, a team file, tiles) fills **x ≈ 80–1000** and **y ≈ 260–1320**: 2 columns of ≈ 440 px cards, never a cluster of small boxes in the middle. Each card's title ≥ 40 px; anything inside it that the voice names ≥ 40 px.
- **No empty board:** if more than half of y 200–1350 is plain blue for more than ~0.7 s, push in on what's there or bring the next element. A shrink-to-corner exit (the team file) is a 6–8 f move, not a held frame.
- **The selfie stays big:** a tight selfie stays full screen, edge to edge (the hair may touch the top edge, eyes to chin always in), never shrunk into a pillar or over its own blur. Captions may cross the chest.

### The feel (read this twice)
This is **a journalist pulling documents out of a folder, one by one, and laying them on your desk**: real mastheads decoding on royal blue, the headline typing itself, a lime highlighter running under the exact words as he says them, a map lighting up the country he names, faces arriving as photo tiles. Between the documents he looks up at you and says what it means. It feels **smart, fast and trustworthy**: curious, never sensational; designed, never decorated. Every frame is evidence or a person; nothing is there to fill time. If it looks like a slideshow of screenshots, it's wrong; if it looks like a hype reel, it's wrong too.

**Who the reference creator is:** an American video journalist who explains geopolitics, history and places through maps, documents and travel. His network's short explainers ("sports and geopolitics, decoded") never show the narrator and are built from receipts; his podcast with co-host Iz Harris is casual, mid-thought, fillers left in. In both, authority comes from **showing the evidence**, not from performing.

**Audience (ICP):** English-speaking, curious adults (news, sport, politics, history, travel) who want to know **why** something is happening. They trust a real headline with its masthead more than an opinion, and they stay for a map that moves.

**Style lineage:** a documentary-journalism explainer in three layers:
1. **Voice:** one tight narrator at 3.1–3.4 words/s, breaths cut (explainers), or a real conversation at 3.5 words/s with its pauses kept (studio). The voice never waits for the picture.
2. **Proof:** real artefacts full screen or in **one** rounded card on a flat brand canvas: headlines, press photos, archive, maps, charts, a comparison deck.
3. **Brand:** royal-blue grained canvas, lime as the only highlight, monospace captions in a black box, hard cuts, SFX on the graphics (the explainer references ran a low dark bed; we don't). The studio kit: a warm real room, white captions on a blue sticker box, no music.
- **This style is not one layout.** One network, two brand kits, **three formats** (§3): a receipts explainer (jh1, six layouts), an archival explainer (jh2, five layouts) and a studio conversation clip (jh3, three layouts).
- **Two of the three references never show the narrator; the people who use this playbook film themselves.** §3.5 (**ADAPTATION**) runs the faceless grammar with the creator's talking-head footage, using the devices seen in these reels plus this edition's elevations.

## §1 Procedure (follow in order)

1. **Inventory.** Probe the footage. Decide what you have: a **talking-head take** (→ `FMT-STUDIO` or `ADAPT`), a **voice-over** only (→ `FMT-RECEIPTS` / `FMT-ARCHIVE`), or both (→ `ADAPT`). Identify the setups (§12.1); conform variable-frame-rate footage to a constant rate.
2. **Transcribe** with word timestamps. Captions are English as spoken, from the script, corrected to what was said; check every name.
3. **Choose the format** (§3.0) and write one line on why.
4. **Segment:** `HOOK` (≤ 4.6 s), `SETUP`, body `BLOCK`s (one idea each), the `PIVOT` question if any, `PAYOFF`, `CTA`/`END`.
5. **Build the receipts list** (§13.4): every factual claim → its real source (masthead, date, exact headline) or real artefact; every proper noun → its picture. **Never invent a headline or a source** (FIX-9); an unsourced claim gets a photo or map instead.
6. **Speaker map (`ADAPT` only, §3.5):** mark each sentence `SPEAKER` or `BLOCK`.
7. **Classify every sentence** with §8.4; mark its trigger word and caption chunks (2–5 words).
8. **Ideate 3 hooks** (§6): formula, frame 0, the first 3 s of changes, the first two captions. Recommend one.
9. **Fill the beat sheet** (§13.1): fast cuts for people and drama, long built holds for facts (M3).
10. **Plan transitions** (§9.3) and check the budget (§9.4).
11. **The 10× pass and the score** (§8.0, §11): for every block, its hero, how each element anticipates, lands and follows through, where the push-to-line goes, how the evidence stack grows; then score every event and log the sound ledger. **Attack the plan:** where is the creator gone for more than ~15 s? Where is small print meant to be read? Where is a frame still? Where does a receipt appear without its beat? Fix it in the plan.
12. **Produce the assets** (§8.6); list anything missing.
13. **Show the creator** the plan and stills (§13.3).
14. **Build block by block,** look at the landing frames (§15), score, render (§16).

## §2 Craft that never bends
- **The whole face is always visible:** the speaker's face at every framing and reframe (a tight selfie stays full screen as shot, the hair may touch the top edge, eyes to chin always in; never shrunk over its own blurred frame, never cropped tighter); a subject's face in a still, tile or archive shot is whole too. Captions may cross the face.
- **Text never sits on text:** no caption over a card; graphics end at y ≤ 1320; a lit word never under a tile.
- **Sound in sync:** every SFX on the frame its picture lands, never on a consonant (slide ±2 f).
- **True and exact:** every headline word for word with its real masthead; no invented source, view count or comment (FIX-9, FIX-10).
- **Readable at phone size:** anything meant to be read ≥ 40 px; the hero object ≥ 860 px wide; a group fills the board.
- **Loudness:** −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1.0 dBTP; a designed card at the end, then a hard stop.

Everything else is direction. You decide; write your reasons into the plan.

### 2.1 The house grammar (what makes it this style)
Not checks: this is what the three reference reels do every time, and why it works. Break one only with a reason in the plan. (The IDs are kept because other sections cite them.)
- M1. **Receipts on screen.** Every factual claim is backed by its source on screen: a headline canvas (real masthead + headline + lit body, P-01), a photo, an archive clip, a map or a chart (jh1: Financial Times, The Guardian, Yahoo Sports). It looks like journalism, not opinion.
- M2. **One picture per noun, cut on the noun** ("Donald Trump" → Trump at 3.13 s; "world leaders" → the card at 45.80 s; "MLB" → the MLB logo), or on a phrase boundary; **never mid-word**.
- M3. **Two tempos (explainers):** picture cuts of 0.25–1.3 s for people and drama; built graphic holds of 6–15 s for facts that are **never static** (elements build per phrase, the camera drifts 1–2 px/frame; jh1 FT canvas 15.35 s). Nothing still for more than ≈ 2 s.
- M4. **One caption system per format, always on while the voice speaks** (§5): hard swaps on the phrase onset, 2–5 words, ≤ 2 lines.
- M5. **Graphics above the caption band:** cards and panels end at y ≤ 1320; `CAP-MONO` starts at y 1385. No caption over a card (jh1, jh2).
- M6. **Novelty wipes only in the hook** (money stack, fly-through, whip; jh1 0–4.7 s). The body uses hard cuts plus at most **one** section wipe per reel (T-11) after a ≈ 0.4 s pause (jh2).
- M7. **The whole face is always visible** (craft rule): the speaker's face, hair to chin, inside the frame at every framing, reframe and crop; how tight a framing goes is decided by the face. **Captions may cross the face**; no check needed. A face that is the **subject** of a still or archive shot is also whole (FIX-6); bodies, hands and action may be cropped.
- M8. **The face and the explainer never share the screen.** No reel has a PiP, a split or a cut-out over a graphic; every non-face element replaces the face by a hard cut (jh3). In `ADAPT` speaker and block alternate by **hard cuts**.
- M9. **Pauses:** explainer VO ≤ 0.5 s (jh1's longest 0.49 s), except one ≈ 0.4 s beat before the section wipe (jh2 0.44 s); studio keeps natural pauses of 0.4–0.75 s; **the first word lands by f3** in every format (FIX-4).
- M10. **A comparison has one fixed code** (§4.3) for the whole reel (jh2 US vs Japan).
- M11. **Text lands on the spoken word:** decodes, sweeps, lit words and pill labels finish on their word (±3 f).
- M12. **Audio:** −14 LUFS, **true peak ≤ −1.0 dBTP** (FIX-1). The references put SFX only on graphic entrances; this edition scores every graphic event (§11), never a plain photo cut; studio and `ADAPT` speaker beats stay nearly dry (jh3).
- M13. **End on a designed card** (subscribe card, platform-UI bridge, or silent link card); hard stop.
- M14. **Spelling:** every on-screen word and name exact (FIX-2); verbatim captions drop stutters (FIX-3).
- M15. **Facts only from the script or supplied sources;** a quoted headline is word for word with its real masthead.

**Never in this style:**
- N1. A narrator inside `FMT-RECEIPTS` / `FMT-ARCHIVE`. In `ADAPT` the speaker appears only full frame, in speaker beats.
- N2. Animated, coloured, highlighted or word-by-word captions.
- N3. Dissolves, flashes, glitches, light leaks, zoom-blurs, film burns. Only §9.
- N4. Invented headlines, misattributed sources, articles that never ran, invented view counts, fake comments (FIX-9, FIX-10).
- N5. The reference network's names, logos, taglines or QR (FIX-8): end cards and links are the creator's own.
- N6. Film or TV clips without rights (FIX-9).
- N7. Music, ever; SFX over the studio format or under the speaker's talking (a single soft crossing whoosh at a speaker ↔ block cut is the only exception).
- N8. A zoom, punch or Ken Burns on the speaker inside a shot (jh3 has none). Framing changes only between segments (T-13), with the whole face.
- N9. More than one card idea at a time (one card, one tile row under one headline, or one deck).
- N10. A black tail without a designed card, or a duration that doesn't match the edit.

### 3.5 ADAPTATION: the speaker + explainer blocks (`ADAPT`)

## §6 Hook system

### 6.1 What the hook must do in about 3 seconds
1. **Name a powerful subject and an action** ("The king of FIFA just launched a new scheme"), **a difference you can see** ("Japanese baseball looks a lot different"), or **a thought in motion** ("I think another thing it teaches me…").
2. **Hand over a stake:** money and a famous name ("…and it involves Donald Trump"), or a promise ("and here's why").
3. **Show it literally:** money covers the screen on "to make money"; unfamiliar archive under "Japanese baseball"; the speaker's hand draws the "box".

### 6.2 Hook formulas (`HF`)
| ID | Formula | Spoken pattern | Seen in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **HF-1** | Nickname + scheme + celebrity | "The [title-like nickname] just [did X] to [make money / gain power], and it involves [famous name]." | jh1 |
| **HF-2** | Visible difference + "here's why" | "[X] looks a lot different than [Y], and here's why." | jh2 |
| **HF-3** | Cold open, mid-thought insight | "I think another thing [this] teaches me is like, the way we [habit]." | jh3 |
- The second clause is the **open loop**; answer it at 15–40% of runtime (jh1 13–23 s of 80; jh2 25 s of 59). A **pivot question** (SD-1) re-opens it later.
- Write **3 variants** (different formulas or frame-0 pictures) and recommend one.

### 6.3 Hook visuals (`H`)
| ID | Visual | Recipe |
|---|---|---|
| **H-1** | ECU still + punch-out + object wipe | f0 ECU of the protagonist (whole face, FIX-6), drift, caption on f0 · f10@24 punch-out to MCU (≈ −40%, T-07) · 1.0–2.3 s money stack on the payoff phrase (T-03), revealing the next shot from the bottom (jh1) |

## §8 Graphics system

### 8.0 The 10× layer (what the best motion designer adds to every receipt)
**Animation principles, applied to this style:**
| Principle | How it shows up here |
|---|---|
| **Anticipation** | The 4-frame empty canvas before a decode; a blinking lime cursor (2 blinks) where the headline will start; a tile's shadow appears 2 f before the tile |
| **Follow-through & overlap** | Headline words land in an overlapping wave (each starts before the last finishes); the pin pops to 1.15 and settles; the connector overshoots its elbow by 4 px and snaps back; the parked deck card trails the active one by 2 f |
| **Squash & weight** | Tiles push in and shove the row with a 1-frame compression; the money stack's bills land with a slight bounce |
| **Arcs** | The fly-through object, map pans and the avatar's rise all travel on arcs |
| **Slow in / slow out** | Ease-out arrivals, ease-in-out re-frames and pans; decodes and write-ons are linear by nature |
| **Staging** | One hero per frame: when the sweep lands, the rest of the headline dims to `dimInk` and the camera pushes toward the swept words |
| **Secondary action** | The canvas camera drifts constantly; grain lives; archive pushes inside; the deck's clip swaps every ~1.2 s |
| **Timing** | People and drama fast (0.25–1.3 s), documents slow and built (6–15 s), the speaker as long as the thought |
| **Exaggeration** | One payoff per reel goes bigger: the evidence stack fans out across the screen (EV-1) |
| **Appeal** | Clean blue, crisp white type, one lime, real paper and photos; it looks like a newsroom's best designer made it |

**Design hierarchy (every frame):** 1. the swept / lit words, or the subject's face · 2. the source's masthead · 3. the creator's face (speaker beats) · 4. the caption · 5. everything else, dimmed.

**Effects toolkit:** fine grain on the canvases; a lime underglow (≈ 20% lime, 12 px blur) under the sweep; a soft depth push on stills (a 2.5D parallax between subject and background on hero photos); a subtle drop shadow under tiles and cards (this edition only; the references had none); the decode's character flicker. Never: flashes, glitches, light leaks, film burns, zoom-blurs.

1. **Cut on the noun** (a canvas cuts on the sentence's first word): people → G-1, a source → G-2, a place → G-4, a count → G-6, a comparison → G-7.

3. **Keep it alive:** the camera drifts or eases; the clip inside a card swaps every 0.6–1.3 s.
4. **Leave by a hard cut** when the subject changes. Build inside a scene; cut between scenes.

### 8.3a Frame-by-frame recipes
**A. Headline canvas (P-01; jh1 f190–222@24, 7.93–9.26 s).**
1. Hard cut to an empty `canvas`; captions hidden; **hold 4 f**.
2. `HL-SOURCE` **decodes** over ≈ 14 f: characters cycle through 2–4 random glyphs and settle; the sheet shows a few letters resolving at scattered positions first.
3. `HL-HEAD` **writes on** one word every ≈ 2 f, each word 30% → 100% in 2–3 f; complete in ≈ 20 f.
4. The lime **pin dot** (Ø ≈ 14 px) appears at the headline's end; the 2 px connector draws from the left edge (y ≈ 985 in the build framing), elbows up and runs under the headline to the dot (≈ 10 f).
5. `HL-BODY` sits dim; the spoken words light white. `HL-SWEEP` runs ≈ 1 letter/frame on the key phrase (±3 f), the rest dims.

7. **Tiles:** the first fades in over 14 f (build framing x ≈ 657–899, y ≈ 822–1133); later tiles push in from the left (6 f), shoving earlier ones right, edge to edge.
8. Exit by a hard cut.

**B. Comparison deck (P-11; jh2 f634–655@25).**

### 11.2 The SFX ledger (Naman's rule, unchanged)
- **Any file at most 2× in a reel,** except the one **list cue** (here `paper-turn-page` for each card added to the evidence stack).
- **No file on two consecutive cues;** rotate within each pool.
- **Density:** about one cue every 1.2–1.8 s **inside blocks**, each tied to a picture; plain photo cuts and speaker beats get nothing (one soft crossing at most).
- **Never more than two cues overlapping;** never on a trigger word's consonant (shift ±2 f).
- Keys, ticks and markers are texture; the section booms, the chapter boom and the payoff are what the viewer feels.

### 11.3 Silence and the mix
- **Go silent (no new SFX) for 0.4–0.6 s before the pivot question and the payoff**, then land the boom.
- Studio gaps gated to ≈ −70 dBFS; the link card is silent.
- Explainer voice: tight and compressed (LRA 2–3 LU), high-pass 80 Hz, de-ess, breaths cut. Studio: natural dynamics (LRA ≈ 8), laughter and overlap kept.
- Master: −14 LUFS, **true peak ≤ −1.0 dBTP** (FIX-1). The voice is always the loudest thing.

- Stills ≥ 1080 px on the short side after the crop where possible; archive keeps its texture; headlines are live text from the real article; rights as §8.6.

- **Audio:** one clean VO (or the creator's take as VO under blocks); studio and `ADAPT` voice gated, natural pauses kept.

## §15 Look at your frames
Not a checklist; the questions the best editor asks while scrubbing:
- **Frame 0, sound off:** is there a story on screen (the creator mid-thought, or the hook picture) with its caption?
- **Their video and every picture:** full screen, or in a designed box it fills on the style's own ground? Never a sharp rectangle (footage, recording, landscape clip, photo) over its own blur, never blurred side bars, never a card on their blurred frame?
- **Every receipt:** real masthead, headline word for word, the sweep landing on the spoken words, the camera going to the line, the lit words readable on a phone?
- **The creator:** back on screen every 8–15 s, whole face, caption under the chin? Is any block longer than it earns?
- **Scale:** shrink the still to thumbnail size. Is the board filled by one big thing (≥ 860 px) or a group edge to edge, or is it blue with small boxes? Is the selfie card wide, not a pillar?
- **The case:** can I see the evidence stack growing, and does the fan-out pay it off?
- **Every frame:** one hero; nothing still for more than ~2 s; nothing small that I'm meant to read?
- **Sound:** keys, paper, marker, a few booms, on their pictures, no file overused, nothing under his talking, no music?
- **Vibe:** smart, fast, trustworthy. Anything sensational or decorative comes out.

## §16 Build + render

- **Pipeline:** the plugin's edit skill: plan in the edit brief (beat sheet, receipts list, transition map, sound ledger), draw every frame in `reel.js` (`frame(t)`), look at stills of every landing frame, score (`plan/sounds.json`), then storyboard and render.
- **Fonts (bundled):** `JetBrainsMono-VF.ttf` (600), `Unbounded-VF.ttf` (600 / 800), `BarlowSemiCondensed-Bold.ttf`, `InterTight-VF.ttf` (500–700), `ArchivoBlack-Regular.ttf` (fallback). Pre-paint `$ ✕ “ ” ’` before the first frame; draw flags as images.
- **Components** (written once per reel in `reel.js`):

| Component | Role |
|---|---|
| `Canvas(hex)`, `VCam(t)` | Grained flat canvas; the virtual camera (drift, T-12, push-in, pan) |
