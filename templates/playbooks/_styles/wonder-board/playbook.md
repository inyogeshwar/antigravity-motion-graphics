# Science Cut-Away Explainer Playbook (v2, "Show Every Noun, Elevated" edition, for Claude Opus 5.5)

> **In this plugin:** this file is the whole playbook: every measured value and recipe from the 3 reference reels is in it, and there is no other playbook file to open. Frame counts are measured at the references' **24 fps** (all three are 720×1280, positions scaled ×1.5 to 1080×1920): convert with t = f / 24 when you draw (`frame(t)` runs at the render fps). The per-reel notes this file cites (`ca1…ca3`) are not shipped. Sounds are picked by id from SOUNDS.md; commands are in the edit skill's REFERENCE.md. *(guess)* marks an estimate, not a measurement. The page gives you `FACE(n)` and `FACE0` (with `head_top` and `chin`) for every speaker frame.

**Purpose:** you (Claude) receive a talking-head video of the creator plus its script. Use this document to **ideate and build** every cut-away, pointer, label, receipt, board, caption, cut and sound, then edit the Reel so it looks and sounds like a fast, curious, high-production science explainer: **the voice never stops, and every noun it says is on screen as its own full-frame picture**, built with such craft that the viewer can't look away and can't stop asking "wait, how?".

**What v2 is:** a frame-by-frame analysis of 3 finished reels by Cleo Abram (an American science and technology explainer: ca1 "How This Giant Animal Could FLY", 59.1 s; ca2 "They Found Something Hidden In The Amazon", 36.7 s; ca3 "This Microscope Is Insane", 53.7 s; cut lists, layout and colour measurements, caption timing, motion-graphic frame recipes and audio), **elevated ten times** by the best motion designer and creative director in the world. The "show every noun" grammar is kept whole; the craft goes up everywhere: cuts that carry the eye from one picture to the next, pointers and labels that move with weight and follow-through, scale you can *feel* (the camera pulls back until the giant thing is beside you), a look-inside lens, still pictures that breathe in 2.5D, captions you can read on a phone, and a bright, curious sound score on every pointer, label and reveal. All measured layouts, recipes and rules are kept. Where a reference reel and a direction disagree, **the directions win**.

**Measured sizes are floors, not targets.** The references' captions (≈ 43 px), source tags (≈ 16 px caps), "?" marks (30–40 px) and the board column (≈ 490 px wide) were too small for a phone. Here they are larger, and the detail being explained owns the frame:
- **Full screen or a box, never any footage or picture over its own blur** (Studio Standard): the creator's video, a screen recording, a landscape clip, a photo or a diagram either fills the whole frame edge to edge (cover-crop to the part that matters, or a push-in), or sits in a designed box (rounded, rimmed or softly shadowed, on this style's own ground, colour, gradient, texture, grid or drawn set) that it fills completely. Never shrink anything inside the frame over a blurred or darkened copy of itself, never pad a box, band or panel with blurred side bars, never set a card on the creator's own blurred frame. A tight selfie stays full screen as shot: move the text, not the video (eyes to chin always in; the hair may touch the top edge). When the whole head must show, use a box sized so the footage fills it with the head inside. (A matte cut-out over a plate is fine: it has no box edge.)
- **Captions** 64–72 px (§5.2); **kinetic labels** ≥ 72 px caps; **source tags** ≥ 24 px caps; **"?" marks** ≥ 72 px; anything named aloud and written on screen ≥ 48 px.
- **The hero of a picture** (the board, the specimen, the receipt, the scale line-up) is **≥ 860 px wide**; a group (board rows, scans, pins) fills **x ≈ 90–990**, never a small cluster in the middle.
- **No mostly-empty frame:** if more than half of y 200–1350 is plain background for more than ~0.7 s, push in on what is there or bring the next element.
- **The selfie stays big:** a selfie too tight for the whole-face rule stays full screen as shot (eyes to chin always in; the hair may touch the top edge): move the text, never shrink the video over its own blurred frame; the `L-9` card is ≥ 940 px wide and filled by the footage, never a narrow pillar (§12.2).

### The feel (read this twice)
This is **a friend who just found out something incredible and can't wait to show you**: she points at the lens, and before the sentence is over you are *looking at the thing*: the giant animal beside a person, the hidden structure under the trees, the blurry image snapping crisp. A bright lime arrow is already on the exact detail she means; the word she just said is typed on screen with a glint; the camera pulls back until you gasp at the size. Every picture leads into the next, so the reel feels like one continuous discovery, never a slideshow. It feels **curious, fast, delighted and honest** (every borrowed picture says where it came from). If it looks like a stock-footage montage, it's wrong; if it looks like hype with nothing proved, it's wrong too.

**Who the reference creator is:** an energetic, wide-eyed explainer who talks fast (3.4–3.8 words/s) in plain English about science: giant animals, archaeology from the air, microscopes. She films herself selfie-style in her office (phone at arm's length, big hand gestures into the lens, raised eyebrows, a scrunched "can you believe this" face), then narrates everything else as one continuous voice-over over pictures. Wonder, not hype: "really freaky", "so many more than we ever knew about", and then she proves it.

**Audience (ICP):** curious general viewers on Shorts/Reels with no science background. They want **to see the thing** (the animal next to a person, the structure under the trees, the blurry vs the crisp image), to leave with one mechanism they can repeat, and to want the next video.

**Style lineage:** a documentary-explainer edit in four layers:
1. **Voice:** one continuous voice-over, every breath cut (gaps ≤ 0.25–0.28 s); white heavy-caps captions on every word (one fixed height over pictures; just under the chin on the face).
2. **Picture:** every concrete noun, number, place and step gets **its own full-frame picture**, cut in 1–3 frames before the word: documentary clips, the evidence itself (a scan, a page, a figure), labelled CG depictions, bespoke boards that return and transform.
3. **Pointer:** **one lime** (`#C3FD00`) that means "look here": arrows, labels typed on the word, a highlighter on the spoken phrase, "?" marks, the subscribe button; plus a small honest **source tag** on every borrowed picture.
4. **Speaker anchors:** the face only as full-frame beats: frame 0, one reaction or open-loop line, the CTA (in this plugin more often, §3.8).
5. **Sound (this edition):** a bright, curious SFX score on the graphic events (pointer pops, typed terms, measures, highlights, reveals); the references ran none; **no music bed, ever** (§11).
- Every rule is either **observed** (seen in the reels, the most consistent value chosen), a **fix** (a reel got it wrong, so this playbook forbids it), or **this edition's** elevation.
- **This style is not one layout.** One voice, one caption system and one lime pointer kit carry **three formats** (§3.0), and inside each the picture changes layout every ~1–3 s: B-roll, a dark board, a white document, a specimen on black, a CG scene, a 3D map.
- **The speaker is full frame or absent, never boxed.** In the references she is on screen 2.6–9.3% of the runtime (the first ~0.6 s, one reaction beat, the subscribe point). Every picture replaces her completely. No picture-in-picture, split, card or cut-out appears anywhere in the references. **This edition adds one adaptation layout (`L-9` card, §3.8):** the speaker shrinks into a wide rounded card for a data beat, then grows back to full frame, because styles move between layouts.
- **This plugin's users film themselves talking.** §3.8 (**ADAPTATION**) sets how much face a reel in this style keeps when the talking head is the main source, using the devices from these reels plus this edition's elevations.

**How to use this file:** follow §1 in order. Look up visuals by ID: formats `FMT-…`, layouts `L-…`, hook formulas `HF-…`, hook visuals `H-…`, structure devices `SD-…`, families `G-…`, patterns `P-…`, recipes `R-…`, transitions `T-…`, endings `E-…`, elevated devices `EV-…`. §2 is the craft that never bends; everything else is taste and grammar you adapt with a one-line reason.

## §1 Procedure (follow in order)

1. **Inventory.** Probe the footage. Identify the speaker setup (§12.1) and measure the face on 3 sampled frames (hair top, eye line, chin, face x, width): every speaker beat must show the whole face (M6).
2. **Transcribe** with word timestamps. Captions are the spoken words in caps, 1–5 words per phrase (§5.2). Check every term, place and name against the script; spell every caption and tag exactly (fixes: "BOUCES", "MIRCOSYSTEMS").
3. **Cut the voice to one line.** Remove every pause, breath, stumble and restart (gaps ≤ 0.25 s; fix: ca3 kept "sp- spread out"). A ≤ 150 ms inhale before a very long sentence may stay (ca2 9.53 s, ca3 2.17 s).
4. **Choose the format** (§3.0) with one line on why.
5. **Mark every showable word** with its onset: concrete nouns, comparisons, numbers, places, terms, mechanism steps, every deictic "this". Each gets its own picture (M2) and the pointer it needs (arrow, label, highlight, count-up).
6. **Plan the speaker map** (§3.1; §3.8 when the talking head is the main source): which beats are full-frame anchors, which are cut-aways.
7. **Choose the home base** (SD-2): the picture that returns 2–3 times and changes each time.
8. **Ideate 3 hooks** (§6), recommend one.
9. **Fill the beat sheet** (§13): one beat per showable word, a new picture or in-shot event every ≤ ~2.5 s.
10. **Plan the transition map** (§9): hard cuts leading the voice by 1–3 f; at most one flash and one graphic wipe; the button bridging cuts.
11. **The 10× pass and the score** (§8.0, §8.8, §11): for every picture, its hero detail, where the eye enters (EV-1), how its pointer anticipates, lands and follows through, which still gets parallax, where the scale pull-back and the look-inside lens go; then score every graphic event and log the sound ledger. **Attack the plan:** where is the creator gone for more than ~12 s? Where is a picture still, small or half empty? Where is a noun said with nothing on screen? Where is the biggest wonder moment, and is it the biggest thing in the reel? Fix it in the plan.
12. **Produce the assets** (§8.6): reusable B-roll (never film or TV clips), the real evidence with exact credits, depictions tagged `ARTISTIC DEPICTION`, bespoke boards and 3D scenes.
13. **Show the creator** the plan and stills (§13.3).
14. **Build, look at your frames (§15), score, render (§16).**

## §2 Craft that never bends
- **The whole face is always visible:** hair to chin inside the frame with ≥ 60 px above the hair on every speaker beat, at every zoom, crop or reframe; a too-tight selfie stays full screen as shot (eyes to chin in; the hair may touch the top edge), never shrunk over its own blurred frame. Captions may cross the face; graphics may not.
- **Text never sits on text:** no caption over a board label, a document's body text or a kinetic label.
- **Sound in sync:** every SFX on the frame its picture lands, never on a consonant (slide ±2 f); nothing ever louder than the voice.
- **True and exact:** every number, quote and credit real; every borrowed picture credited, every depiction tagged; every word spelled right.
- **Readable at phone size:** captions 64–72 px; labels ≥ 72 px caps; anything to be read ≥ 48 px; the hero of a picture ≥ 860 px wide.
- **Never pale:** footage and pictures keep their rich colour; no desaturating, fading or lifted blacks as a look.
- **Loudness:** −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP; a hard stop after the last word.

Everything else is direction. You decide; write your reasons into the plan.

### 2.1 The house grammar (what makes it this style)
Not checks: this is what the three reference reels do every time, and why it works. Break one only with a reason in the plan. (The IDs are kept because other sections cite them.)
- M1. **One continuous voice:** 3.4–3.8 words/s, gaps ≤ 0.25 s (max seen 0.28), at most a ≤ 150 ms inhale before a very long sentence (3/3). Never ducked or paused for a picture.
- M2. **Every showable word gets its own full-frame picture, on the word.** The cut lands **1–3 f before** the phrase's first word (ca1 1–3 f, ca2 0–3 f, ca3 0–2 f, once 4 f). One idea per shot. A comparison gets the picture that proves it (the person beside the life-size model; the animal standing up).
- M3. **The speaker is full frame or absent** (3/3), **or, in this edition, inside the `L-9` card** for a data beat (§3.8). Every illustration replaces her; she returns full frame. Beats: frame 0, one reaction/open-loop line, the CTA (§3.1; §3.8).
- M4. **Captions on every word** (`CAP-HOUSE`, §5.2), hard swaps, no box, no colour (3/3); one fixed band over pictures, just under the chin on the face (this edition), 64–72 px.
- M5. **One accent, lime `#C3FD00`,** for every pointer, label, highlight, "?" and the button. Other colours only where lime would vanish (§4.3) or for the machine's own signal (red, §4.4).
- M6. **The whole face is visible on every speaker beat:** hair to chin inside the frame with ≥ 60 px above the hair (fix: ca2's CTA frame let the hair reach y≈38), at every zoom, crop or reframe; never punched in, never cropped. How far any zoom goes is decided by the face, not the frame; a too-tight selfie stays full screen as shot (eyes to chin in; the hair may touch the top edge), never shrunk over its own blur, never a narrow pillar (§12.2). Hands and shoulders may leave the frame. **Captions may cross the face**; graphics may not (the button sits on the chest).
- M7. **Credit every borrowed picture** (`* SOURCE: …`) and tag every generated/CG one (`* ARTISTIC DEPICTION`), top-left (3/3). Fix: ca1's first clip and ca2's pull quote had none; tag everything borrowed unless its masthead is legible.
- M8. **Show the receipt, not the claim:** the real page, figure or quote, slammed in and zoomed out, with the lime highlighter on the exact spoken words (ca2).
- M9. **Numbers are animated:** a measuring arrow that grows, a count-up that lands on the comparison word, a pill on the number, a range on the size line-up (3/3).
- M10. **New terms are spelled on screen as they are said** (GEOGLYPHS, LiDAR, AQUIRY CIVILIZATION, 250 NANOMETERS).
- M11. **Something moves every ≤ ~2 s** (the references: ≤ ~2.5 s); no picture holds still > ~4 s; a held still or scan gets 2.5D parallax (EV-7). Long shots carry in-shot events (ca1's 7.3 s bat: push-in + 3 arrow sets; ca3's 9.8 s stadium: 5 overlay events).
- M12. **One home base returns and transforms** 2–3 times (3/3).
- M13. **Every loop is paid on screen** (the "freaky way" shown; "how can it see past light?" answered by the analogy and the callback).
- M14. **End on the creator's own call to action:** a tease of what's next, the button popping on the tease picture, a cut to the speaker pointing at it on the last word, hard stop (3/3). The button carries the creator's own CTA verb; never a third-party product.
- M15. **Audio:** −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP (measured −14.0 LUFS on all three; peaks −1.4 / −3.4 / −2.2 dBTP). The references carried no SFX; this edition scores every graphic event (§11), never a speaker beat.
- M16. **Spelling:** every caption, label and tag exact (fixes: "BOUCES BACK OFF THE LEAVES", "LEICA MIRCOSYSTEMS").

**Never in this style:**
- N1. A box, picture-in-picture, split or cut-out of the speaker (3/3), except the one `L-9` card (§3.8): never a small corner bubble, never a narrow pillar, never a cut-out over a picture.
- N2. Punch-ins, Ken Burns, crops or reframes on the speaker. All zoom energy lives in the pictures.
- N3. A caption over a board label or a document's body text: keep labels and text columns out of the band y 1460–1560 (fixes: ca1 f170–175 "WINGS ARE ESSENTIALLY" over the rising PTEROSAUR label; ca2 6.6–9.6 s caption over the article text).
- N4. A white caption carried only by its outline on a white frame (fix: ca3 23–25 s white-out): above ~85% luma the outline goes to 4 px with a soft dark shadow (still no box).
- N5. Film or TV clips (fix: ca1 used clips from a documentary series), generic stock clichés, or any picture whose source can't be named.
- N6. Sound that fights the voice: music of any kind, comedic or cartoon sounds, meme hits, crowd reactions, glitches, a whoosh on every plain cut, anything under a speaker beat, any cue louder than the voice. (The references ran no SFX at all; this edition scores the graphic events only, §11.)
- N7. More than one flash switch (T-02) or one graphic wipe (T-03) per reel; any dissolve, whip, glitch, leak or spin.
- N8. Coloured, popping or word-by-word captions.
- N9. A word without a caption, or a caption in the bottom ≈ 380 px UI band.
- N10. Invented numbers or quotes, fake screenshots, a depiction passed off as real.
- N11. Stumbles, restarts or dead air left in the voice.
- N12. A title card, banner or persistent headline. Text on screen is captions, labels, tags and the button only (3/3).

## §3 Formats, layouts, where the face is, safe zones

### 3.0 Choosing the format
| The script… | Format | Why |
|---|---|---|
| …makes a surprising claim about a filmable thing and explains one mechanism | `FMT-DOCU` | Clips prove each clause; a board explains the mechanism and returns |
| …reports a discovery or study with numbers and a source | `FMT-EVIDENCE` | Receipts carry trust; depictions fill what can't be filmed |
| …explains something invisible (too small, fast, abstract) | `FMT-ANALOGY` | One concrete analogy built step by step, then the real image again |
| …mixes them | The dominant one; borrow at most one device (e.g. a receipt inside `FMT-DOCU`) |

### 3.1 The speaker map (observed)
| Beat | When | Duration | Speaker | What she does |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Open** | f0 → the first showable word | 0.54–0.67 s (13–16 f) | **Full frame** | Mid-word, mid-gesture: hand thrust at the lens, raised eyebrows or a scrunched "look at this"; caption on |
| **Reaction / open loop** | ca1 4.96–6.58 s (8% in), ca2 22.42–24.54 s (61%) | 1.6–2.1 s | **Full frame** | The emotional line: "and did it in a really freaky way!", "So many more geoglyphs than we ever knew about!" |

| Everything else | — | — | **Absent** | The voice continues over full-frame pictures |

Share: ca1 4.9% (3 beats), ca2 9.3% (3), ca3 2.6% (2; no mid-reel beat).

### 3.2 `HOOK` (0–~5 s)
- **Frame 0 is the speaker full frame, mid-gesture,** caption on; no title, no black (3/3).
- **The first cut-away lands on the first showable word** at 0.54–0.67 s (the model on "was as big as", the globe on "hidden in the Amazon", the specimen on "between this").
- **3–5 visual changes by 3 s,** plus continuous motion inside the shots (a pan, a push-in, an arrow growing, a half wiping in).
- Then a return to the face for the open loop (ca1 4.96 s) or straight into the proof (ca2, ca3).

## §6 Hook system

### 6.0 The 10× hook
- **Frame 0 is never empty:** the creator mid-gesture, whole face, the first caption under the chin, already moving (a hand at the lens, eyebrows up).
- **The thing on screen by ~0.5 s,** on the first showable word, with its pointer landing 0–5 f after the word and its sound (a pop, a measure rising).
- **4–6 visual changes by 3 s**, each one a new noun or a change inside the picture; the eye lands where the last pointer pointed (EV-1), so the cuts feel like one move.
- **If the hook's claim is a number** (a size, a count), she may shrink into the side card (T-10) while it counts up beside her, then grow back for the loop line (T-11).
- **One wonder moment inside 5 s:** the scale pull-back (EV-2), the crisp half snapping in, the hidden thing lit by its outline. It gets the hook's one soft sub.
- **The loop is said by the face or shown as a lime "?"** that pops ≥ 72 px, and the body pays it on screen.

### 6.1 What the hook must do in about 3 seconds
1. **Stop the thumb with a face and a picture:** frame 0 is the speaker mid-gesture with the caption on; the first cut-away lands on the first showable word by ~0.6 s.
2. **Make a claim the viewer can see:** a size against things they know ("as big as a school bus… as much as a grizzly bear"), a hidden thing in a famous place, a before/after they can judge.
3. **Open a loop the body pays:** "and did it in a really freaky way" (paid 28.9–56 s), "no one knows for sure" (→ the next-video tease), "how can this see past light?" (paid by the analogy at 39.4 s).

### 7.2 The noun ritual (identical every time)
1. **Cut** to the picture 1–3 f before the phrase's first word; the caption swaps on the word.
2. **Tag** it on its first frame.
3. **Point** at the exact detail 0–5 f after the word starts: arrow, label, highlight, count-up.
4. **Keep it alive** while the phrase runs: the clip's motion, a slow push or pan, the pointer tracking.
5. **Hand off:** a hard cut to the next noun, with the next subject where the eye already is (EV-1), or the same picture changed (re-focus, second lens box, punch-in, the look-inside lens); never a still hold past ~4 s.
6. **Sound it:** the pointer's cue on its landing frame (§11); the plain cut itself stays dry.

- **The next video** (3/3): the tease line + the button.

## §8 Graphics system

### 8.0 The 10× layer (what the best motion designer adds to every picture)
**Animation principles, applied to this style:**
| Principle | How it shows up here |
|---|---|
| **Anticipation** | A lime dot appears 2 f before a write-on arrow starts; a pill's empty outline flashes 1 f before its letters; the picture pushes in 2–3% and dims its edges for 0.3–0.5 s before a reveal (EV-5) |
| **Follow-through & overlap** | Block arrows land at 1.12 and settle in 4 f; a leader line's label card trails its dot by 2 f; board rows overlap their rises (each starts before the last settles); the "?" marks pop with a 1.15 overshoot |
| **Squash & weight** | The button squashes 6% on the press and its shadow shrinks; a slammed receipt lands with a 1-frame compression; a pin drops, squashes, and its ring spreads |
| **Arcs** | Arrows glide along the bone on an arc, the lens travels on an arc, the camera's pull-backs and pushes ease on a gentle curve, never a straight robotic zoom |
| **Slow in / slow out** | Strong ease-out on arrivals (rise, slam, pill), ease-in-out on tracks and camera moves; typing and write-ons stay linear (they're "drawn") |
| **Staging** | One hero per frame: the detail under the lime pointer; when it lands, the rest of the picture dims ~15% and softens slightly so the detail reads first |
| **Secondary action** | Held clips push 2–4%; stills breathe in 2.5D parallax (EV-7); the board drifts up 1–2 px/f; lights blink, signal lines travel; the caption is the only thing that holds still |
| **Timing** | A new noun every 1.0–2.3 s; a wonder moment held 2–4 s so it can land; the face as long as the feeling |
| **Exaggeration** | One wonder moment per reel goes biggest: the scale pull-back (EV-2), the X-ray flash, the white-out, the crisp half snapping in |
| **Appeal** | Rich true colour, one electric lime, crisp white captions, honest tags; it looks like a science studio's best designer made it in an afternoon of delight |

**Design hierarchy (every frame):** 1. the exact detail under the lime pointer (or the creator's face on an anchor) · 2. the picture that holds it · 3. the caption · 4. the kinetic label or pill · 5. the source tag · 6. everything else, dimmed.

**Effects toolkit:** bloom on the X-ray switch and the white-out; a glint across every typed term; a soft lime underglow (≈ 20% lime, 12 px blur) under arrows and labels on busy pictures; 2.5D parallax on stills (EV-7); a gentle depth-of-field rack on held clips (the detail sharp, the rest −1 stop softer); a spotlight dim before reveals (EV-5); a few frames of motion blur on fast arrow tracks and the lens glide. Never: dissolves, whips, spins, glitches, light leaks, grain, a vignette on the speaker, colour grades that make anything pale.

### 8.2 Staging choreography
1. **Lead (−1 to −3 f):** the cut.
2. **Land (0–5 f after the word):** the pointer, label, highlight or count starts (pills may pop ~5 f early, on the number's phrase).

4. **Hand-off:** a hard cut to the next noun, or a change inside the same picture.
Change the picture between nouns; change *inside* it while the same thing is still being explained.

### 8.5 Density and variety
- A new picture every **1.0–2.3 s** (medians ca2 1.0, ca1 2.2, ca3 2.3 s); means 1.6–3.0 s.
- A shot longer than ~3 s carries an in-shot event every ≤ ~2.5 s; longest seen 7.3 s, 4.75 s (the title animation), 9.8 s.
- **≥ 4 families per 60 s** (ca1 used 6, ca2 8, ca3 9). The home base returns 2–3 times; at most one recap montage; a pointer every ~3–6 s, never two on the same detail.
- **Scale:** the hero of a picture ≥ 860 px wide; a group fills x ≈ 90–990; no mostly-empty frame for more than ~0.7 s; anything named aloud and written ≥ 48 px.
- **Elevated devices** (§8.8): EV-1 and EV-7 everywhere; EV-2 once or twice; EV-3 at most twice; EV-5 at most twice (the hook's wonder moment and the payoff).

### 8.6 Asset rules
- **Reusable sources only:** footage the creator owns or has a licence for, openly licensed or public-domain material, open-access figures, press-kit material. **Never film or TV clips** (fix for ca1). If the source can't be named on the tag, don't use it.
- **Depictions** for what can't be filmed (the past, the invisible, a method in action), always `* ARTISTIC DEPICTION`, never passed off as real (ca2 tagged every one).
- **Evidence is captured, not retyped:** a real screenshot of the real page or figure; recreate only if it can't be captured, with the exact text and the tag.
- **Bespoke boards and 3D scenes** are drawn: cut-out objects with no baked-in text, live labels.
- **Crop landscape clips to 9:16 on the subject;** never letterbox.
- **What the references used:** a life-size model with a person beside it; documentary animal clips; a museum skeleton; an eagle take-off; bat lab footage (ca1). Scans from a science magazine and a journal, the journal's article page, the paper's study map, a pull quote, CG of a plane, a forest, a campfire and a village (ca2). A journal figure, a super-resolution image, a stock objective, an algae micrograph, a CG stadium and cells, a vendor before/after, an institute image, a CG miniature (ca3).

### 10.1 Motion tokens (24 fps)
| Token | Value |

| Specimen | Shrink 100 → ~85% in 6 f; half wipe 4 f |
| 3D scenes | Ghost fade 30 → 100% in ~6 f; pin ring ~5 f; continuous push ~1.6× / 28 f, pull-back ~15% / 2 s |
| Button | Pop 1 f · hold ~19 f · cursor 5 f · press 3 f · fade 20 f |

| Parallax (EV-7) | Layers ≈ 1.5% vs 0.5% over the hold, plus a 2–4% push |

### 10.2 Camera
- **Speaker:** handheld selfie; its own sway is the only movement; no punch-in, Ken Burns or reframe (N2); the whole face in frame (M6).
- **Pictures:** all zoom energy lives here: pans across the model, push-ins on held clips, the globe push, the line-up pull-back, the miniature dolly, slam zoom-outs, punch-ins on images, the scale pull-back (EV-2), the push-to-line (EV-6). Boards drift slowly upward. Pans and pushes hand off across cuts (EV-1).

### 10.3 Layer order (back to front)
1. The picture (clip, board, document, specimen, CG, 3D) or the speaker
2. Picture-attached graphics (rows, lens boxes, 3D labels, signal lines, pins, outline)
3. Pointers (arrows, measures, "?"; the highlighter sits **behind** the document's text)
4. Kinetic labels and pills
5. Source tag
6. Button + cursor
7. Caption (always on top)
8. Light passes (T-02 ramp, T-07 bloom) over everything except the caption

### 10.4 Finishing
No grain, no vignette on the speaker, no unifying grade, nothing pale. Glow where the references have it (X-ray bloom, leading-letter bloom and glint, neon outline, lasers) plus this edition's toolkit (§8.0): a lime underglow on busy pictures, the spotlight dim of EV-5, a soft depth rack on held clips. Labels and arrows get a thin dark outline or shadow so they read on any picture. Every word spelled right (M16).

### 11.2 The SFX ledger (Studio Standard Rule, unchanged)
- **Any file at most 2× in a reel,** except the one **list cue** (here `selection-click` for each item in a run).
- **No file on two consecutive cues;** rotate within each pool.
- **Density:** about one cue every 1.0–1.6 s **inside visual blocks**, each tied to a graphic event on screen; plain cuts and speaker beats get nothing (one soft crossing at most).
- **Never more than two cues overlapping;** never on a trigger word's consonant (shift ±2 f).
- Pops, keys, ticks and markers are texture; the slam, the flash, the pull-back and the wonder reveal are what the viewer feels.

### 11.3 Silence and the mix
- **Go silent (no new SFX) for 0.3–0.5 s before each wonder reveal and the payoff** (EV-5), then land the reveal cue.
- The voice: high-pass 80 Hz, de-ess, firm compression (LRA ≈ 2 LU), every breath cut.
- SFX sit under the voice (ducked ≥ 6 dB while it speaks); the loudest cue never above the speech median.
- Master: **−14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP**. The voice is always the loudest thing.

- **Audio:** one clean voice track that runs under every cut-away; pauses, breaths, stumbles cut (M1, N11); the chain in §11.

### 12.4 Picture handling
- **Landscape → 9:16:** crop on the subject; a slow pan may travel across a wide subject (ca1 f16–23). Never letterbox.
- **Documents:** captured at high resolution; the key line in the upper half, the text column ending above y≈1440.

- **Held clips:** one clip per phrase (~1–3 s); longer only with a push-in and pointer events (M11).
- **Re-use is a device** when the story returns (ca1's skeleton at 14.5 and 40.9 s; ca2's recap).

## §15 Look at your frames
Not a checklist; the questions the best editor asks while scrubbing:
- **The footage frame:** is every video, recording and picture full screen or in a designed box it fills, never a sharp rectangle over its own blur, never padded with blurred bars?
- **Frame 0, sound off:** is the creator there mid-gesture, whole face, the caption under the chin? Is the thing on screen by ~0.5 s?
- **The hook:** 4–6 changes by 3 s? Is there a wonder moment that makes me go "wait, what?", and does the loop get paid on screen?
- **Every noun:** is it on screen on its word, with the pointer on the exact detail? Does my eye land where the last pointer was, or do I have to search?
- **Scale:** shrink the still to thumbnail size. Is the hero ≥ 860 px and the first thing I see, or is the frame half empty? Can I read every caption, label and tag on a phone?
- **Motion:** is anything still for more than ~2 s? Do the arrows and labels land with weight and settle, or just appear?
- **The creator:** back every 6–12 s, whole face, never a pillar, rich colour, caption under the chin? Is any visual block longer than it earns?
- **The layouts:** does she move (full frame → card for the data → full frame on the next claim), or is the reel frozen in one layout? Is the card wide (≥ 940 hero / ≥ 520 side), her face whole, the shrink eased and settling?
- **Honesty:** every borrowed picture tagged, every depiction marked, every number and quote real and spelled right?
- **Sound:** a cue on each pointer, label and reveal, silence before the reveal, nothing under the face, no file overused, no music?
- **Vibe:** curious, fast, delighted, honest. Anything that feels like hype or a stock montage comes out.
