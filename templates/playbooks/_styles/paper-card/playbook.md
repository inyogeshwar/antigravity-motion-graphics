# Paper Proof Explainer Playbook (v2, "Show It on Paper, Elevated" edition, for Claude Opus 5.5)

> **In this plugin:** this file is the whole playbook: every measured value and recipe from the 3 reference reels is in it, and there is no other playbook file to open. Frame counts are measured at each reel's own rate: **pv1 at 23.976 fps, pv2 at 30 fps, pv3 at 25 fps** (the reel is named beside every number; a "→ 30 fps" value is given where it matters). Convert with t = f / fps when you draw; `frame(t)` runs at the render fps (30). Positions are on 1080×1920 (the sources are 720×1280, scaled ×1.5). The per-reel notes this file cites (`pv1…pv3`) are not shipped. Sounds are picked by id from SOUNDS.md; commands are in the edit skill's REFERENCE.md. The page gives you `FACE(n)` and `FACE0` (with `head_top` and `chin`) for every speaker frame.

**Purpose:** you (Claude) receive a creator's video plus its script. Use this document to **ideate and build** every format choice, card, caption, diagram, typographic beat, move and sound, then edit the Reel so it looks and sounds like a calm, design-literate "creator school" explainer: **warm paper, one real thing in a frame, one short phrase above it, and the structure made visible as type**, built with such taste that the viewer keeps watching for how it looks and stays for what it teaches.

**What v2 is:** a frame-by-frame analysis of 3 finished reels by peter.visuals, a faceless voice-over page that teaches short-form content formats: **pv1** "Image-to-video style content" (23.8 s), **pv2** "6 ways to hook viewers in the first 3 seconds" (33.3 s), **pv3** "Storytelling isn't talent: a four-letter formula" (18.0 s). The analysis covered cut lists, layout and colour measurements, caption timing, motion recipes and audio (SFX, loudness). Then **elevated ten times** by the best video editor and creative director in the world: paper that feels like real stock under a desk lamp, cards that land with weight and glide from one place to the next instead of popping, a pen that draws itself and marks the proof, the creator stepping into the card and out to full frame, a hub that breathes and fills as a progress ring, letters with depth that fly home for the recap, serif words that ink in, readable type everywhere, a face that is never pale, and a calm editorial sound score (paper, pen, soft air, a few deep weights). Every rule below is either **observed** (seen in the reels, the most consistent value chosen as the house standard), a **fix** (something a reel got wrong), or **this edition** (the elevation). All measured layouts, recipes and rules are kept. Where a reference reel and a direction disagree, **the directions win**.

**Measured sizes are floors, not targets.** The references drew their cards 441–588 px wide, captions drifting 52–84 px and connector words at 25–30 px. On a phone that reads as small, quiet and empty. Here:
- **Minimal means few, not small.** One real thing per screen, and that thing is **big**: a thing card is **≥ 640 px wide** (phone ratio 640×1138, x 220–860, y 362–1500); a screenshot, profile or table card **≥ 900 px wide**; the face card **≥ 880 px wide** (≥ 940 px for a tight selfie); the resource card **≥ 900 px wide**.
- **Type:** `CAP-PHRASE` **84 px** (one band per reel, floor 76); display serif ≥ 110 px; labels ≥ 56 px; kinetic connector words ≥ 52 px; nothing a viewer is meant to read under 52 px.
- **No near-empty paper.** If more than half of y 200–1500 is bare ground for more than ~0.7 s, the thing is too small or the next element is late.
- **The selfie stays big:** a close selfie stays full screen (the hair may touch the top edge) or goes into the wide face card, which it fills completely; the face card stays ≥ 940 px wide, never a narrow pillar, never shrunk over its own blur.

**Never pale.** The paper is a background colour, never a look on the person. The creator's footage keeps its own rich colour (or a touch richer): no desaturation, no fade, no lifted or milky blacks, no paper-coloured wash over the face. Skin warm, blacks true.

**Full screen or a box, never any footage or picture over its own blur** (Naman, 13 Oct): the creator's video either fills the whole frame edge to edge, or sits in a designed box (rounded corners, a rim or soft shadow, on this style's own ground) that it fills completely, cropped around the face. The same for every other medium: screen recordings, landscape clips, photos, receipts, diagrams and other people's footage either fill the frame (cover-cropped to the part that matters, or a push-in) or sit in a designed box on the style's own ground or scene (its colour, gradient, texture, grid or drawn set). Never shrink anything over a blurred or darkened copy of itself, never pad a band or box with blurred side bars, never set a card on the creator's blurred frame: a hard rectangle against its own blur looks cheap. A tight selfie stays full screen: move or shrink the text above the head, not the video; the hair may touch the top edge, eyes to chin are always in. When the whole head must be seen, use a box layout where it fits.

**How to use this file:**
- Follow §1 (procedure) in order.
- Look up visuals by ID: formats `FMT-…`, layouts `L-…`, adaptation states `S-…`, hook formulas `HF-…`, hook visuals `H-…`, structure devices `SD-…`, graphic families `G-…`, patterns `P-…`, elevated devices `EV-…`, transitions `T-…`.
- §2 is the only craft that never bends; the house grammar (§2.1) is what makes this style itself, and everything else is direction you can adapt with a one-line reason.

- **This style is not one layout.** One voice, one palette temperament and one caption attitude carry **three formats** (§3): the paper card explainer, the hub map with full-screen proof, and the letter panels. Pick the format from the script.
- **The shown thing is never locked to one rect.** pv1's card changes size and place every scene; pv2's proof takes the full frame. The face, when there is one, moves the same way (§3.0a).
- **The reference creator is faceless and shows other creators' reels.** Both facts change how this style is used in this plugin. §3.8 is a clearly marked **ADAPTATION**: how the same grammar carries one person talking to their own camera. And every slot that held another creator's reel, face, profile or product now holds **the user's own** clip, screen, image or resource (N1).

**Who the reference creator is:** a faceless creator-education page. A dry, fast, US-neutral male voice (3.3–3.7 words/s, breaths cut, no filler) teaches content formats, hooks and storytelling frameworks. He never appears. The authority comes from **showing the actual thing** the instant it is named, and from editorial typography that looks like a magazine spread rather than a template.

**Audience (ICP):** aspiring and early short-form creators and faceless-page builders, English-speaking, who want a format they can copy today. They want to *see* the format, *name* it, and get *one rule* they can repeat, in under 30 seconds. They trust real examples with real view counts more than any claim.

**Style lineage:** an editorial "paper desk" explainer, built from three layers:
1. **Ground layer:** a flat ground that changes colour by section: warm paper `#E1DFDB` with a faint square grid (pv1, end cards), warm dark brown `#3C2E2D` (pv1's claim section), pure black `#000000` (pv1's punch line), cool grey `#EAEAEA` with a dot grid (pv2, pv3 hook and recap), near-black `#141414` with a dot grid (pv3 body). **A ground change is a section change.**
2. **Proof layer:** one real thing in a phone-ratio frame (a reel, a profile, a table, a resource), centred, square-cornered or barely rounded, never decorated, drifting slowly so a 5 s hold still breathes. In pv2 the proof takes the full frame with its own sound.
3. **Type layer:** a tight two-family contrast: a **bold geometric sans** for the spoken phrase (lower-case, 1–3 words, hard-swapped on the word) and a **tall condensed serif** for display words (word stacks, the punch line, the equation); plus a neo-grotesk for diagram labels and a condensed caps face for giant letters. No accent colour anywhere: the colour comes from the proof.

### The feel (read this twice)
This is **a design editor laying one real thing at a time on a warm desk under a lamp**, and talking you through it. The card lands on the paper with a soft weight and a whisper of air; a pen draws an arrow to it; a short phrase sits above it in bold lower-case, swapping on every word; a serif word inks in like a magazine headline. When the section turns, the desk turns: the paper gives way to brown for the claim, to black for the one-line verdict. When the creator talks to you, the card opens into their face, full frame, warm and whole, then folds back into a card when the next thing is shown. It feels **calm, smart and premium**: curated, never decorated; editorial, never a template. Nothing shouts, nothing is still. If it looks like a slideshow of screenshots, it's wrong; if it looks like a hype reel, it's wrong too.

## §1 Procedure (follow in order)

1. **Inventory.** Probe the footage (resolution, fps, duration, audio). Identify the setup (§12.1). **Compare audio and video durations** (§12.4): two references drifted 1.2–2.1 s; if they differ by > 0.1 s, conform first.
2. **Transcribe** with word-level timestamps. English speech is captioned as spoken (lower-case, the speaker's words). Check every name and number against the script.
3. **Find the things.** List every noun the viewer must *see* (a format, tool, screen, result, profile, number, count) with its word onset; each appears within ~0.3 s of its word (M3). Mark which the user can supply as their own clip, screen or image (§8.6); never another creator's (N1).
4. **Choose the format** (§3.0) from the shape of the script, one line why:
   - one format, idea or tool explained by showing it → `FMT-CARD`;
   - a list of N ways / types / mistakes with something provable for each → `FMT-HUB`;
   - a framework with a name, an acronym or an N-step formula → `FMT-LETTERS`.
   With the user's own talking-head footage (the normal case), the reel is built as **`FMT-SELF`** (§3.8) on top of the chosen grammar.
5. **Plan the face's movement** (§3.0a, §3.8.3): mark every beat `S-FULL`, `S-CARD`, `S-THING`, `S-TYPE` or `S-END`. Frame 0 is the user full frame (or the real thing, §6.1); the first shrink to the card on the first thing shown; type beats faceless and short; back full frame for the turn or opinion; the end card last.
6. **Segment** the script into: `HOOK` (0–2.5/3.6 s), `SETUP` (the claim and its proof), body `ITEM`s / `LETTER`s / `HOW` + `WHY`, the `PUNCH` (the one-line payoff, "It's that simple."), `RECAP` (the name again), `CTA`.
7. **Classify every clause** with a line type from §8.4. Mark its **trigger word** and its **caption chunks** (1–3 words each, as spoken).
8. **Assign the grounds** (§4.2, SD-1): one ground per section, a change only at a section boundary. Write the ground sequence into the plan (e.g. paper → brown → paper → black → paper → paper-CTA).
9. **Ideate 3 hook variants** (§6), each with a formula, the frame-0 picture, the first 3 s of events (≥ 8 visual changes, §6.0) and the first captions. Recommend one.
10. **Fill the beat sheet** (§13.1): one beat per caption chunk or element entrance. Each beat gets the layout, the state (adaptation), the ground, the element and its rect, the caption, the entrance and the trigger frame.
11. **Plan transitions** (§9.3): a hard cut and a slide or rise on every ground change; a card relay (T-13) between cards on one ground; the card opening into the face and folding back (T-14 / T-15); the hub's smash zoom once and a spin per return (FMT-HUB); letter assembly (FMT-LETTERS). Check the budget (§9.4).
12. **The 10× pass and the score** (§8.0, §11): for every beat, its one hero and its size (≥ the §5.0 / header floors), how it anticipates, lands, drifts and leaves; where the pen marks the proof (EV-3, EV-5); how the card relays; then score every event and log the sound ledger. **Attack the plan:** where is a frame mostly bare paper? Where is a card small? Where is the creator gone for more than ~10 s? Where does a card pop instead of glide? Where is a word not on screen? Fix it in the plan.
13. **Produce the assets** (§8.6): the user's own clips, screens, profile, posts and resource; all type drawn live. List anything missing.
14. **Show the creator** the plan and stills (§13.3), then **build section by section,** look at the landing frames (§15), score, render (§16).

## §2 Craft that never bends
- **The whole face is always visible:** hair to chin at every size, card, crop and panel; a close selfie stays full screen (the hair may touch the top edge, eyes to chin always in) or fills the wide face card on the paper, never shrunk over its own blurred frame. Captions may cross the face.
- **Never pale:** the creator's footage keeps its own rich colour; no desaturation, fade, lifted blacks or paper wash over a person.
- **Text never sits on text:** no caption on a card or over a thing that already carries text (`S-FULL` captions may cross the face).
- **Sound in sync:** every SFX on the frame its picture lands, never on a consonant (slide ±2 f); nothing louder than the voice.
- **True and exact:** every caption matches the spoken word; every card, count, profile and resource is the user's own and real.
- **Readable at phone size:** every floor in §5.0; the hero thing at the header's sizes.
- **Loudness:** −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1.0 dBTP; end on a complete sentence and a hard stop.

Everything else is direction. You decide; write your reasons into the plan.

### 2.1 The house grammar (what makes it this style)
Not checks: this is what the three reference reels do every time, and why it works. Break one only with a reason in the plan. (The IDs are kept because other sections cite them.)

- M1. **One format per reel**, chosen from the script with a written reason (§3.0). The paper end card (`L-7`, §3.7) is shared by every format. In the adaptation the user's face moves between states (§3.8.3) with eased opens and folds (EV-4); it is never locked to one rect from start to end.
- M2. **Frame 0 is never empty.** The real thing (or the user, §3.8) is on at full size on f0, the first word by **f1–f2** (pv1: card f0, arrow + "This" f1; pv3: cloud moving f0, "Storytelling" f2). **Fix:** pv2 opened on a ~20 px dot and grew the map over f0–20 (§6.1).
- M3. **Show the thing named, on its word.** The card, profile, clip or resource is fully on within **~0.3 s** of the word that names it (pv1), and every named item is proven **≤ 0.2 s** after its label finishes (pv2). The incoming element starts its entrance on the cut frame.
- M4. **Scenes change on clause starts.** Each new scene starts on the first word of a new clause ("one…", "of views…", "Just…", "It's…", "This format…", "More loops…"; pv1), or a beat before a section word so the letter is on as the word is said (pv3). A ground change is a hard cut with the incoming element's own entrance; **on one ground the card relays** (T-13: it glides to its next rect, the content swapping on the word). The motion lives in the elements (§9), never in a transition effect.
- M5. **A ground change is a section change** (SD-1). One ground per section; switch only at a boundary. The punch line gets black (pv1); the body of a framework gets near-black (pv3); the recap inverts the body's ground (pv3); the end card is always paper.
- M6. **Phrase captions are 1–3 words, lower-case as spoken, hard-swapped on the word** (0-frame swap, no pop, no highlight, no stroke, no colour; pv1, pv3 CTA). They sit **above** the card, never on it (pv1); on a full-frame face they sit **just under the chin**. **Every spoken word is on screen** (this edition): phrase captions while the voice runs, the type beat's own words on type beats.
- M7. **One caption size band per reel.** pv1's caption glyph height drifted 52–84 px phrase to phrase; **fix:** set one size for `CAP-PHRASE` per reel (**84 px** default, floor 76, §5.2) and keep it.
- M8. **Readable at phone size.** Phrase captions ≥ 76 px font (house 84); labels ≥ 56 px; kinetic connector words ≥ 52 px; end-card lines ≥ 64 px (§5.0). **Fix:** pv3's connector words (~25–30 px) and "Top Creators use" read as texture, not text; pv1's end-card lines (~46 px glyph) were borderline.
- M9. **Something is alive at every frame.** Cards keep drifting after they land (pv1); captions swap every 0.25–0.6 s; a hold > 3 s needs both (pv1's 5.1 s hold lived only this way).
- M10. **The whole face is visible, hair to chin, at every size, crop, card, circle or panel**; the face decides how far a crop goes. Footage tighter than the face allows (a tight handheld selfie whose hair and chin leave the frame) stays **full screen as shot, or fills the wide face card on the paper** (never shrunk inside its frame or card over a blurred copy of itself); eyes to chin always in (§12.3). Captions may cross the face. **Fix:** pv1's hook card cut its subject's head at the card's top edge.
- M11. **Counts and names are kept.** The hub has exactly as many filled spokes as items said (pv2: 6 bars = 6 ways); a framework's name is spelled on screen when first said **and again at the end** (pv3 "It's called HRST" / "That's HRST").
- M12. **The CTA is spoken and shown** (pv3), the keyword as its own caption beat, over the user's own resource on the paper ground; hard stop on the last word (§6.7). **Fix:** pv1's CTA was text-only on a 2.1 s silent card; pv2 had no CTA at all.
- M13. **Audio:** −14 LUFS integrated, **true peak ≤ −1.0 dBTP**. All three references peaked above 0 dBFS (+0.1, +0.5, +0.3): never again. Proof clips with their own sound are normalised to the voice (pv2's clips ran 5–8 dB hotter than the narrator).
- M14. **Spelling is exact.** Every caption matches the spoken word. **Fix:** pv3's T panel showed "End" for the spoken "And" (f285).
- M15. **Real material only.** Cards, profiles, clips, numbers and resources are the user's own and real. A view count, a follower count, a table row is only shown if it is true.
- M16. **Never pale** (this edition): the creator's footage keeps its own rich colour or a touch richer (contrast +5–10%, true blacks, warm skin). The grounds are backgrounds; they never tint a person.
- M17. **Sizes are floors** (this edition): the thing card ≥ 640 px wide, a screenshot/profile/table card ≥ 900, the face card ≥ 880 (≥ 940 for a tight selfie), the resource card ≥ 900; no mostly-bare ground for more than ~0.7 s.
- M18. **Nothing is ever still** (this edition, from pv1's drift): every card drifts, the hub breathes, the letter's planes slide, the grid parallaxes a few px behind the card; a hold > 3 s also needs a new caption or a pen mark.

**Never in this style:**
- N1. **Another creator's reel, face, profile, post or product as the shown thing.** The references are built from third-party reels (cards, profiles, six proof clips, a thumbnail cloud) and a resource the creator sells; here every slot holds **the user's own** material (§3.8.5). No asset → the §8.4 fallback; never borrow.
- N2. Flashes, whips, dissolves, light leaks, glitches, film burns, zoom-blur effects on cuts. None in the 3 reels (all 30 scene changes are hard cuts). The eased card relay, open and fold (T-13…T-15) are element moves, not effects.
- N3. Accent colours, coloured words, gradients or glows in captions; **pure black text on the paper ground** (text on paper is warm near-black `#3B2F2D`).
- N4. A caption on the card, or a caption over a thing that already carries text. On `S-FULL` face beats captions may cross the face (§3.8).
- N5. A head cut by a card edge, the frame edge or a zoom (M10).
- N6. **A near-empty frame on any cut.** **Fixes:** pv2 f0 (a 20 px dot); pv1 19.02 s (the equation scene's first frames are bare paper before "More loops" slides in); pv3 3.64 s (a dark frame with only a ~35 px "It's" for ~0.6 s before the letters arrive) and 12.52 s (the recap: a lone "That's"). The scene's first element lands at readable size **on the cut frame** (§9.2).
- N7. Text under the phone floor (M8), even as "texture".
- N8. Reusing the hook's visual as a later beat's visual (pv1 showed the same example card in the hook and again in the how-to). Evolve it or show a new thing.
- N9. Comedic, cartoon or novelty sounds, game blips, cash registers, risers under the voice, or any SFX under the creator talking to camera; SFX on caption swaps (captions are silent; the pictures carry the sound, §11).
- N10. Ending with no CTA, a silent unspoken end card, or a black tail.
- N11. A music bed of any kind (SFX only), or a level jump between the voice and a proof clip.

## §3 Formats, layouts, where the face is, safe zones

### 3.0 Choosing the format
| The script… | Format | Why |
|---|---|---|
| …explains one format, tool, technique or idea by showing it, with a "how" and a "why" | `FMT-CARD` | One framed thing per sentence is the whole argument; the paper makes it feel curated |
| …lists N ways / types / mistakes / examples, each provable | `FMT-HUB` | The map shows the count and progress; the proof gets the full frame |
| …teaches a framework with a name, an acronym or an N-letter formula | `FMT-LETTERS` | The structure becomes the layout; each section is legible in one glance |
| …is a pure opinion with nothing to show | `FMT-CARD` with the user's face as the card (§3.8) and type beats (`P-08`, `P-09`) for the payoff | The style still frames something on every sentence |
| …mixes them | the dominant one; borrow at most **one** device from another (e.g. a black punch card inside `FMT-HUB`) | The references never mixed formats; one borrow keeps the identity |

### 4.2 The meaning of a ground change
- **Paper** = "here is the thing" (the default explaining ground; the end card).
- **Brown** = the claim / the promise section (pv1: "one of the easiest types of content… millions of views").
- **Black** = the punch line, one sentence, ≤ 1 s (pv1 "It's that simple.").
- **Grey dot grid** = the map / the hook / the recap (pv2, pv3).
- **Night dot grid** = the body of a framework (pv3).
- **One accent colour: none.** All saturated colour comes from the shown things (cards, clips, thumbnails, the resource's tags). The monochrome grounds make each colourful proof pop (pv2).

### 4.3 Rules
Text on paper/grey is `ink`/`titleInk`, on dark grounds `cream`/`white`; never coloured, highlighted or gradient (N3). A ground holds for its whole section (M5) and never tints the footage.

### 4.4 Footage and screen look (never pale)
- Cards keep their source grade (pv1: warm studio, black tweet cards; pv2: every clip's own colour). Don't regrade a user clip to match the paper.
- In the adaptation, normalise exposure between the user's setups; keep skin as shot or a touch richer (contrast +5–10%, true blacks, warm skin); no LUT, no vignette.
- **Never pale** (this edition): no desaturation, fade, lifted or milky blacks, or paper-coloured wash on the creator's footage, the face card or the full-frame face. The monochrome grounds make the colourful proof and the warm face pop; they never drain them.
- No blurred-self fill anywhere: the footage is full screen or fills its card on the paper (Naman, 13 Oct).

## §6 Hook system

### 6.0 The 10× hook
- **Frame 0, three layers deep:** the warm paper with its lamp falloff (or the creator full frame, mid-word, in rich colour), **the real thing big** (a card ≥ 640 px wide, or the face), and the first word on by f1–f2. Never a bare ground, never a dot.
- **By ~0.4–0.6 s the pen draws** its arrow to the thing (EV-3) with a marker stroke; **by ~1.5 s** the name lands in the serif/sans stack, the key word inking in (EV-8); **by 2.3–3.6 s** the first section move (a ground change with a slide, or a card relay). **≥ 8 visual changes in the first 3 s.**
- **The open loop is drawn, not just said:** the hub's six redaction bars, four empty letter slots under "4-LETTER FORMULA", or the thing shown before it's named (SD-8).
- **Sound:** one soft sub thump on f2–3 under the first motion, the pen stroke, and nothing else; the voice owns the hook.
- **Attack it:** sound off, at thumbnail size, is the first frame a beautiful page with one big real thing and a word on it? If it reads as a grey square with a small picture, make the thing bigger.

### 6.1 What the hook must do in about 3 seconds
1. **Stop the thumb with the real thing on f0** (M2): the example in its frame (pv1), a wall of real work in motion (pv3), or in the adaptation the user's face full frame mid-word. **Fix for pv2's opening:** the hub is at **full overview size on f0** with "6" already on; the title builds on top; or the reel opens on `S-FULL` with the promise and cuts to the hub on its first spoke.
2. **Name the format, the count, or the myth** within 1.5–3.6 s (pv1 "This is the image to video style content", pv2 "6 ways…", pv3 "Storytelling isn't talent").
3. **Open a loop** the body pays off: the format seen before it is named (pv1), six redacted bars (pv2), "a 4-letter formula" withheld until "It's called HRST" (pv3).
- **≥ 7 visual changes in the first 3 s** (measured 7, ~12, ~8); this edition aims for ≥ 8 (§6.0).

## §8 Graphics system

### 8.0 The 10× layer (what the best motion designer adds to every card)
**Animation principles, applied to this style:**
| Principle | How it shows up here |
|---|---|
| **Anticipation** | The card's contact shadow appears 2 f before the card lands; the pen's nib touches down (a 2 px dot) 2 f before the stroke; the hub dips 1% before the spin |
| **Follow-through & overlap** | A rising card overshoots its rect by 6–8 px and settles in 4 f; the caption swaps 2 f after the card lands, never the same frame; in a stack, each line starts before the last has settled |
| **Squash & weight** | Cards land with weight: the last 3 f of the entrance compress the motion (a soft stop, not a float); "simple." lands 1.08 → 1.00 |
| **Arcs** | The card relay (T-13) travels on a slight arc, never a straight slide; the arrow curves; recap letters fly home on arcs |
| **Slow in / slow out** | Ease-out on every entrance (`bezier(0.22,1,0.36,1)`), ease-in-out on relays, opens and folds; caption swaps are hard (0 f) |
| **Staging** | One hero per frame: when the proof spotlight pushes in (EV-5), the rest of the card dims 25% and the caption names the detail |
| **Secondary action** | Every card drifts (pv1's ~116 px then ~22 px); the grid parallaxes 2–4 px behind it; the hub breathes; the letter panel's two planes slide |
| **Timing** | Calm: holds 0.6–5.1 s (pv1), type beats 0.75–2.6 s, the face as long as the thought; never two state changes within 0.6 s |
| **Exaggeration** | One moment per reel goes bigger: the black punch's HUGE word, the hub's final pull-back with every label lit, or the recap letters flying home |
| **Appeal** | Warm paper, one ink, one real thing, a tall serif and a bold sans; it looks like a design magazine laid out by hand |

**Design hierarchy (every frame):** 1. the real thing, or the face · 2. the phrase caption · 3. the display word (serif) · 4. the pen marks · 5. the ground, grid and everything else.

**Effects toolkit (paper only, all subtle):** paper fibre (2–3% luminance noise, static), the grid printed into the stock, a warm lamp falloff (the top-left 4% lighter, the corners 4–6% darker), a soft contact shadow under cards on paper (2 px y, 10 px blur, 16% `ink`; on brown 30% black), the entrance motion blur (as observed), a 1 px ink settle on serif words, pen pressure on strokes. Never: glows, lens flares, light leaks, glitches, shake, RGB splits, film burns, coloured highlights, a pale or tinted face.

### 8.2 Staging choreography
1. **Cut** on the clause's first word; the ground and the element's starting frame are on the cut frame (N6). On the same ground, **relay** instead (T-13): the card glides to its next rect and swaps its content on the word.
2. **Land** in 6–9 f (pv1) with motion blur, ease-out (soft land), a 6–8 px overshoot and a 4 f settle; the contact shadow lands 2 f first.
3. **Drift** for the rest of the hold: never fully still (pv1 card y 756 → 640 → 618 over f205 → 230 → 300: ~116 px in the first 25 f, then ~22 px over 70 f).
4. **Captions** swap on every 1–3 words above it.

- **Any file at most 2× in a reel,** except the one **list cue** (here `paper-turn-page` for each new item, letter or spoke).
- **No file on two consecutive cues;** rotate within each pool.
- **Density:** about **one cue every 1.5–2.2 s on shown beats** (cards, type, map, letters), each tied to a picture; plain face beats get nothing (one soft crossing at most).
- **Never more than two cues overlapping;** never on a trigger word's consonant (shift ±2 f).
- Air, pen and paper are texture; the ground changes, the three weights and the landing are what the viewer feels.

### 11.3 Silence and the mix
- **Go silent (no new SFX) for 0.4–0.6 s before the punch ("It's that simple.") and before the payoff** (the equation's last line, the hub's full ring, the recap), then land the weight.
- **End:** the sound stops with the hard end (pv3); no voiceless tail (N10).
- **Mix:** high-pass 80 Hz, light compression, two-pass loudnorm to −14 LUFS, **true peak ≤ −1.0 dBTP** (all three references peaked +0.1 to +0.5 dBFS). The voice is always the loudest thing; proof clips are normalised to it (M13).

### 12.3 The face rule (every state)
**The whole face is visible, hair to chin, at every size, crop, card, circle or panel.** How far a crop goes is decided by the face, not the frame. If the footage is tighter than the face allows (the failing case: a tight handheld selfie whose hair and chin leave the frame), **keep it full screen as shot, or put it in the wide face card on the paper, which it fills** (a taller card if the head needs it); never shrink it inside its frame or card over a blurred copy of itself; eyes to chin always in; the card stays wide (≥ 940 px), never a pillar. Captions may cross the face. The footage keeps its rich colour (never pale). No other checks.

### 12.4 Clock drift (check first)
pv1 and pv3 were exports whose audio decoded shorter than the picture (pv1 21.73 s vs 23.85 s, factor **1.0975**; pv3 16.82 s vs 18.0 s, factor **1.069**; pv2 1.006, usable). Every transcript time in those reels had to be multiplied by the factor to meet the picture (pv1 "It's" 12.01 × 1.0975 = 13.18 s vs the punch card at 13.14 s). With the user's footage: if audio and video durations differ by > 0.1 s, re-mux or resample before transcribing; never place captions on a drifting clock.

- One voice track; −14 LUFS, ≤ −1.0 dBTP (§11).

## §15 Look at your frames
Not a checklist; the questions the best editor asks while scrubbing:
- **Frame 0, sound off:** is there a beautiful page with one big real thing (or the creator mid-word, warm and whole) and a word on it by f2? Does the pen draw to it?
- **Their video and every picture:** full screen, or in a designed box it fills on the style's own ground? Never a sharp rectangle (footage, recording, landscape clip, photo) over its own blur, never blurred side bars, never a card on their blurred frame?
- **Scale:** shrink the still to thumbnail size. Is the card the first thing I see, or a small picture in a sea of paper? Is any frame mostly bare ground for more than a breath? Is the selfie card wide, not a pillar?
- **Every move:** does a card on the same ground glide to its next place, or does it pop? Does it land with weight and keep drifting? Does the face card open and fold on a clause start?
- **The proof:** when the voice names a number or a line, does the card push in and does the pen circle it?
- **The face:** whole hair to chin in every state, rich colour (never pale), the caption just under the chin on full frame and above the card in a card?
- **The words:** is every spoken word on screen at a size I can read on a phone? One caption band? Spelled as spoken?
- **The structure:** does the ground change only when the section does? Do the hub's ring and the letter slots fill and pay off?
- **Sound:** paper, pen, soft air and a few weights, on their pictures; nothing under the creator talking; no file overused; silence before the punch; no music?
- **Vibe:** calm, smart, premium. Anything loud, colourful or busy comes out.
