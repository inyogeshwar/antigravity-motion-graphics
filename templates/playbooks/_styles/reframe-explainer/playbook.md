# Reframe Explainer Playbook (v2, "Room Above the Head, Elevated" edition, for Claude Opus 5.5)

> **In this plugin:** this file is the whole playbook: every measured value and recipe from the 3 reference reels is in it, and there is no other playbook file to open. Frame counts are measured at each reel's own rate (aa1 at **25 fps**, aa2 and aa3 at **30 fps**; the reel is named beside every number): convert with t = f / fps when you draw (`frame(t)` runs at the render fps). Positions are on 1080×1920. The per-reel notes it cites (`aa1…aa3`) are not shipped. Sounds are picked by id from SOUNDS.md; commands are in the edit skill's REFERENCE.md. The page gives you `FACE(n)` and `FACE0` (with `head_top` and `chin`) for every reframe and crop.

**Purpose:** you (Claude) receive a talking-head video of the creator plus its script. Use this document to **ideate and build** every reframe, branded title, screen card, explainer scene, chat receipt, caption, move and sound, then edit the Reel so it looks like the best version of a calm, bright, premium productivity explainer: **the speaker stays himself, full frame, and the picture moves to make room for the proof**, and every piece of proof arrives so well-made that the viewer can't look away.

**What v2 is:** a frame-by-frame analysis of 3 Ali Abdaal reels (aa1 "5 productivity apps I use every single day", a branded listicle; aa2 "Focus on one thing per quarter", a concept explainer with full-screen dark-canvas scenes; aa3 "Our new AI employee", a week-in-the-life case study with recreated Slack receipts), **elevated ten times**: the same grammar, drawn by the best motion designer and creative director in the world, for more retention and more engagement. The references' measured layouts, recipes and rules are all kept here. What changes is the craft on top of them: animation principles on every element, a strict design hierarchy in every frame, effects that make each brand and each idea feel physical, and a full sound score where the references had none. Where a reference reel and a direction disagree, **the directions win**.

**Measured sizes are floors, not targets.** The references' card sizes and positions are what they measured; the thing being named is drawn as large as its zone allows and is the one hero of its frame. A top zone with a small logo floating in empty space is unfinished.
- **Full screen or a box, never any footage or picture over its own blur** (Naman, 13 Oct): the creator's video, a screen recording, a landscape clip, a photo or a diagram either fills the whole frame edge to edge (cover-crop to the part that matters, or a push-in), or sits in a designed box (rounded, rimmed or softly shadowed, on this style's own ground, colour, gradient, texture, grid or drawn set) that it fills completely. Never shrink anything inside the frame over a blurred or darkened copy of itself, never pad a box, band or panel with blurred side bars, never set a card on the creator's own blurred frame. A tight selfie stays full screen as shot: move the text, not the video (eyes to chin always in; the hair may touch the top edge). When the whole head must show, use a box sized so the footage fills it with the head inside. (A matte cut-out over a plate is fine: it has no box edge.)

### The feel (read this twice)
This is the edit of **a friend who has tested everything and is excited to show you what actually works**. He's warm, quick and certain; he never shouts. The frame is bright, clean and generous: his face, a bookshelf glowing behind him, and above his head, whatever he names, arriving **in its own identity** as if the product itself had walked in: the app's logo landing with a little weight, its wordmark settling letter by letter, its real screen sliding in on a card that glows in the brand's own colour. When he explains an idea, the room goes dark and the idea becomes an object you could touch: bars that fill, a calendar that books itself, a question that grows out of a dot. When he tells a story, the receipts pop up one by one. Every move has weight, follow-through and a sound; nothing is decoration, nothing is loud. It should feel like **Apple-level polish on a YouTube-friend energy**. If a frame looks like a hype reel, it's wrong; if it looks like a slideshow, it's wrong too.

**Who the reference creator is:** a warm, articulate, fast-talking (4.5–4.9 words/s) British doctor-turned-entrepreneur who teaches productivity, tools and running a creator business. He speaks to the camera like a friend who has tested everything, with constant open-handed gestures. He never shouts; enthusiasm comes from pace and specificity ("I've used this since 2018", "142 applications, up 18%").

**Audience (ICP):** knowledge workers, students, founders and creators, English-speaking, ambitious and tool-curious. They want **the specific thing** (the app, the framework, the actual workflow) and the proof that it works, delivered quickly and cleanly. They trust receipts: real screens, real numbers, real people.

**Style lineage:** a clean, bright, "explainer-doc" creator edit, built from three layers:
1. **Speaker layer:** one tripod setup, medium close-up, full frame. He is never boxed, never shrunk, never put in a picture-in-picture of the live shot. When something must be shown, **the footage eases down** to make room above his head, and back up when it leaves.
2. **Proof layer:** whatever is named appears in its own identity: the app in its own logo, type and colour; the screen recording in a rounded card glowing in that brand's colour; the idea as a concrete object (progress bars, a calendar block); the claim as the actual chat thread with a red circle on the number.
3. **Caption layer:** a pale pill, 2–5 words, where each word turns from grey to black as it is spoken (colour karaoke). The thread of speech never breaks, across every layout.
- **This style is not one layout.** One creator, one caption system and one temperament carry **three formats** (§3): the top-zone listicle, the dark-canvas concept explainer and the receipts documentary. Pick the format from the script.

## §1 Procedure (follow in order)

1. **Inventory.** Probe the footage. One tripod medium close-up is the house setup; note whether it's framed loose (aa1, head top ≈ 300–350) or tight (aa2, head top ≈ 15–130). A loose frame is needed for `FMT-TOPZONE` and `FMT-RECEIPTS` (the footage must be able to move down ~270 px and still show the whole head).
2. **Transcribe** with word timestamps. Captions are the spoken words in phrases of 2–5 (§5.2), each word timed for the grey→black sweep. Check every product and person name.
3. **Choose the format** (§3.0): a list of things → `FMT-TOPZONE`; an idea → `FMT-CANVAS`; a story with proof → `FMT-RECEIPTS`. One line on why.
4. **Segment** the script: `HOOK` (0–4 s), `ITEM-n` / `IDEA-n` / `DAY-n` (each opened by an ordinal or chapter word: "Firstly / Next up / Thirdly…", "On Monday… On Tuesday…"), `PAYOFF`, `CTA`/`RECAP`.
5. **Mark every named thing** (app, book, author, person, metric, tool) with its onset: each gets its visual in its own identity (§8), landing 0–4 f after the name.
6. **Plan the speaker map:** for each beat, full frame clean (framing A), full frame pushed down for a top graphic (framing B), absent (full-screen scene), or B-roll.
7. **Ideate 3 hooks** (§6) and recommend one.
8. **Fill the beat sheet** (§13): one beat per named thing or chapter, with the layout, the pattern, the reframe, the transition, the caption phrases, **the hero of the frame and its sounds**.
8b. **The 10× pass** (§8.0): for every beat, name its hero, how it anticipates, lands and follows through, its secondary action, and where it came from and goes. Then **attack the plan** like the best editor you know: where is a frame still? Where is a logo small in empty space? Where does something appear from nowhere? Where would a viewer swipe? Where is it busy or flashy? Fix it in the plan.
8c. **Score it** (§11): every graphic event gets its sound, logged in the sound ledger.
9. **Produce the assets** (§8.6): real logos in their real colours, real screen recordings of the creator's own use, recreated UIs when a screenshot would be unreadable, real photos for cited people, book covers, B-roll of the creator and team at work.
10. **Show the creator** the plan and stills (§13.3).
11. **Build, look at your frames (§15), score, render (§16).**

## §2 Craft that never bends
- **The whole face is always visible:** hair to chin at every reframe, punch-in and jump cut, with air above the hair (head top ≥ 80 px from the frame top); every move is capped by the face (`FACE0`, `FACE(n)`). A caption may cross the chin; a graphic may not cover the face. A source tighter than that stays full screen as shot (eyes to chin always in; the hair may touch the top edge): move the text, never shrink the video over its own blur.
- **Text never sits on text;** nothing sits on the caption pill; graphics keep their labels clear of the pill's band.
- **Sound in sync:** every SFX on the frame its picture lands, never on a consonant of the word it illustrates (slide ±2 f).
- **True and exact:** every number, date, name and quote from the script or real sources; real logos in real colours; spelling exact.
- **Readable at phone size:** anything that must be read is ≥ 26 px; crop a screen to what matters rather than shrinking it.
- **Loudness:** −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP; end on the last word (≤ 0.4 s), never on accidental black.

Everything else is direction. You decide; write your reasons into the plan.

### 2.1 The house grammar (what makes it this style)
Not checks: this is what the three reference reels do every time, and why it works. Break one only with a reason in the plan. (The IDs are kept because other sections cite them.)
- M1. **The speaker stays full frame and whole.** He is never boxed, never shrunk into a picture-in-picture, never split-screened (all 3 reels). When a graphic needs the top of the frame, **the footage eases down** (§3.2), never the graphic down onto him.
- M2. **The whole face is always visible.** At every reframe, punch-in and jump cut, the head (hair to chin) stays inside the frame with a little air above it. Fix: aa2's tight framing let the hair touch the top edge on punch-ins; ours stops before that. How far a punch-in or a reframe goes is decided by the face, never by the frame. A caption may cross the face; a graphic may not.
- M3. **Every named thing appears in its own identity, on its name:** the app's own logo, type and colour; the book's real cover; the person's real photo with their name; the metric in the real (or recreated) interface. Lands 0–4 f after the word starts (aa1, aa2).
- M4. **Show the receipt, not the claim** (aa3): a request → a reply → an output, timed to the narration; a red hand-drawn circle walks down the numbers as they are read.
- M5. **Ideas become objects** (aa2): goals are progress bars, focus is one bar filling green, a schedule is a calendar block.
- M6. **The caption pill rides on top of every layout** (§5.2), each word turning from grey to black as it is spoken: under his chin when he's on screen, at one fixed low y over full-screen scenes. The thread of speech never breaks across scene changes.
- M7. **Pauses and breaths cut:** 4.5–4.9 words/s, no gap > ~0.35 s; a jump cut on each ordinal ("Firstly", "Next up", "On Monday").
- M8. **Something changes every ≤ ~2 s** (a cut, a reframe, a graphic event); a talking-head shot never runs > 5 s without a jump cut or an overlay change.
- M9. **Brand colour = glow colour.** A screen card glows in its brand's colour (Notion/Day One teal, Momentum purple); a card's header colour codes its task (aa3).
- M10. **Captions and graphics never collide with a key label:** fix aa2 (the pill covered the Hobby bar at 27–30 s): move the graphic, not the pill.
- M11. **Facts from the script or sources:** every number, date, name and quote real.
- M12. **Audio:** −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP. Fixes: aa1 −0.1 dBTP (too hot), aa3 −22 LUFS (too quiet), aa2 −18 LUFS.
- M13. **End clean:** a hard stop ≤ 0.4 s after the last word.

**Never in this style:**
- N1. A picture-in-picture or split of the live speaker, a cut-out of the speaker pasted on a canvas (except matting his hair in front of a card's bottom edge, §3.2).
- N2. Flashes, glitches, light leaks, spins. Transitions are eased reframes, pushes, morphs, blurs and cuts (whooshes belong to the sound score, §11, never to the picture).
- N3. Coloured caption words or keyword highlight colours inside the pill; emphasis is only the grey→black sweep.
- N10. A music bed, ever (§11).
- N4. Generic stock "tech" footage; every visual is the actual thing named, or a designed metaphor of it.
- N5. Illegible scramble reveals (aa1's "Momentum" read "Om en um" for 12 frames: keep letter reveals ≤ 10 f and legible at every frame).
- N6. Mixing stepped (on-twos) and smooth motion in one reel: the motion character is chosen per reel (§10.1).
- N7. B-roll at the wrong frame rate (aa3's 25 fps clips judder in a 30 fps timeline: conform them).
- N8. Unreadable holds: a zoom that blurs the chat for more than ~1 s (aa3 carousel) is a transition, never a hold.
- N9. Cropping the speaker's face in the talking head. (Documentary B-roll of other people may be framed close, but the creator's own talking head never is.)

### 3.2 The reframe (signature move, `T-REFRAME`)
- When a top-zone graphic is due, the **whole footage layer eases down 250–300 px** (and may scale −5…−15%) over **8–12 f**, ease-in-out; when the graphic leaves, it eases back up. The graphic's exit and the reframe up happen together, so the frame "closes over" the gap (aa1 f389–399, f664–676, f1158–1168).
- The top zone (y ≈ 100–560, x ≈ 180–920) belongs to the graphic; the face lives below it.
- **Head-in-front-of-card:** when a card's bottom edge meets his hair, matte him so the hair sits **in front of** the card (aa1 24.4 s). The face always wins.
- Under the reframe, the hands may leave the bottom of the frame; the face never does.

### 3.2b The reframe, elevated
- **Anticipation:** 2–3 f before the move, the footage drifts up ~6 px (a breath in), then eases down; the graphic's spark or shadow appears in the top zone during that breath, so the eye already knows where to look.
- **Overlap:** the graphic arrives 2–4 f *before* the footage settles; it overshoots ~4% and settles as the footage lands. Two things moving together, never one after the other.
- **Follow-through:** when the footage lands, it settles with a 1–2% ease (no bounce on his face); the graphic's glow blooms ~15% brighter and relaxes over 10 f.
- **Depth:** while the footage is down, a soft parallax: the card drifts ±6 px against him over its hold, the background bokeh of his room drifts the other way by a hair (scale 1.00 → 1.01). It feels like the card is in the room with him.
- **Up on the opinion:** the graphic exits *toward where it is going* (into the next item's slot, off the side in the story's direction), and the footage rises under it in the same move; the frame closes like a door, never with a gap.
- **Sound:** a soft low whoosh on the way down (`woosh-2025-08-27-02-12-31-utc-woosh-02` / `air-low-woosh`), a lighter one up (`light-woosh-7119`), rotated; never the same file twice in a row.

### 4.3 Meaning of colour
- **Brand colour** = that brand, nothing else.
- **Green** = progress, the chosen focus, "it works".
- **Gold / salmon** = categories of life or work (neutral).
- **Red circle** = "look at this number".
- **Yellow neon** = the one idea the reel is about.
- **Pale pill, black words** = what he's saying right now.

### 4.4 Footage look (don't fight it)
Natural, warm-to-neutral, soft contrast, lifted blacks, moderate saturation, shallow depth of field with colourful bokeh (a bookshelf, a travel poster, a plant). Keep it as shot or a touch richer; **never pale** (no fading, no milky blacks). `FMT-CANVAS` graphic scenes and full-screen recordings get grain, dust and a vignette (an archival, hand-made feel); the talking head stays clean.

### 5.2 `CAP-PILL` (the one caption system)
| Property | Spec |
|---|---|
| Text | **Every spoken word**, sentence case, punctuation kept ("we have Notion,") |
| Phrase | 2–5 words (typical 3–4), phrase-aligned |
| Font | Poppins Bold/SemiBold, ~42–46 px (cap ~32 px) |
| Pill | `#E6E3E7`, radius 16–18, height 96–100, width hugs the text (≈420–770 px), centred x 540; no stroke, no shadow |
| Colour karaoke | A new phrase hard-swaps in **all grey** (`#A3A2A5`); each word turns **black** (`#141414`) over 2–3 f as it is spoken |
| Position | **Just under his chin** (the pill's top ≈ 40–80 px below `FACE(n)` chin) so it is seen the moment the eye leaves his face, and it **travels with the footage** in the same eased move when he reframes (never jumps). Over full-screen scenes with no face it sits at one fixed low y (≈ 1224–1435). The references fixed it at the chest (≈ 1435 aa1, 1414 aa3, 1224 aa2): too low to be noticed when the frame is full |
| Layering | Always the top layer, over every layout, B-roll and graphic scene; it may cross his chin, never his eyes |
| Absent | Only while a word-synced hook title or kinetic text *is* the caption (aa3 0–4.5 s) |

**Measured caption timing (aa1 f243–272, 25 fps):** "Next up we have" on screen; at f254 "Claude from Anthropic." hard-swaps in all grey; "Claude" black by f260 (its onset ~f256), "from" by f264, "Anthropic." by f270. The black arrives 2–4 f after each word's onset, never before it.

**Phrase breaking:** break at commas and before a new clause; never leave a single function word alone in a pill ("apps that I" is fine, "I" alone is not); brand names and numbers stay in one pill with their noun ("142 applications", "Claude from Anthropic").

**Pill width:** hugs the text with ~32 px side padding; when a phrase would pass ~770 px, split it. The pill resizes instantly on each new phrase (the pill itself never animates).

## §6 Hook system

### 6.0 The 10× hook
- **Frame 0 is never empty and never still:** his face mid-word and, already in the top zone, the first piece of the promise mid-arrival (a tile mid-flight with its shadow, the title blob filling, the neon word flickering on).
- **One hero at a time:** in the first 3 s the eye is led by a single moving thing per beat: tile → numeral → title → his face. Never three things arriving at once.
- **The open loop is visible:** the five tiles are there but unnamed; the answer bar is grey; the doubt is underlined. The viewer can *see* what they don't know yet.
- **Sound from frame 0:** a soft appear on the first tile, a rising 3-step blip set across the tiles, a sparkle on the title's last word.

### 6.1 What the hook must do in about 3 seconds
1. **Frame 0 is already moving:** an icon flying in, a title blob on screen, an eased push-in on him (aa2 f0–15).
2. **Make a specific promise** with a number or a novelty: "five productivity apps I use every single day", "one thing for the next 90 days", "we hired an AI employee".
3. **Open a loop the structure will pay:** five unnamed icons (which is which?), a withheld answer ("what would happen if…"), an honest doubt ("I genuinely did not know if the team would use him").
Measured: 8–12 visual changes in the first 3 s.

**Measured first 3 s:**
- aa1 (25 fps): f3 icon 1 · f8–24 icons 2–5 · f10 "5" · f18 "PRODUCTIVITY" · f22–24 "APPS" · f32–55 sub-line types · 5 caption swaps · f71 cut on "Firstly" (~12 changes).
- aa2 (30 fps): f0–15 push-in · f3 caption · 4 caption swaps · f17 jump cut · f53 hard cut to the "ONE THING" scene · orbiting objects stepping every 2–3 f (~8).
- aa3 (30 fps): f4–38 title words one by one + sparkles · f54 cut to B-roll · ~f60 "I genuinely" · ~f72–80 "did not know" + scribble · ~f90 "if the team" (~10).

## §7 Structure devices

### 7.0 The shape of each format
- **`FMT-TOPZONE`:** hook (the whole list as icons) → item 1…N, each **name → proof → opinion** (logo on the name, card on the description, reframe up for his opinion) → the comment question. 5 items in ~50 s, ~9 s per item.
- **`FMT-CANVAS`:** hook question → the problem (talking) → the problem as a picture (the list) → the turn (talking) → the answer as a picture (the focus fill) → authority (covers) → the method (the question card) → the practice (the calendar) → the last practical line. Talking and pictures alternate every ~3–8 s.
- **`FMT-RECEIPTS`:** hook (story + doubt + promise) → chapter per day/step, each **request → work → output → reaction** → the payoff line on the A-cam → the recap that repeats the promise.

| ID | Device | Recipe |
|---|---|---|
| SD-1 | **Ordinal chapters** | "Firstly / Next up / Thirdly / Fourthly / And finally" or "On Monday… On Tuesday…", each landing on a jump cut or a directional push |
| SD-2 | **Logo → card ritual** (per item, `FMT-TOPZONE`) | Reframe down; the logo lands on the name; ~1.5–2.5 s later the logo pushes out left as the glowing screen card pushes in from the right; the card exits on the next ordinal while the footage reframes up |
| SD-3 | **The list that becomes the answer** (`FMT-CANVAS`) | The problem as a list of bars building on each spoken item; later the same list returns with all fills grey except the one chosen, which glows and fills green on the payoff phrase |
| SD-4 | **Cite with covers** | Each source as a 3D book mockup that "lights up", its author as a cut-out photo with a handwritten name |
| SD-5 | **Key question card** | The method's core question on a white card that grows from a dot, with the key words bold, lines tracking in |
| SD-6 | **The thread** (`FMT-RECEIPTS`) | Each chapter: request bubble → agent working → reply → output card → the human's thanks; the stack scrolls up; the oldest bubble slides off the top |
| SD-7 | **Red-circle walk-down** | A hand-drawn ellipse writes around the first number and re-draws on each next line as it's read (~1.2–1.5 s per line) |
| SD-8 | **People with names** | Team members on B-roll with a handwritten name label |

**Open loops:** unnamed icons paid by ordinals; the withheld answer paid by the green fill; the doubt paid by the recap.

## §8 Graphics system

### 8.0 The 10× layer (what the best motion designer adds to every element)
**Animation principles, applied to this style:**
| Principle | How it shows up here |
|---|---|
| **Anticipation** | A spark, a soft shadow, a tiny pull-back (3–5% scale down for 2–3 f) before every landing; the footage breathes up before it eases down |
| **Squash & stretch (subtle)** | A dropping logo stretches ~6% along its fall and squashes ~4% on impact for 2 f; a popping bubble 0.95 → 1.05 → 1.00. Never rubbery: this is premium, not cartoon |
| **Follow-through & overlap** | Wordmark letters land in a wave 2 f apart after the icon; a card's glow, shadow and content settle 1–3 f after the card itself; bars in a list land with a 3 f stagger |
| **Arcs** | Tiles, logos and covers travel on curved paths (never straight lines); the scatter-out spins along arcs to the nearest edge |
| **Slow in / slow out** | Expo-out entries, ease-in-out moves, ease-in exits; nothing moves linearly except stepped canvas scenes and typing |
| **Staging** | One hero per frame; everything else dims 15–25% or blurs slightly when the hero lands |
| **Secondary action** | While a card holds: the recording plays with a slow 1.00 → 1.04 push, dust drifts through the canvas light, the glow breathes ±5%, a cursor moves in the screen |
| **Timing** | Small things fast (pops 4–5 f), big things heavier (cards 10–12 f, reframes 8–12 f), ideas slowest (fills 30–35 f) |
| **Exaggeration** | One moment per reel goes bigger: the focus fill (a 105% scale, a glow, a warm swell), the payoff receipt (a 2.5× push), the last logo |
| **Appeal** | Rounded corners, soft real shadows, brand-true colour, warm light; everything looks like something you'd want to touch |

**Design hierarchy (every frame):**
1. **One hero:** the thing he's naming, at the largest size its zone allows, the brightest and sharpest element on screen.
2. **His face:** always second, never covered, never competing (no graphic overlaps the face).
3. **Supporting:** the wordmark, a label, the handwritten name: smaller, quieter, placed by the hero.
4. **The caption pill:** constant, below the face, never fighting the hero for the eye.
Read the frame in that order. If the eye goes anywhere else first, the frame is wrong.

**Effects toolkit (use with taste, one or two per element):**
- **Brand glow:** the screen card's outer glow in the brand colour (~15 px), blooming on landing and breathing gently while it holds.
- **Light sweep:** a soft diagonal band of light (white at 18–25%, 120 px wide) crosses a card's glass once on arrival (14–18 f).
- **Depth:** a soft blur layer in front (bokeh specks, ~8 px blur) drifting slower than the card; a contact shadow under every card and logo that tightens as it lands.
- **Shared-element morph:** a logo becomes its card (the icon expands into the card's corner while the card grows from it); a bubble's number becomes the output card's stat.
- **Sparkles** (`sparkle` yellow) around a name or a payoff, 3–5 small four-point stars twinkling out over 12 f, once per name, never as decoration.
- **Grain, dust and vignette** on the dark canvas only (aa2's hand-made look); the talking head stays clean.
- **Motion blur** on anything moving > 40 px per frame.
Never: flashes, lens flares, RGB splits, glitches, shakes, neon outside the aa2 neon title.

4. **Leave on the next ordinal**, with the footage reframing back up, or push it out as the next arrives.

### 8.3a Frame-by-frame recipes (the ones that make the style)
**R-1 Listicle icon scatter (aa1 f3–24 in, f80–92 out, 25 fps):** each tile starts at ~10% scale on a curved path from below-centre and settles into an arc (y 390–650) with a few degrees of tilt, staggered ~4 f, ease-out; white keyline, soft shadow. Out: every tile rotates 15–40° and flies to the nearest frame edge in ~10 f while the first item's tile spins to its new slot left of centre.

**R-2 Carousel push (aa1 f139–150):** the outgoing element and the incoming card move left together over 10–12 f, ~70% of the travel in the first 4 f, horizontal motion blur strongest at f2–5; the incoming card arrives blank white and its recording cross-fades in 4–6 f after it lands.

**R-3 Logo drop (aa1 f457–462):** the icon falls from above the frame rotating ~30°, lands in ~6 f with a ~5% overshoot and settles; its wordmark letters reveal over the next ~10 f.

**R-4 Letter rise (aa1 f1021–1033, "DAY ONE"):** a spark dot appears (f1017), the icon pops from that point (f1021), each letter rises ~20 px from below with a fade, ~2 f apart.

**R-5 Recreated UI build (aa1 f289–299):** the input box starts as a thin vertical line at centre and grows to full width in ~10 f (ease-out) with its amber glow; the greeting's words appear one at a time; the placeholder types in.

**R-6 Progress-bar wipe (aa2 f298 onward, stepped):** a thin rounded sliver appears at the left edge and grows rightward to full width over ~20 f in visible steps (every 2–3 f), revealing the emoji and the label; the coloured left fill (20–30% of the bar) sits under the emoji; the stack scrolls up so the newest bar sits near y 900–1100.

**R-7 Focus fill (aa2 26.4–29.3 s):** the scene cuts in already built; the other bars lose their fill to grey; the chosen bar scales to ~105% with a white outer glow; a green fill sweeps left→right over ~35 f, finishing on the payoff phrase ("enormous progress").

**R-8 Card from a dot (aa2 f1305–1320):** a white dot → a small square → the full card in ~15 f; the emoji tab pops at the top-left; each text line starts with very wide letter-spacing and tightens to normal over ~10 f, line after line; key words bold.

**R-9 Chat pop (aa3):** the bubble appears at its final centre at ~20% scale with zoom blur, reaches ~105% in 4–5 f and settles to 100% in 2–3 f (ease-out-back); the stack above nudges up ~8 f; when the stack is too tall, the oldest bubble slides off the top with a fade and blur.

## §9 Transition system (`T`)
| ID | Transition | Frames | Recipe | Seen in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T-01 | **Jump cut** | 0 | Straight, on a word boundary; often with a 5–15% scale change (always keeping the whole head) | all |

| T-03 | **Carousel push** | 10–12 | Outgoing element slides left, incoming from the right, heavy motion blur, ease-out (most travel in the first 4 f) | aa1 |
| T-04 | **Hard cut to a scene** | 0 | Talking head ↔ full-screen graphic scene, on the noun; the scene usually already built on its first frame | aa2 |
| T-05 | **Blur-through cut** | ~8 + ~6–8 | Outgoing ramps a Gaussian blur (with a slight push-in) over ~8 f, cut, incoming starts blurred and sharpens over 6–8 f | aa2 (second half) |
| T-06 | **Directional push** | 6–10 | Both shots move together up / down / left / diagonal, strong directional blur in the middle frames, ease-in-out; a new direction per chapter | aa3 |
| T-07 | **Camera move on a graphic** | ~20 + 15/swipe | Push into a deliverable, then swipe it | aa3 |

| T-09 | **Hard end** | 0 | ≤ 0.4 s after the last word | all |

| T-10 | **Logo → card morph** (EV-1) | ~12 | The logo becomes the card's corner while the card grows from it | aa1 elevated |
| T-11 | **Orbit turn** (EV-2) | ~10 | The hook's tile orbit rotates the next tile to the front | aa1 elevated |

## §10 Motion tokens, camera, layers, finishing

### 10.1 Motion character (pick one per reel)
| Character | Use | Values |
|---|---|---|
| **Eased** (aa1, aa3) | Listicles, receipts | Ease-out entries with a slight overshoot (~105% → 100%), 4–12 f; pushes 6–12 f with motion blur |
| **Stepped** (aa2) | Concept explainers on the dark canvas | Graphics animate on twos/threes (~10–15 fps) with paper texture, grain and soft glow; the talking head stays at the full rate |

### 10.1a Stepped motion in detail (aa2)
- **What is stepped:** only the graphic scenes. The talking head, the caption pill and its grey→black sweep stay at the full frame rate.
- **The rate:** the graphic changes every 2–3 frames at 30 fps (frame-difference spikes at f57, 59, 62, 65, 68…), i.e. ~10–15 drawn poses per second. Hold each pose for 2 or 3 frames and alternate irregularly (2, 3, 3, 2…) so it feels hand-made, not mechanical.
- **Texture:** a fine film grain over the canvas, a few dust specks that change every few poses, a soft glow on bright elements (the neon title, the chosen bar), a gentle vignette.
- **What moves in steps:** bar wipes, orbiting objects, the focus fill, the book light-reveal, the card from a dot, calendar blocks dropping.
- **Never:** stepped motion over the talking head, or mixed with smooth easing inside the same scene.

### 10.1b Eased motion in detail (aa1, aa3)
- Entries ease out with a small overshoot (~105% → 100%); pushes and reframes ease in-out; motion blur on anything crossing more than ~40 px per frame.
- Every graphic lands within its trigger word's first 4 frames; nothing drifts in late.
- Exits happen with the reframe up or the next push; nothing fades slowly out on its own.

### 10.2 Motion tokens
| Token | Value |
|---|---|
| Lead | Visuals land 0–5 f after the spoken name starts |
| Reframe | 8–12 f, ease-in-out, 250–300 px |
| Pop | ~20% → 105% in 4–5 f (zoom-blurred), settle 2–3 f |
| Letter reveal | ≤ 10 f, legible every frame; rise-in ~2 f/letter |
| Wipe-in bar | ~20 f from the left |
| Focus fill | ~35 f left→right |
| Light-reveal | ~30 f |
| Track-in text | wide spacing → normal over ~10 f per line |
| Walk-down circle | write-on ~6 f, ~1.2–1.5 s per line |
| Caption sweep | 2–3 f per word, grey → black |
| Anticipation | 2–3 f, a 3–5% pull-back or a 6 px rise before a landing or a reframe |
| Squash on impact | 4% for 2 f, recovering by f+4 (logos, bubbles); never on his footage |
| Follow-through | Glow, shadow and content settle 1–3 f after their card |
| Light sweep | 14–18 f, once per card |
| Parallax hold | ±6 px over a hold; foreground blur layer at half the speed |
| Pill travel | The caption pill moves with the reframe: same frames, same ease |

### 10.3 Camera
One tripod medium close-up. The only camera "moves" on the talking head are the eased reframes, an eased push-in at the open (aa2), 5–15% jump-cut scale changes and an eased zoom-out at the close. **Every one keeps the whole head in frame** (M2). Graphics may have their own camera (the zoom into an output).

### 10.4 Layer order (back to front)
1. Canvas / gradient / footage
2. Graphic cards (screen cards, bubbles, bars)
3. The speaker's matte (only where his hair meets a card's bottom edge)
4. Labels, names, annotations
5. Title blobs / kinetic text
6. Caption pill (always on top)

### 10.5 Finishing
- Talking head: clean, as shot.
- `FMT-CANVAS` scenes: grain, dust, a vignette, soft glows.
- Cards: soft shadows; brand-colour glows on screen cards.

## §11 Sound (D4)

**No music bed, ever.** Sound is SFX only (the creator's direction, 12 Oct 2026): never a music track, bed, drone or loop under the voice.

The references ran a dry voice with no SFX at all (aa1 −16.1 LUFS and −0.1 dBTP, aa2 −18.3, aa3 −22.0). This edition **scores every graphic event**, quietly: the viewer should feel the weight of each landing more than notice the sound.

**Never in this style:** meme sounds, cartoon boings, crowd reactions, record scratches, glitches, alarms, heavy hits, risers that scream.

### 11.2 The SFX ledger (Naman's rule, unchanged)
- **Any file at most 2× in a reel**, except the one **list cue** (here the 0552611/21/31 set for the hook tiles, or `paper-turn-page` once per chapter or cover).
- **No file on two consecutive cues;** rotate within each pool.
- **Density:** about one cue every 1.2–1.8 s on average, each tied to a picture; plain jump cuts get nothing.
- **Never more than two cues overlapping;** never on a consonant of the word it illustrates.
- Landings and steps are texture; the payoff shine and the one weight hit are the ones the viewer should notice.

### 11.3 No music bed, silence
No music bed, ever: the voice and the SFX carry the reel. **Go silent (no new SFX) for 0.5–0.8 s before the payoff** (the focus fill, the result, the recap line), then land it.

### 11.4 Mix
- **The voice carries it:** a loud, compressed, dry voice, every pause cut; high-pass 80 Hz, light compression, de-ess.
- **−14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP** (fixes all three references). The voice is always the loudest thing.

## §15 Look at your frames
Not a checklist; the questions the best editor asks while scrubbing:
- **The footage frame:** is every video, recording and picture full screen or in a designed box it fills, never a sharp rectangle over its own blur, never padded with blurred bars?
- **Frame 0, sound off:** is his face there mid-word and is the promise already arriving above it?
- **Every landing:** did it anticipate, land with weight and follow through? Did it come from somewhere?
- **Every frame:** is there one hero, and is it big? Is his whole head in, with air above the hair? Is the pill right under his chin?
- **Every named thing:** is it in its own identity, on its name, glowing in its own colour?
- **Every idea:** is it an object you could touch, built in steps on the grainy canvas?
- **Every claim:** is the receipt on screen, with the number circled as he reads it?
- **Sound:** does every event have its sound, quiet and on the frame, with no file overused and no music?
- **Vibe:** bright, warm, premium. Anything flashy, cartoonish or busy comes out.

## §16 Build + render
- **Pipeline:** the plugin's edit skill: plan, draw every frame in `reel.js` (`frame(t)`), look at stills, score (`plan/sounds.json`), storyboard, render.
- **Fonts (bundled):** Poppins, Anton, Barlow Condensed, Instrument Serif, Fraunces, Caveat, Inter Tight.
- **Helpers you'll want:** `Footage(t)` (framing A/B with the eased reframe, matte for hair-in-front), `CapPill(phrases, words)` (grey→black per word), `Wordmark(brand)`, `ScreenCard(clip, glow)`, `CarouselPush`, `RecreatedUI`, `SyncDiagram`, `ProgressList`, `FocusFill`, `BookMockup`, `PersonCutout(name)`, `QuestionCard`, `GradedCalendar`, `ChatThread(bubbles)`, `OutputCard`, `RedCircle(lines)`, `TitleBlob`, `KineticText`, `Push(direction)`, `BlurThrough`, `LogoMorph`, `Orbit`, `LiveScreen`, `TypingDots`, `RollStat`, `Shelf`, `DayBuilder`, `FocusFill`, `LightSweep`, `Parallax`, `Sparkles`. All pure functions of t.
- **Final:** 1080×1920, H.264, AAC, −14 LUFS; check a contact sheet of the final file.
