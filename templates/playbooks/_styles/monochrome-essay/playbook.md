# Black Page Essay Playbook (v2, "Gold Serif, White Light, Elevated" edition, for Claude Opus 5.5)

> **In this plugin:** this file is the whole playbook: every measured value and recipe from the 3 reference reels is in it, and there is no other playbook file to open. Frame counts are measured at each reel's own rate (dk1 at **24 fps**, dk2 at **29.97 fps**, dk3 at **30 fps**; the reel is named beside every number): convert with t = f / fps when you draw (`frame(t)` runs at the render fps). Positions are on 1080×1920. The per-reel notes it cites (`dk1…dk3`) are not shipped. Sounds are picked by id from SOUNDS.md; commands are in the edit skill's REFERENCE.md. The page gives you `FACE(n)` and `FACE0` (with `head_top` and `chin`) for every face frame.

**Purpose:** you (Claude) receive a talking-head video of the creator plus its script. Use this document to **ideate and build** every black page, caption, drawn scene, transition and sound, then edit the Reel so it feels like a quiet essay read aloud in a dark room: **few words on screen, one picture per thought, and the face only when the face is the point**, drawn so beautifully that the viewer stays to the last word.

**What v2 is:** a frame-by-frame analysis of 3 Dan Koe reels (dk1 "How to change your life in 1 day": a black voice-over page, then a locked talking head with gold serif subtitles; dk2 "Life is a game but you aren't even playing": faceless white line art on black, carried by one white dot; dk3 "You weren't born to work 40 hours a week": faceless glowing light objects that morph, ending on his own book), **elevated ten times** by the best motion designer and creative director in the world. The restraint is the style, so the craft goes **into** the restraint: line art that draws itself with real hand and weight, light that blooms and breathes, one dot that remembers its whole journey, a face in full, rich colour against the black, captions you can actually read, and a whisper-quiet sound score of air and light. All measured layouts, recipes and rules are kept. Where a reference reel and a direction disagree, **the directions win**.

**Measured sizes are floors, not targets.** The references' captions (52–54 px) and in-world text were too small for a phone; here they are larger, and the one drawn object of each scene owns the middle of the frame.

**Full screen or a box, never any footage or picture over its own blur** (Naman, 13 Oct): the creator's video either fills the whole frame edge to edge, or sits in a designed box (rounded corners, a rim or soft shadow, on this style's own ground) that it fills completely, cropped around the face. The same for every other medium: screen recordings, landscape clips, photos, receipts, diagrams and other people's footage either fill the frame (cover-cropped to the part that matters, or a push-in) or sit in a designed box on the style's own ground or scene (its colour, gradient, texture, grid or drawn set). Never shrink anything over a blurred or darkened copy of itself, never pad a band or box with blurred side bars, never set a card on the creator's blurred frame: a hard rectangle against its own blur looks cheap. A tight selfie stays full screen: move or shrink the text above the head, not the video; the hair may touch the top edge, eyes to chin are always in. When the whole head must be seen, use a box layout where it fits.

**Minimal means few, not small.** This style has few things on screen, so each one has to be big. A thin serif at 60 px and a 120 px line icon in a sea of black read as *empty*, not as calm. The 12 Oct test drew exactly that, so these are the floors:
- **Captions:** page phrase **96–112 px**, scene caption **76–84 px**, gold face caption **76–88 px**, all EB Garamond **600** (not 500). If a phrase doesn't fit at that size, chunk it shorter; never shrink it.
- **The hero drawing:** a single object is **≥ 560 px** wide; a group (the constellation, a row of teammates, a list) spans **≥ 820 px** (x ≈ 130–950); each figure in a group is **≥ 170 px** tall. Lines are **5–7 px** with their glow, never hairlines.
- **The dot / point:** **≥ 72 px** with its halo (the references' 56 px read as a speck on a phone).
- **Black is the frame, not the subject:** the drawing fills the middle **y ≈ 560–1400**; if more than half of that band is empty black for more than a second, the drawing is too small or the scene needs its next element.
- **The face:** full frame, edge to edge, never shrunk over its own blur; a tight selfie stays full screen with the page text moved, not the video shrunk.

### The feel (read this twice)
This is **a philosopher reading his own essay in a dark room at 2 a.m.** Calm, low, certain. The screen is a black page; when he names a thing, it appears as light: a single white line that draws itself into a phone, a hammer, a ladder; a dot that is *you*, moving through every idea; a sun that opens like a thought. When he looks at you, he's alone in the frame, in his own warm colour, lit against the dark, with one line of gold under his chin. Nothing shouts, nothing bounces, nothing is decoration, and yet every frame is alive: light drifts, lines breathe, the dot pulses like a heartbeat. It should feel like **a beautifully designed book that reads itself to you**. If it looks like a hype reel, it's wrong; if it looks empty or static, it's wrong too.

**Who the reference creator is:** a calm, low-voiced American essayist who speaks slowly (3.0–3.4 words/s) and deliberately, often with his eyes closed mid-sentence, about escaping the default life: environment, attention, work, self-education, building your own world. He never raises his voice, never jokes, never sells inside the talk. The authority comes from certainty and from restraint: a black page, a few serif words, one image at a time.

**Audience (ICP):** English-speaking self-improvement and one-person-business viewers, roughly 18–35, who feel stuck in a loop (job, scroll, sameness) and want a worldview more than a tutorial. They respond to **a sentence they can screenshot** and an image that makes the idea obvious. They distrust hype; they trust calm.

**Style lineage:** a "book page / title sequence" essay edit, built from three layers:
1. **Voice layer:** one dry, close-miked, compressed voice reading a tight paragraph. It is the spine of every format; the picture serves it. Breaths are trimmed, never chopped into a staccato.
2. **Page layer:** black. A black page with a few white serif words (dk1 open), a black stage for white line icons (dk2), a near-black fog for light (dk3). When the face appears (dk1) it is alone, full frame, locked, with gold serif subtitles on the chest. **Nothing ever shares the screen with the face.**
3. **Metaphor layer:** one literal drawn image per sentence or clause, all greyscale, all glowing, all centred: a dot hunted by megaphones, a nail hammered flat, a phone filling with cards, an eye with a clock for an iris, a lighthouse beam, a light cone choosing a side. One object (a dot, a point of light) carries continuity from scene to scene.
- **This style is not one layout.** The three reels are **three formats** with one temperament: `FMT-PAGE` (black page → face), `FMT-LINE` (line art on black) and `FMT-LIGHT` (light objects + product card), recorded faithfully in §3.1–§3.5.
- **The plugin's users film themselves talking.** §3.6 is a clearly marked **adaptation**, `FMT-BRIDGE`: how the faceless motion-graphic grammar of dk2/dk3 joins the talking head of dk1, using only devices seen in these reels plus this edition's elevations.

## §1 Procedure (follow in order)

1. **Inventory.** Probe the footage. Measure the framing on 3 frames (head top, eye line, chin, face centre x, face width; §12.2). Note what is behind the caption band (y ≈ 1340–1410): a dark top is the house case; a light or busy one needs the §5.3 shadow upgrade.
2. **Transcribe** with word timestamps; check names and numbers against the script.
3. **Choose the format** (§3.0), one line why. With the creator's footage the default is `FMT-BRIDGE`; `FMT-PAGE` for pure opinion; `FMT-LINE` / `FMT-LIGHT` only if the creator asks for a faceless reel.
4. **Segment into sentences and clauses.** Mark the **thesis** (the black page), every **concrete image** word with its onset ("programmed", "scrolling", "zoom out", "40 hours"), the **"you" object**, the **turn** ("or / but / instead") and the **callback**.
5. **Plan the speaker map:** PAGE, FACE or SCENE per sentence (§3.6.3).
6. **Pick the scene grammar** and **one continuity object** (§3.6.4).
7. **Ideate 3 hooks** (§6) and recommend one.
8. **Fill the beat sheet** (§13): one beat per sentence (line) or clause (light), with its hero, its continuity path and its sounds.
8b. **The 10× pass** (§8.0): for every scene, how the line draws itself or the light grows, what it does while held, how the dot reacts and where it goes; where the constellation (EV-1) is built. Then **attack the plan**: where is a frame empty or still? Where does something appear from nothing? Where is a caption small or low? Where is it loud or busy? Fix it in the plan.
8c. **Score it** (§11), quietly, logged in the sound ledger.
9. **Plan the transition map** (§9.3) against the budget (§9.4).
10. **Assets** (§8.5): everything is drawn; the only image is the creator's **own** product art for an end card.
11. **Show the creator** the plan and stills (§13.3).
12. **Build, look at your frames (§15), score, render (§16).**

## §2 Craft that never bends
- **The whole face is always visible:** hair to chin and both ears inside, ≥ 150 px above the hair, alone in the frame, never zoomed (M2); the footage always fills the frame (a tight selfie stays full screen; the hair may touch the top edge, eyes to chin always in), never shrunk over its own blurred frame.
- **Text never sits on text:** a caption never over a drawn word, the object or the product cover.
- **Sound in sync:** every SFX on the frame its picture lands, never on a consonant (slide ±2 f); nothing ever louder than the voice.
- **True and exact:** facts and spelling from the script; the product card only for the creator's own product.
- **Readable at phone size:** captions ≥ 76 px (page ≥ 96 px), weight 600; in-world text ≥ 48 px inside x 70–970.
- **Big, few things:** the hero drawing ≥ 560 px wide (a group ≥ 820 px), the dot ≥ 72 px, lines 5–7 px. Minimal means few, not small.
- **Loudness:** −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP; end on a complete sentence.

Everything else is direction. You decide; write your reasons into the plan.

### 2.1 The house grammar (what makes it this style)
Not checks: this is what the three reference reels do every time, and why it works. Break one only with a reason in the plan. (The IDs are kept because other sections cite them.)
- M1. **One format per reel** (§3.0); in `FMT-BRIDGE`, one scene grammar (line *or* light).
- M2. **The whole face is always visible:** hair to chin, both ears inside, in every shot. dk1 keeps ≈ 240–252 px above the head; ours keeps **≥ 150 px**. **No punch-ins** (dk1: identical head scale across every cut). Tighter footage stays **full frame as shot** (the hair may touch the top edge; eyes to chin always in), never shrunk over a blurred fill of its own frame and never punched into the head (§12.2). **Captions may cross the face**; nothing else does.
- M3. **The face is alone on screen:** its only overlay is the caption (dk1: 0 graphics over the face in 32.9 s). Page, scenes and face **alternate** full screen; they never share the frame.
- M4. **One locked framing**, jump cuts only in breath gaps (dk1: 5 of 6 cuts in pauses, the 6th at a comma inside a list). A continuous thought may run 10 s unbroken (dk1 18.75–29.38 s).
- M5. **One picture per sentence or clause, landing 0–9 f before its noun** (dk2, dk3).
- M6. **One continuity object** (dk2's ≈ 56 px dot, dk3's point of light) is "you" and survives every scene change: it morphs into the next object, or the scene collapses into it and the next grows out of it.
- M7. **Monochrome scenes:** saturation 0 (dk2, dk3, every sampled frame). Hierarchy is luminance and glow.
- M8. **The only added colour is the gold caption** `#FFCB41` on footage (dk1). The footage keeps its own colour: **never desaturate, wash out or lift the blacks** (Naman, 12 Oct: "dont make the videos pale"). Natural saturation, deep blacks, contrast +5–10% so the face pops against the black scenes (§4.3).
- M9. **A held scene always moves** (dk2's longest, 9.6 s, fills, resets and grows; dk3 drifts and spins constantly).
- M10. **Captions in sync:** readable **by the first word's onset** (fix: dk3 lagged up to 0.5 s: "Every day" spoken 9.01, readable ≈ 9.5). Hard swaps on the onset frame (dk1 f34).
- M11. **Calm pace:** 3.0–3.4 words/s; pauses kept ≤ 0.4 s (dk1 0.40, dk2 0.52, dk3 0.32); never speed the voice up.
- M12. **Audio:** −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP (fixes: dk1 −18.6 and dk2 −18.9 LUFS too quiet; dk3 TP −0.5 too hot).
- M13. **End on a complete sentence:** hard stop ≤ 6 f after the last word, or a flicker-off ≤ 8 f (fix: dk2 cut the voice mid-sentence).
- M14. **In-world text ≥ 44 px, inside x 70–970** (fix: dk2's "WAKE UP" ≈ 24–28 px; its level digit at x ≈ 1000).
- M15. **End-card headline top ≥ y 230** (fix: dk3's at y ≈ 155–290 sat under Instagram's top bar).
- M16. **The product card is the creator's own product only** (dk3: his own book): their real art, supplied by them; the headline in their words (default "NOW AVAILABLE · LINK IN BIO"). No own product, no card.
- M17. **Facts and spelling** from the script.

**Never in this style:**
- N1. A PiP, split, stacked box, card, circle or cut-out of the speaker. No reel has a divided layout; **never invent one**.
- N2. Emoji, coloured or bold keywords, all-caps "social" captions, pop/bounce caption animations (the book-page tone breaks). A soft per-word ink-in (EV-3) is the only caption motion.
- N3. Glitches, risers, comedy or game sounds, anything sharp or loud (the references used no SFX at all; this edition's whisper score in §11 is the only sound).
- N4. Colour in a scene, colour flashes, light leaks, RGB splits, lens-flare overlays.
- N5. Punch-ins, Ken Burns or shake on the face (dk1: zero digital zoom).
- N6. Stock footage, photos, screenshots, real UIs or brand logos in scenes; generic "AI" imagery. Everything is drawn.
- N7. Any product, store brand, app or tool that isn't the creator's own, anywhere.
- N8. A hard cut between two drawn scenes (dk2: scene score never above 0.11).
- N9. The thin serif over a bright or busy area without its dark shadow (dk1 watch-out).
- N10. Text over text: a subtitle over a drawn word, the object or the product cover.
- N11. Two scene grammars in one reel, or a drawn scene over the face.

### 3.2 `FMT-PAGE`: black page → face (dk1)
- **0–3…6 s, L-1:** the thesis sentence plays as voice over pure black; 2–4-word white serif phrases swap on the first word of each phrase (`CAP-PAGE`, §5.2). The face is withheld.
- **Reveal, T-01:** a hard cut to the face (L-2) inside the breath pause before the **next** sentence (dk1: pause 4.98–5.18 s, cut at f122 = 5.083 s), so the face arrives on that sentence's first word ("You just change everything overnight").
- **Body, L-2:** one locked framing to the end, gold serif subtitles (`CAP-GOLD`), jump cuts (T-02) only in breath gaps. Shot lengths 3.6–10.6 s (dk1 median 5.0 s). Nothing else appears.
- **Ending:** a hard stop on the face on a line that calls back the hook (E-1).
- The "motion" of this format is the creator's hands gesturing into the bottom third: frame so they stay in shot (dk1: fingertips cross the bottom edge; they rise into the caption band at 8–10 s and the caption stays on top of them, legible through its shadow).

### 3.3 `FMT-LINE`: line art on black (dk2)
- **Every frame is L-3:** pure `#000000`, white 3–4 px line icons with a 10–20 px soft white glow, grey `#5A5A5A`–`#6E6E6E` secondary fills. The scene is centred on (540, 960) and stays inside the middle ≈ 45% of the height (y 553–1399): the top ≈ 550 px and bottom ≈ 520 px are always black.
- **The dot is the protagonist:** a white disc (dk2 ≈ 56 px; ≥ 72 px here) with glow at (539, 960). It is the pac-man's pellet, the target of the megaphones, the nail head, the ball bouncing in the phone, the glowing cluster on the grid, the ball under the screen.
- **Scene changes** on the first word of a new sentence: power-off/power-on flickers (T-03/T-04), a black gap with a ghost (T-05), dot-continuity morphs (T-07), shape-growth morphs (T-08), a CRT squeeze (T-06). 9 changes in 50 s.
- **No subtitles.** Text only as a designed title (the hook) or as in-world UI (a lock-screen clock, a notification, a level digit).
- **Ending:** a flicker-off to black (E-2), on a complete sentence (fix).

### 3.4 `FMT-LIGHT`: light objects + product card (dk3)
- **L-4 for the voice-over:** a near-black canvas with a heavy vignette (corners `#0D0D0D`) and fog `#1A1A1A`–`#2A2A2A` around one luminous greyscale object at (540, ≈ 960); beams, cones, lattices and flares may run full-bleed. A small white subtitle line sits at y 1519–1565, **under** the object, never on it.
- **One metaphor per clause, morphing continuously:** ≈ 11 scenes in 21 s (one every ≈ 1.9 s). Changes are light bursts, pushes through an object, collapses to a point, zoom-out morphs, a 1-frame drop (T-09…T-13).
- **The point of light** is the continuity: a scene ends by collapsing into it; the next grows out of it.
- **After the voice:** the last scene collapses to the point → 1 black frame → the product card warps in (T-14) on the reveal swell (§11), the headline scrambles in (≈ 30 f), and the card holds to the end (E-3). Total ≈ 4 s.

### 3.6 ADAPTATION (not one of the reference reels): `FMT-BRIDGE`, the motion-graphic grammar over the creator's own talking head

> **Read this as an adaptation, not an observation.** None of the three reels joins a talking head to drawn scenes. This section joins them because the plugin's users film themselves. It uses **only devices observed in these reels**: the black voice-over page (dk1), the speaker full frame with gold serif captions (dk1), the full-screen line-art or light-point scenes playing while the voice continues (dk2, dk3), and the one object that carries continuity (dk2's dot, dk3's point). Every measurement in it is borrowed from the reel that owns the device.

#### 3.6.1 The three states (and nothing else)
| State | Layout | From | Face |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PAGE** | L-1 black page, white serif phrases, the creator's own voice | dk1 0–5.08 s | **Absent** |
| **FACE** | L-2 the creator full frame, locked, gold serif caption on the chest | dk1 5.08–38.0 s | **Full frame, alone, whole:** hair to chin and both ears inside, head top ≥ 150 px (house 200–260), no zoom |
| **SCENE** | L-3 (line grammar) or L-4 (light grammar), full screen, the voice continuing | dk2 / dk3 | **Absent.** Never a PiP of the face over a scene, never a scene in a box over the face |

#### 3.6.2 The shape of a `FMT-BRIDGE` reel
1. **PAGE (≤ 2.5 s; the references ran 3–6 s):** the thesis's first clause over black, set large (`CAP-PAGE` 96–112 px) with the **point of light already breathing** at the centre beneath it. Frame 0 already has the first phrase on it (dk1 f0 "the fastest way"); never an empty black frame. If the thesis runs longer, the face arrives on its second sentence and the rest of the thesis plays on the face.
2. **FACE:** hard cut (T-01) in the pause before the next sentence; the face lands on its first word.
3. **SCENE ↔ FACE alternation** through the body: a scene for each metaphor, list or system; the face for each claim, feeling or turn. The continuity object waits in the scene world: when a scene returns, the dot/point is where the last scene left it (dk2: the dot "remains from the previous scene").
4. **FACE for the payoff and the callback;** hard stop (E-1).
5. **Optional EC-1** only if the creator has their own product: the last scene (or a black beat) collapses to the point → warp-in card. Never another brand's product.

#### 3.6.3 Speaker map rules
| Sentence type | State | Why (observed behaviour it borrows) |
|---|---|---|
| The thesis / hook sentence | PAGE | dk1: the thesis as a read-along page, face withheld |
| A direct claim to "you", a feeling, a warning ("it's going to be uncomfortable") | FACE | dk1: eye contact carries the weight |
| A metaphor, a system, a process ("programmed by parents, teachers and society") | SCENE | dk2: one literal icon set per sentence |
| A concrete list of things ("the places you go, the accounts you follow…") | SCENE (one item lit per noun) or FACE (list in captions only, as dk1 did) | dk3 light cone lighting icons one by one; dk1 kept lists on the face |
| The turn ("or you can…", "but if you…", "do that and ignore…") | FACE, or SCENE if the turn is a choice that can be drawn (the cone swinging) | dk3 light cone swing on "ignore anyone" |
| The payoff and the last line | FACE | dk1 hard stop on the callback |

- **Share (default):** FACE 45–60% of the runtime, SCENE 30–45%, PAGE ≤ 6%. A scene lasts 2–8 s (dk3 1.9 s average, dk2 5.6 s average); a face beat 2–8 s.
- **Never two state changes within 1.5 s.** A face beat shorter than ~2 s reads as a flash; fold it into the scene.
- **Calm pace, alive frames.** Each FACE or SCENE beat holds **≥ 2.5 s** (usually 3–6 s); **at most 3 face ↔ scene crossings in any 20 s**, the opening included. Retention comes from what happens *inside* a beat (the drawing growing, the dot travelling, a line writing on the word), not from cutting back and forth. Several sentences in a row about one idea stay in **one** scene that evolves; the face comes back for the claim, the feeling or the turn. (The 12 Oct test crossed five times in 20 s; it read busy for this style.)
- **The dot is never alone and idle.** A lone dot on black holds **≤ 0.5 s**. Whenever it's on screen it is doing something big on the word: a ring blooming out of it, a trail it draws, seats or stars appearing around it, splitting, travelling to the next thing. Before a split or a reveal, build the tension *on* it (it swells, pulses faster, a ring tightens) rather than leaving one small dot in the dark.

#### 3.6.4 Scene grammar: pick one per reel
| Grammar | Pick when | Continuity object | Scene entrance / exit | Caption in scenes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Line** (dk2) | The script is a system, a game, a routine, a list of everyday objects; 40–60 s | The white dot, ≥ 72 px with glow (dk2 ≈ 56 px) | Entrance T-03 power-on flicker; exit T-04 power-off; scene → scene T-07 dot morph or T-05 black gap + ghost | `CAP-PAGE` white serif at the scene subtitle line (y ≈ 1500–1565) |
| **Light** (dk3) | The script is about time, attention, seeing, choosing, becoming; 20–40 s | The point of light, ≈ 15 px core + wide halo | Entrance: grows out of the point or T-09 light burst; exit T-11 collapse to a point; scene → scene T-10 / T-11 / T-12 | `CAP-PAGE` white serif at y ≈ 1500–1565 |

**Caption colour rule for the bridge** (extends dk1's own rule): white serif on black (PAGE, SCENE), gold serif on footage (FACE). One typeface for the whole reel. In scenes the caption moves to the bottom line (dk3's subtitle position) because the centre belongs to the drawing.

#### 3.6.5 Crossing between FACE and SCENE
| From → to | Recipe | Borrowed from |
|---|---|---|
| PAGE → FACE | T-01 hard cut in the breath pause; face on the new sentence's first word | dk1 f121→f122 |
| FACE → SCENE | Hard cut in the breath pause to black, then the scene's own entrance: line grammar = T-03 power-on (10 f, lands on the stressed noun); light grammar = the point appears and the object grows out of it, or T-09 burst on the first strong noun | dk1 cut placement + dk2 R1 / dk3 M1, M4 |
| SCENE → FACE | Line: T-04 power-off (≈ 9 f) to black, hard cut to the face on the sentence start. Light: T-11 collapse to a point (≈ 18–21 f), the point holds 2–4 f, hard cut to the face | dk2 R2 + dk1 cut; dk3 M4 + dk1 cut |
| SCENE → SCENE | Line: T-07 dot morph, T-05 black gap + ghost, T-06 CRT squeeze, T-08 shape growth. Light: T-10 push, T-11 collapse, T-12 zoom-out, T-09 burst | dk2, dk3 |
| FACE → FACE | T-02 jump cut in a breath gap, same framing | dk1 |

- **Never** dissolve the face into a scene, flicker the face itself, or burst light over the face: the face states only cut.
- The **voice never stops** across a crossing: the cut sits in a pause of ≤ 0.4 s.

#### 3.6.6 The face rule in the bridge
- FACE is always full frame and whole: hair to chin, ears inside, ≥ 150 px above the hair (house 200–260 px, measured on dk1 at 240–252).
- No zoom of any size on the face (dk1). How the face sits is decided by the face: if the footage is tighter than the rule, keep it full frame as shot and move the caption, never shrink it over its own blurred, darkened frame (§12.2). Never punch in to "fill" the frame.

### 4.2 Meaning of colour
- **Black** = the page: silence, focus, "listen".
- **White** = what matters right now (the word, the object, "you").
- **Grey** = the background world: other people's voices, notifications, the crowd, things not chosen.
- **Glow / bloom** = importance and life. The brightest thing on screen is the idea; the dot glows because it is "you".
- **Gold** = the speaker's own words over his own face. Warm against a cool room.
- **No other hue exists.** A red X, a green tick, a blue link: none of them appear. The X marks on the critics (dk3) are white.

### 4.3 Footage look (don't fight it)
- dk1's room: a plain teal-grey wall `#465559`–`#516064`, two vertical LED tubes (left x ≈ 60–130, right x ≈ 975–1020, highlight `#EDFAFE`) softly out of focus, a soft frontal key with a slight sheen, warm skin (`#E3AEAC` on the cheek), a black crew-neck tee `#161F24` with lifted blue-black. A calm, cinematic "studio at home".
- **The references kept the footage as shot, and so do we, only richer.** Never pale: no desaturation, no lifted or milky blacks, no faded look. Natural saturation (up to +10%), contrast +5–10%, blacks crushed to true black, skin warm; no LUT, no teal push.
- **Wardrobe matters for this style:** a dark, plain top gives the thin serif its page. If the creator wears a light or patterned top, use the §5.3 shadow upgrade and move the caption to the darkest band (it may cross the chin or face).

## §6 Hook system

### 6.0 The 10× hook
- **Frame 0 is a full thought, not an empty page:** the first phrase large on black, the point of light already breathing under it (a 1.00 → 1.08 pulse every ~1 s, like a heartbeat), and a faint first line already drawing from it.
- **The face by ~2.5 s,** on the next sentence's first word, in full warm colour, gold caption under the chin.
- **The first scene grows out of the point** (never a cold cut), so the opening reads as one continuous gesture: word → light → face → light.
- **Sound from frame 0, at a whisper:** an airy breath under the first phrase (`air-low-woosh`), a glassy shimmer as the point blooms (`musicfx-ima02-24-1`, low).

### 6.1 What the hook must do in about 3 seconds
1. **Say one sentence that sounds like a law** (a superlative, a worldview, an identity claim), in the calm voice, from frame 0 (dk1 "The" at 0.00 s, dk2 "Life" at 0.10 s, dk3 "You" at 0.00 s).
2. **Give the eye one thing to read or one thing to watch**, never both competing: a white serif phrase on black (dk1), a designed title around one iconic image (dk2), one object transforming (dk3).
3. **Withhold something:** the face (dk1, 5 s), the way out (dk2: "programmed by…" implies an escape), the answer (dk3: the trap first, the instruction at 9 s, the product at the end).

**Visual changes in the first 3 s are few, by design:** dk1 3 (phrase swaps), dk2 6, dk3 7 (+3 subtitle swaps). The hook stops the thumb with **contrast** (black, one word, one light), not with speed. Never pad the hook with extra events to raise the count.

### 6.2 Hook formulas (`HF`)
| ID | Formula | Pattern | Seen in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **HF-1** | **Superlative + violent prescription** | "The fastest way to [want] is to [unexpected hard verb] out of [comfort]." | dk1 "The fastest way to change your life is to rip yourself out of your physical and digital environment." |
| **HF-2** | **Worldview in four words + designed promise** | Voice: "[Life] is a [metaphor]." Title: "TURN YOUR [LIFE] INTO A [METAPHOR]" | dk2 "Life is a game." + "TURN YOUR LIFE / INTO A VIDEO GAME" |
| **HF-3** | **Identity / anti-norm with a number and a pain** | "You weren't born to [norm with a number] doing something you [pain]." | dk3 "You weren't born to work 40 hours a week doing something you hate." |

Add-ons that stack with any formula:
- **"Overnight" promise:** the line after the hook raises the stakes in time ("You just change everything overnight", dk1).
- **Villain named early:** "your mind is programmed by your parents, teachers and society" (dk2, at 1.5 s).
- **The trap before the way out:** describe the loop ("numb your mind with endless scrolling and wake up one day…") before any instruction (dk3).

Write **3 variants** per reel with different formulas and recommend one.

### 7.2 The scene ritual (one recipe per scene, identical every time)
1. **Entrance** 0–0.3 s before the noun: flicker-on (line), growth from the point or a burst (light), or a morph from the last scene.
2. **The image lands whole** by the noun's onset: the main object at 100% (it may still be drawing its details).
3. **It acts** for the rest of the sentence: waves pulse, the hammer strikes every 1–1.5 s, cards stack every ≈ 0.3 s, the bar fills and resets every 0.5–1 s, the beam rotates. **Never a still.**
4. **The continuity object reacts:** it swells (programming), sinks (conformity), bounces (distraction), collects (growth), rises (insight).
5. **Exit** on the next sentence's first word: flicker-off, collapse into the point, or a morph that turns this scene's shape into the next.

### 7.3 The face ritual (`FMT-PAGE`, `FMT-BRIDGE`)
1. Hard cut in a breath gap; the face lands on a sentence start.
2. Gold caption phrases swap on onsets; nothing else appears.
3. Jump cuts only in breath gaps; a continuous thought runs unbroken (up to ≈ 10 s).
4. Leave on a sentence end, in a pause.

### 7.4 Open loops (retention)
- **The withheld face** (dk1): 3–6 s of black page; the reveal is the reward.
- **The implied escape** (dk2): name the villain ("programmed") and the label ("You are an NPC unless…") so the viewer waits for the way out.
- **The trap first** (dk3): describe the loop before the instruction; the instruction lands at ≈ 35–40% of the runtime (dk3 9.0 s of 20 s).
- **The unspoken answer** (dk3): the product card answers "how?" without a word.
- **Fix:** dk2's unresolved last sentence is not a loop device for us. End on a complete sentence (M13); let the callback (SD-4) make the loop.

## §8 Graphics system

### 8.0 The 10× layer (what the best motion designer adds to a quiet style)
**Animation principles, applied to light and line:**
| Principle | How it shows up here |
|---|---|
| **Anticipation** | Before a scene appears, the dot or point dims for 3 f, then flares as the first line leaves it; before a morph, the object holds perfectly still for 4 f |
| **Squash & stretch (subtle)** | The dot stretches ~8% along its path when it travels and settles with a 1-frame squash; light objects never deform |
| **Follow-through & overlap** | Lines draw with overlapping strokes (the next starts before the last finishes, 3–5 f overlap); the glow catches up 2 f after the stroke's tip; a scene's secondary elements settle 4–6 f after the hero |
| **Arcs** | The dot travels on arcs, never straight; orbiting objects and cones sweep on circular paths |
| **Slow in / slow out** | Ease-in-out on every draw and move; light grows and dies with long exponential tails |
| **Staging** | One lit thing at a time: everything not named sits at 30–40% brightness; the named object at 100% with bloom |
| **Secondary action** | While held: haze drifts in two layers at different speeds, dust motes float through beams, the hero pulses like a heartbeat (±4% every ~1 s), lines shimmer at their tips |
| **Timing** | Line scenes slow and deliberate (a long stroke ≈ 1 s); light scenes quicker (clause by clause); the face beats as long as the thought |
| **Exaggeration** | One moment per reel blooms big: the turn or the payoff (the sun spiral, the full constellation, the cone swinging) |
| **Appeal** | Pure blacks, clean whites, soft bloom, fine grain only in light scenes; everything looks like it was drawn with light |

**Design hierarchy (every frame):** 1. the one lit object (or his face), **big** (≥ 560 px; a group ≥ 820 px) · 2. the caption (gold on the face, white serif in scenes) · 3. the dot/point, if it isn't the hero · 4. everything else, dim. Nothing competes with the lit thing.

**Effects toolkit (light only):** bloom with clipped whites; two-layer volumetric haze with parallax; god-rays from the point; a stroke's bright leading tip; fine grain in light scenes; a soft vignette in light scenes; the trail of the dot (EV-1). Never: colour, flares from an overlay pack, RGB splits, glitches, shake.

- **Line scenes:** pure `#000000`, white lines with a soft glow; no grain, no vignette (dk2).
- **Light scenes:** heavy vignette, bloom with clipped whites, volumetric haze, fine grain/noise in sand and fog; filmic and dreamy (dk3).
- **Everything in scenes is greyscale,** but bright: whites hit true white with a real glow, blacks are true black. Grey, dim or foggy-flat scenes read as pale; verify saturation 0 *and* a white peak on stills.
- Spell-check every on-screen word against the script.

## §11 Sound (D4)

**No music bed, ever.** Sound is SFX only (the creator's direction, 12 Oct 2026): never a music track, bed, drone or loop under the voice.

### 11.0 What the references do (measured)
| Format | Under the voice in the reference (we don't use it) | SFX | Loudness measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| `FMT-PAGE` (dk1) | None (gap floor −36 to −43 dB under speech RMS; hiss and room tone only) | None | −18.6 LUFS, LRA 4.2, TP −2.5 dBFS |
| `FMT-LINE` (dk2) | A steady piano/pad bed | None (no chomp, no hammer hit, no flicker sound) | −18.9 LUFS, LRA 2.7, TP −4.0 dBFS |
| `FMT-LIGHT` (dk3) | A bass-heavy cinematic bed with bass pulses at the burst (0.73 s) and the lighthouse rise (≈ 2.6 s) and a swell on the card | None separate | −14.0 LUFS, LRA 2.6, TP −0.5 dBFS |

**The voice:** close-miked, warm, dry, no reverb, compressed (LRA 2.6–4.2 LU). It is the loudest and clearest thing in every format.

This edition adds **a whisper score of air and light**: sparse, soft and low, tied to the drawing's events. The viewer should feel the room breathe, never hear "effects".

### 11.1 The palette (air, light, weight)
| Role | When | Pool (ids from SOUNDS.md; rotate) | Level vs voice |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Breath / crossing** | PAGE → FACE, FACE ↔ SCENE crossings, the page returning | `air-low-woosh`, `light-woosh-7119`, `swoosh-2025-01-08-03-02-26-utc-swoosh-02`, `transitionaleleme-ec09-64-3` | −24…−30 dB |
| **Light** | A light object blooms, a scene grows from the point, the sun spiral | `musicfx-ima02-24-1`, `ui-sparkle`, `appear-magic` (trimmed), `game-key-collect-2025-02-11-03-32-33-utc-game-key-collect-2` | −24…−30 dB |
| **Line** | A long self-drawing stroke, a flicker-on | `light-woosh-7119` (trimmed), `air-low-woosh` (trimmed) | −28…−32 dB |
| **The dot collects** | Orbs collected, a level up, an item lit in the cone (list cue) | `0552611` → `0552621` → `0552631`, `selection-click` | −26…−30 dB |
| **In-world UI** | The phone's notifications, the XP bar | `digital-text-2026-05-18-01-34-24-utc-digital-text-02` (very low) | −30 dB |
| **Weight** (≤ 2 per reel) | A light burst (T-09), the payoff / the constellation | `abstract-hit-sub-bass-07`, `cinematic-hits-2025-02-19-18-27-16-utc-cinematic-hit-5` | −16…−20 dB |
| **Product reveal** | T-14 warp-in (own product only) | `impactfadeinout-tfx2697` | ≈ −6 dB under the voice, never above |

**Never in this style:** whooshes that rush, clicks that snap, glitches, risers, game or comedy sounds, anything as loud as the voice.

### 11.2 The SFX ledger (Naman's rule, unchanged)
- **Any file at most 2× in a reel,** except the one **list cue** (here the 0552611/21/31 set as the dot collects).
- **No file on two consecutive cues;** rotate within each pool.
- **Density:** the sparsest of all styles: about one cue every 2–3 s, each tied to a drawing event or a crossing; face-to-face jump cuts get nothing.
- **Never more than two cues overlapping;** never on a consonant.

### 11.3 Silence
The black page and the face are allowed to be silent; **go fully silent for 0.5–1 s before the payoff**, then the constellation's weight (or nothing).

### 11.4 Mix
Voice high-pass 80 Hz, de-ess, light compression; **−14 LUFS, TP ≤ −1 dBTP** (fixes: dk1 −18.6 and dk2 −18.9 LUFS too quiet; dk3 TP −0.5 too hot). The voice is always the loudest thing by a wide margin.

## §12 Footage handling

### 12.1 Shoot setups (identify which the footage uses)
- **A: Locked medium close-up** (house, dk1): tripod, frontal, eye level, ≈ 50–85 mm feel, mid-chest up, plain wall with soft practicals, dark plain top, hands gesturing into the bottom of the frame.
- **B: Selfie** (gently stabilised), **C: tight** (full frame as shot, 12.2), **D: wide** (as shot if the face is ≥ ≈ 300 px wide; never crop past 1.2×). The face rule is the same for all.

### 12.2 Crop table (starting values)
| Situation | Placement |
|---|---|
| FACE, house (dk1 measured) | Full frame; head top y **200–260** (dk1 240–252), eye line ≈ **640**, chin ≈ **1000**, face centre x **480–560** (dk1 494–530), face width ≈ **430–520 px** (dk1 ≈ 490); caption band y 1346–1402 on the chest |

## §15 Look at your frames
Not a checklist; the questions the best editor asks while scrubbing:
- **Frame 0, sound off:** is there a thought on screen (large words, a living point), not an empty black page? Does the face arrive by ~2.5 s?
- **Their video and every picture:** full screen, or in a designed box it fills on the style's own ground? Never a sharp rectangle (footage, recording, landscape clip, photo) over its own blur, never blurred side bars, never a card on their blurred frame?
- **Every scene:** did it grow from the dot or the point, draw itself with a hand's rhythm, and keep breathing while held? Is there one lit thing? Is there any second where only a small dot sits on black? Fill it.
- **The pace:** count the face ↔ scene crossings in each 20 s: three or fewer, each beat ≥ 2.5 s? Calm, but never idle.
- **Every face frame:** alone, whole (hair, chin, ears), unzoomed, in full rich colour (never pale), with the gold line just under the chin?
- **The captions:** can I read them on a phone at arm's length? Do they ink in on the word?
- **Scale:** shrink the still to thumbnail size. Is the drawing still the first thing I see, or is it a speck in black? Is the face card wide, not a pillar?
- **The dot:** can I follow it the whole reel? Does the constellation pay it off?
- **Sound:** a whisper, on the drawing's events, never louder than the voice, no file overused, no music?
- **Vibe:** calm, beautiful, certain. Anything loud, colourful or busy comes out.

## §16 Build + render
- **Pipeline:** the plugin's edit skill: plan in the edit brief, draw every frame in `reel.js` (`frame(t)`), look at stills, score (`plan/sounds.json`), then storyboard and render.
- **Fonts (bundled):** EB Garamond (captions, page), Poppins (light subtitles, end-card headline, in-world clock/digits), Montserrat (arcade title), JetBrains Mono or Silkscreen (in-world notification word).
- **Helpers you'll want** (pure functions of t): `Page(phrases)`; `Face(t)` (jump-cut list, full frame edge to edge, never a blurred fill); `CapGold`, `CapPage(y)`, `CapWipe`; `Glow(drawFn)`; `Dot(state)`; `Flicker(table, t0)` (T-03…T-06); `Collapse`, `Burst`, `PushThrough`, `ZoomOutMorph` (T-09…T-12); one helper per pattern P-02…P-19; `ProductCard(img, headline)` (EC-1); `Vignette`, `Haze(layers)`, `Grain`; `Constellation(trail)` (EV-1), `SelfDraw(path, t0)` (EV-2), `InkIn(words)` (EV-3), `Heartbeat` (EV-4), `RichGrade(face)` (contrast and true blacks, never desaturate).

- **Final:** 1080×1920, H.264, AAC, −14 LUFS, TP ≤ −1 dBTP; check the frame count, saturation 0 on scene stills, and a contact sheet of the final file.
