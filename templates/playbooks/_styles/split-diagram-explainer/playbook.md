# Split Diagram Explainer Playbook (v2, "Living Diagram" edition, for Claude Opus 5.5)

> **In this plugin:** this file is the whole playbook: every measured value and recipe from the 3 reference reels is in it, and there is no other playbook file to open. Frame counts here are at **30 fps** (the references were 24 fps; their measured counts are converted). Positions are on 1080×1920 (the references were 720×1280, scaled ×1.5). The per-reel notes it cites (`kw1…kw3`) are not shipped. Sounds are picked by id from SOUNDS.md; commands are in the edit skill's REFERENCE.md. The page gives you `FACE(n)` and `FACE0` (with `head_top` and `chin`) for framing the panel and every full-frame beat.

**Purpose:** you (Claude) receive a talking-head video of the creator plus its script. Use this document to **ideate and build** the one diagram that carries the idea, every camera move over it, every title, caption, panel move and sound, then edit the Reel so the viewer **watches the idea being drawn while it is said**, and leaves able to redraw it.

**What v2 is:** a frame-by-frame analysis of 3 Kallaway reels (kw1 "the modern story arc", kw2 "the dopamine addiction loop", kw3 "turn Claude into a social media machine"), elevated around the creator's standing directions. Every measured layout, recipe and rule from those reels is kept here; where a reference reel and a direction disagree, **the directions win**.

**Measured sizes are floors, not targets.** The diagram owns the canvas: when a part is named, the camera brings it close and big; a label that can't be read means the camera should be nearer, never the text smaller. Empty canvas for more than a second is unfinished.
- **Full screen or a box, never any footage or picture over its own blur** (Studio Standard): the creator's video, a screen recording, a landscape clip, a photo or a diagram either fills the whole frame edge to edge (cover-crop to the part that matters, or a push-in), or sits in a designed box (rounded, rimmed or softly shadowed, on this style's own ground, colour, gradient, texture, grid or drawn set) that it fills completely. Never shrink anything inside the frame over a blurred or darkened copy of itself, never pad a box, band or panel with blurred side bars, never set a card on the creator's own blurred frame. A tight selfie stays full screen as shot: move the text, not the video (eyes to chin always in; the hair may touch the top edge). When the whole head must show, use a box sized so the footage fills it with the head inside. (A matte cut-out over a plate is fine: it has no box edge.)

### The feel (read this twice)
This is the edit of **a strategist at a whiteboard in a dark room**: he talks fast and sure, and behind him a single diagram comes alive exactly as he speaks. A line writes itself across the black, a seat lights up, the camera glides to the next part of the model, a label snaps in on the word. It feels **intelligent and premium**: neon on black, one bold grotesk with an italic serif keyword, a soft dark beat underneath. It is never busy: one idea on the canvas at a time, everything else waiting in the dark. The viewer should feel they're getting a framework from someone who has tested it, and the final pulled-back diagram should be worth a screenshot.

### Who this is, and who it's for
- **The reference creator:** a confident, fast, articulate English-speaking content strategist (3.9–4.1 words/s) who teaches creators and marketers frameworks: story arcs, hooks, retention, AI workflows. He sells ideas with diagrams and proof, not with jokes.
- **The audience:** creators, marketers, founders and operators who want a framework they can use today. Smart and impatient: the model in one picture, the proof in the next, a resource at the end.
- **The lineage:** a presenter in front of a whiteboard, in three layers.
  1. **Canvas:** the top ~73% of the frame, dark neon dot grid (frameworks, psychology) or light graph paper (product, UI, proof); one diagram drawn, labelled and re-coloured as he speaks, with a virtual camera moving over it.
  2. **Presenter:** a rounded 16:9 panel at the bottom with him chest-up, alternating with full-frame face beats for asides, turns and punchlines.
  3. **Type:** one-word captions; titles pairing a bold grotesk with an italic serif keyword or a red keyword.
- **The biggest fix:** in all three references his matted head and hands break 75–120 px out of the top of the panel over the canvas. **Ours never does:** the head sits fully inside the panel with clear room above it.

## §1 Procedure
0. **Know the footage and the words.** One locked camera is enough: the panel and the full-frame face are two crops of one take (kw3); with two angles, the wide feeds the panel and the tight the face (kw1, kw2). Transcribe with word timestamps (captions are the spoken words, one at a time). Segment: `HOOK` (0–4 s), `NAME` (the framework + its size), `STEP-n`, `PROOF`, `PAYOFF`, `CTA`. Pick the canvas per section (§ The two canvases).
1. **Watch and listen to the whole cut.** Mark the claim, the framework's name and size ("five tools", "four steps"), each part of it, every aside, the turn and the CTA.
2. **Design the one diagram** (§8.1): the picture of the whole framework when complete (a team chart, a loop, an arc, a pipeline, a before/after). Draw its final state first; it is the last frame's screenshot.
3. **Break it into camera positions:** each part of the framework is a place on the diagram the camera travels to (§9.1). Write the camera path.
4. **Plan the speaker map** (§3): which lines are full-frame face (claim, asides, turns, CTA lead-in), which are panel under the diagram.
5. **List every term with its onset;** each one adds or lights a part of the diagram on the word.
6. **Write 3 hooks, second by second** (§6), each with frame 0, the first 3 s of events and the moment the framework is named; keep the one that promises most.
7. **Score it** (§11) with the sound ledger.
8. **Attack the plan:** where is the canvas showing something unrelated to the diagram? Where does a label arrive before the camera gets there? Where is a part too small to read? Where is it busy? Where is his face cut?
9. **Draw, look at the frames (§15), fix.**

## §2 Craft that never bends
- **The whole face is always visible.** In the panel, the head sits fully inside with ≥ 40 px air above it, and the panel's crop never cuts the hair or chin; at full frame, every push and punch stops while the whole head (hair to chin) is in frame. For a tight handheld selfie: full frame stays full screen as shot (eyes to chin in; the hair may touch the top edge; move the text, not the video), and in the panel the footage fills the panel, cropped on the face; never zoom out into blurred side bars. Size every crop from `FACE0` (`FACE(n)` when he moves). A caption may cross the face.
- **Text never sits on text;** nothing sits on the caption; graphics stay above the caption gutter (≥ 30 px).
- **Sound in sync:** every SFX on the frame its picture lands, never on a consonant (±2 f).
- **True and exact:** names, numbers, products and claims from the script or real sources; real logos and photos for real things; no invented numbers on labels.
- **Readable at phone size:** labels ≥ 26 px; if a part of the diagram can't be read, the camera goes closer instead of the text getting smaller.
- **Loudness:** −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP (all three references were hot: fix). End on the last word or the pulled-back diagram, never on accidental black.

Everything else is direction. You decide; write your reasons into the plan.

### 2.1 The house grammar (what makes it this style)
Not checks: this is what the three reference reels do every time, and why it works. Break one only with a reason in the plan.
- **Draw the idea as it is said:** every term gets its visual (label, segment, object, card) landing 0–10 f before the word (median ~5 f); big moves complete **on** the word.
- **Layout ping-pong:** split for steps and proof (≈ 67–77% of runtime), the full-frame face for asides and bridges (≈ 16–31%). A camera/layout shot lasts ≤ ~7.5 s (median 2.5–3.4 s); inside a split something changes every 0.5–1.5 s.
- **Something always moves:** held graphics drift or push 1–3% per second; no frame dead-still for more than ~1.3 s.
- **The breaths are cut:** no gap over ~0.45 s; micro jump cuts hide inside push-ins or layout changes.
- **Titles only in split sections, in one band per reel** (kw3's title drifted from y 105 to 265: never); they leave by a hard cut or by riding the camera off frame.
- **Colour by meaning,** consistent for the whole reel (§4).
- **Facts and proof are real:** his own profile counter, his own doc pages, real UI screenshots, real creator reels as examples (credited); every number from the script or the source.
- **Never:** graphics crossing into the panel; a caption over a card's bottom (keep ≥ 30 px); flashes, glitches, white frames, emoji, stickers; coloured or animated captions, or a caption duplicating a label; copyrighted film clips (kw2 used movie footage); more than ~2 annotation types on screen at once (kw2 36–46 s bordered on clutter).

## §3 Layouts, panel moves, framing the face

### 3.1 Layouts
| Layout | What | Share |
|---|---|---|
| **SPLIT** | The canvas on top (the diagram, title band y≈100–400, graphic zone to y≈1250), the caption gutter (y≈1240), the panel at the bottom (x 66–1011, top y≈1402–1408, radius ≈ 40, bleeding off the bottom) | 65–75% |
| **FACE** | Full-frame face, caption at chest height | 20–30% |
| **PROOF** | The light graph-paper canvas for real screenshots, results and the CTA, with the panel | 5–15% |
| **B-ROLL** | Full-frame: a burst of 4–5 micro-shots (1–7 f each) to kick off an analogy (kw2's casino), or a reaction clip that zooms out into a phone mockup on the light canvas (kw1). Licensed or his own footage only | ≤ 7% |

```
┌─────────────────────────┐ 0
│   Title line 1 (grotesk)│ ← title band y≈100–400 (one band per reel)
│   Title *keyword* serif │
│   ONE diagram / object  │ ← graphic zone y≈300–1250, owned by the diagram
│   / UI card             │   (large objects may pass BEHIND the panel)
│          word           │ ← caption gutter, centre y≈1240 (≥ 30 px below any card)
│╭───────────────────────╮│ ← panel top y≈1402–1408, x 66–1011, radius ≈ 40–42 (top corners)
││  him, chest-up,       ││   bleeds off the bottom; no border (1 px light rim at most)
││  head INSIDE the panel││   head top ≥ panel top + 40
└┴───────────────────────┴┘ 1920
```
**Safe zones:** key text inside x 60–1020, y 100–1250 in the split (the panel owns the bottom).

### 3.2 Panel moves (D2)
| ID | Move | Recipe | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PM-1** | **Rise to face** | The panel grows from its rect to the full frame in 10–12 f (expo-out); the canvas and diagram slide up and darken behind it | An aside, a turn, the claim, the CTA lead-in |
| **PM-2** | **Back to the board** | The reverse: the face settles into the panel while the canvas slides back down with the diagram exactly where it was, the camera already moving to the next part | Back to the model |
| **PM-3** | **Hard cut** | A plain cut between the split and the face | When the line is short and punchy (≤ 1.5 s), or for rhythm after two glides |
| **PM-4** | **Push-in on the face** | A slow push (+5–10%) over a full-frame beat; one faster punch (+12–15%, 12 f) on an emphasis word per reel; both stop with the whole head in frame | Keeps full-frame beats alive |

Rhythm: the model in the split, the human in the face. A full-frame beat every 5–10 s. Never two full-frame beats in a row longer than 6 s together.

### 3.3 Framing the face (D5)
- **Panel:** the wide framing (chest-up), head top ≥ panel top + 40, eye line ≈ panel top + 150–190, face centre x 495–545; the presenter is **clipped to the panel** (no cut-out over the canvas); hands may leave at the sides but nothing crosses the panel's top edge. For vertical source footage, take the 16:9 band from above the hair to mid-chest. The footage always fills the panel edge to edge (cover): never letterboxed, never padded with a blurred copy of itself; if the head won't fit, make the panel taller (up to 4:5) or play that beat full frame. (The references sat the eye line only 25–80 px below the edge because the head was allowed out.)
- **Full frame:** an MCU, head top ≈ y 180–270, eye line ≈ 620–645, face centre x ≈ 515–555; beats of 0.8–7.5 s; every push and punch capped by the face box.
- **Selfie footage:** full frame stays full screen as shot (eyes to chin always in; the hair may touch the top edge; skip pushes that would cut the chin); in the panel the footage fills the panel, cropped on the face, and a head that won't fit gets a taller panel (up to 4:5), never a scaled-down frame with blurred side bars.

## §5 Type and captions
- **Titles:** Inter Tight 800 + Instrument Serif italic keyword ("**Traditional** *Story Arc*", "The Dopamine *Addiction* Loop"), ~70 px cap, white on dark (y ≈ 270–368), black + red keyword on light ("Why Your **Video Fails**", with an optional red underline drawn centre-out); a negative line sits in a red rectangle ("No Longer Works", white on `#BC0001`, radius ≈ 4). **Word-synced build:** title words blur in on their spoken onsets while unspoken words wait at ~40% grey. One title band per reel; titles change per chapter with the camera move and never appear on full-frame beats.
- **Chapter labels:** a big numeral (Inter Tight 800, ~180 px) fades grey → white, the label follows 6–10 f later.
- **Diagram labels:** Inter Tight 700, ≥ 26 px, in pills or beside parts; they snap in on their word.
- **Captions:** **every spoken word, one at a time** (2–3 only for short function words: "we were", "it'll give you"), sentence case as spoken, punctuation kept ("arc.", "Claude,"); Inter Tight 800, **≥ 64 px on the canvas, ≥ 72 px full frame**; white `#FDFDFD` on dark and on footage, black `#020200` on the light canvas; no box, no colour, no animation; hard swap ~1 f before the onset, never blank between words. Split: centre y ≈ 1240 (≥ 30 px under any card, above the panel). **Full frame: high, just under his chin** (top ≈ 40–80 px below the chin) so it's seen the moment his face fills the frame (the references sat it at the chest, y ≈ 1160–1278). The key term may swap to italic serif once or twice per reel; hidden completely while a label or title on screen says the same words (never shrunk to a 10 px glitch, as kw1 did).
- **Handwritten labels** ("HOOK"): Caveat.

## §6 Hook

### 6.1 What the first 3 seconds do
1. **Frame 0 is already drawing:** the diagram's first element writing on in the top half (a crashing curve, a single node glowing red, the first stroke of the model), the panel below with him mid-word, the title building word by word with his voice.
2. **The claim** in one sentence: the diagnosis or the promise ("the reason your videos don't hold attention…", "here's how to make your storytelling so addictive…").
3. **The contrast or the proof by ~2 s:** the lone element multiplies into the full framework's skeleton, or a real proof counter climbs.
4. **The framework named with its size by ~5 s** ("five tools", "four steps"), the skeleton visible with every part still dim.

### 6.2 Hook devices
| ID | Device | Recipe |
|---|---|---|
| **HK-1** | **One becomes the system** | The single element (red, alone) clones outward into the framework's skeleton (stagger ~4 f per part), each part dim until named |
| **HK-2** | **The crashing proof** (kw1) | A real-looking analytics card grows 60 → 100% (f0–21, ease-out), its UI builds top-down, a magenta retention curve writes on and crashes |
| **HK-5** | **Montage → brain → wall** (kw2) | Other creators' clips hard-swap every 5 f in one card, a connector draws to an x-ray brain with a red ripple, a wall of reels scrolls and tints red |
| **HK-6** | **Logo → proof counter** (kw3) | A product logo with rotating rays; a zoom-blur match swap into his profile card; a red pill counts 1K → 20K over ~38 f |
| **HK-3** | **The word-synced title** | The title builds word by word with the voice, the keyword in italic serif |
| **HK-4** | **Face for the claim** | PM-1 rise to face on the claim's punch word, then PM-2 back to the board as the framework is named |

### 6.2b Hook formulas
| ID | Formula | Seen in |
|---|---|---|
| HF-1 | **Diagnosis + the wrong thing named** ("The reason your videos don't hold attention is… because you're using the wrong story arc") | kw1 |
| HF-2 | **How-to + extreme claim** ("so addictive it is scientifically impossible to skip") | kw2 |
| HF-3 | **Transformation promise + insider secret** ("turn Claude into a social media machine… most people don't know this, but…") | kw3 |
| HF-4 | **Named framework + number + borrowed authority** ("only four steps… the same psychology casinos use") | kw2 |

The references measured 7–11 visual changes in the first 3 s.

### 6.3 Hook sound
A soft rising swell under the first write-on, a click per part as the skeleton appears (the rising 3-step set), a single soft boom when the framework is named.

## §7 Structure: the spine and its chapters
- **The spine** is the one diagram (§8.1). Every chapter is a **camera position** on it and a **state change** of it (a seat fills, a step lights, a loop segment glows), with a counter or a strip showing progress ("1/5 hired", steps 1–4).
- **The chapter ritual (identical every time):**
  1. The camera travels to the next empty part (§9), the chapter numeral + label appear, the caption hides.
  2. The part opens into its own small machine (§8.2) on the following words: a mini-screen inside the node, a doc, a terminal, a before/after.
  3. It resolves (✓, a colour change, a counter tick) and **folds back into the diagram**, now lit.
  4. Optional: PM-1 to the face for his opinion on it, PM-2 back.
- **The turn:** the part that changes everything (the locked seat, the twist road, the payoff step) gets the dark-room moment: the sound goes silent (no new SFX), the diagram dims except that part, it unlocks.
- **The payoff:** the camera pulls back to the complete, lit diagram under its final title; it holds as the screenshot frame.
- **The CTA:** a rise to the face for the lead-in ("Now, if you want…"), then the light canvas with the deliverable: a 3×3 grid of his doc pages popping in ~2 f apart, a video card the camera dives into for the social proof, or a setup-video thumbnail, under 'Comment *"KEYWORD"* / To Get The Doc'. Hard stop on the last word.

**Devices that live inside the spine** (from the references):
| ID | Device | Recipe |
|---|---|---|
| SD-1 | **Living chart** | One chart carries the explanation: write it on, label each part on its word, re-colour it to show the new idea, annotate, area-fill (kw1) |
| SD-2 | **Hero-object chapter map** | One stylised object stands for the framework (a neon infinity loop with 1–4 around it); each step returns to it with a whip or diagonal pan; a giant numeral + label appear; the caption hides (kw2) |
| SD-3 | **Noun-to-card stack** | Each listed noun gets a card that blurs in 4–10 f early; the cards stack and scroll up (kw3) |
| SD-4 | **Callout stack** | A bold label + a hand-drawn curly arrow + a screenshot card, alternating right/left, with a continuous pull-out (kw3) |
| SD-5 | **Dims-then-lights list** | Stacked grey pills; each brightens on its spoken word (kw1) |
| SD-6 | **Recap rebuild** | The model rebuilt small with every label popping on its word, under a payoff title ("Super Addicting *Story*") (kw2) |

**Open loops:** the framework's number shown up front (1–4 on the loop), the wrong thing named before the right one, a viewer's question bubble ("Did he actually hit 10k?"), the deliverable named in the CTA.

## §8 The diagram

### 8.1 Diagram families (pick one for the whole reel)
| ID | Diagram | Fits |
|---|---|---|
| D-1 | **Team / org chart** (a centre node, N seats, a top seat) | Tools as roles, a stack, "a team" |
| D-2 | **Loop** (a neon infinity or circle with N stations) | A repeating process, a habit, a cycle |
| D-3 | **Arc / curve** (a chart that is redrawn) | Story, growth, retention, before vs after |
| D-4 | **Pipeline** (stations left to right with a packet travelling) | A workflow, an automation |
| D-5 | **Two roads** (A dashed, B glowing) | A contrast, a mistake vs the right way |
| D-6 | **Stack** (layers building upward) | Fundamentals, a system built on parts |

### 8.2 Part machines (what opens inside a node)
| ID | Machine | What runs | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| MC-1 | **Mini-screen** | A small window inside the node: the tool's real UI or a drawn version, its key action happening (a plan writing, tests passing, a scan finding bugs) | Clicks / soft typing |
| MC-2 | **Swap** | The old thing (red, struck) replaced by the new (green, ✓) | `objects-drawing-board-marker-on-paper-drawing-line-medium-length-02-vadi`, a shine |
| MC-3 | **Compress** | A bloated block shrinking to a clean one, a line count rolling down | `paper-away`, a click on the land |
| MC-4 | **Unlock** | A padlock opening, the real face or logo appearing, sub-parts fanning out with a count | A low swell into a soft boom, a pop per row |
| MC-5 | **Proof box** | A neon green box drawn around real text in a real screenshot or clip | `objects-drawing-board-marker-on-paper-drawing-line-medium-length-02-vadi` |
| MC-6 | **Packet** | A dot travels the path to the next station | `data-woosh` (quiet) |
| MC-7 | **Viewer bubble** | A small avatar with the viewer's question in an outlined bubble | `hint-appear-2025-08-27-02-25-12-utc-hint-appear-02` |

### 8.3 Assets
Drawn diagrams in code; real UI screenshots and real logos; real photos for real people; the creator's own doc pages for the CTA. No movie clips (kw2 used them: never), no stock "tech" footage.

### 8.4 Graphic families (measured)
| ID | Family | Motion signature |

## §10 Motion language
| Token | Value |
|---|---|
| Write-on | Lines draw with ease-in-out (≈ 1 s for a long stroke); dots/nodes pop as the stroke reaches them |
| Light-up | A dim part brightens to its colour with a 20 px glow in 6–8 f on its word |
| Labels | Snap in (opacity + 6 px rise) in 4 f, on the word (0–4 f early) |
| Counters | Roll in 8–12 f ("1/5 hired") |
| Camera | Ease-in-out, 12–40 f; holds drift 1–2%/s |
| Panel moves | Expo-out 10–12 f, no overshoot |
| Pushes | +5–10% over a full-frame beat; one +12–15% punch per reel; capped by the face |
| Timing | The camera lands before the word; the part lights on the word; the resolve on the key word |
| Pop | ~4–5 f |
| Blur-in | 30% opacity / 95% scale → sharp in 5–6 f |
| Flicker-in title (dark) | Grey/white alternating over ~8 f |
| Drift | Every held graphic scales or drifts 1–3% per second |
| Glow | ~20 px outer bloom on dark-canvas lines and objects |

**Layers:** canvas → diagram (may pass behind the panel's top edge) → labels → panel (clipped presenter) → caption → title.
**Finishing:** neon glow on dark lines only; soft shadows on light-canvas cards; the footage as shot.

**Never:** meme sounds, cartoon effects, glitches, alarms, crowd reactions, bright arcade chimes.

### 11.2 The SFX ledger (Studio Standard Rule, unchanged)
- **Any file at most 2× in a reel,** except the one list cue (here `abstract-hit-sub-bass-07`, once per chapter).
- **No file on two consecutive cues;** rotate within each pool.
- **About one cue every 1.2–1.8 s,** each tied to a picture; plain cuts get nothing.
- **Never more than two at once,** never on a consonant.
- Parts and camera moves are texture; chapter hits, unlocks and the payoff are what the viewer should feel.

### 11.3 No music bed
No music bed, ever: this style is scored with sound effects only. The voice and the SFX carry the reel. **Go silent (no new SFX) for 0.5–1 s before the turn's unlock**, then land the hit.

### 11.4 Mix
Voice chain (high-pass 80 Hz, de-ess, light compression); −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP.

## §12 Footage handling
- One camera is enough: the panel and the full-frame face are two framings of one take; two cameras are better (wide for the panel, tight for the face). Never upscale the face crop past the footage's sharpness.
- The presenter is clipped to the panel, never matted over the canvas.
- Every crop is sized from the face; the footage always fills its frame or panel (never a scaled-down frame over its own blur); if the head won't fit a panel, make the panel taller or use full frame.
- Conform VFR; render at the plugin's rate.

## §15 Look at your frames
- **The footage frame:** is every video, recording and picture full screen or in a designed box it fills, never a sharp rectangle over its own blur, never padded with blurred bars?
- **Frame 0, sound off:** is something already drawing, with his face in the panel and the claim building?
- **Every chapter:** did the camera get there before the word? Did the part light on the word?
- **The diagram:** is it the same diagram all reel, getting fuller? Is the last frame worth a screenshot?
- **Every frame with him:** is his whole head visible, inside the panel or full frame?
- **Readable:** can every label be read on a phone?
- **Vibe:** premium, dark, calm camera, one idea at a time. Nothing busy, nothing flashy.
- **Craft:** no text on text, sounds on their pictures.
