# Receipts Explainer Playbook (v2, "Exhibit, Elevated" edition, for Claude Opus 5.5)

> **In this plugin:** this file is the whole playbook: every measured value and recipe from the 4 reference reels is in it, and there is no other playbook file to open. The references run at different rates (dr1 **59.94 fps**, dr2 **25 fps**, dr3 **60 fps**, dr4 **30 fps**); every motion value here is given in **seconds and in frames at 30 fps** (the render rate); source frames at the reel's own rate are named in Appendix A. Positions are on 1080×1920. The per-reel notes it cites (`dr1…dr4`) are not shipped. Sounds are picked by id from SOUNDS.md; fonts are the bundled ones (§5.1). The page gives you `FACE(n)` and `FACE0` (with `head_top` and `chin`) for every box, PIP and re-frame.

**Purpose:** you (Claude) receive a talking-head video of the creator plus its script. Use this document to **ideate and build** every layout change, receipt, stamp, label, data card, transition, caption and sound, then edit the Reel so it looks and sounds like a fast, indignant, evidence-first explainer: **every claim the speaker makes is proved on screen within a second, and the speaker never stops being the one presenting the evidence**, built with such craft that the viewer can't stop watching the case being made.

**What v2 is:** a frame-by-frame analysis of 4 Dhruv Rathee reels (dr1 "Burj Khalifa has been Destroyed… by Indian Media #IranWar": media critique on a fire texture, 157 s; dr2 "Don't Travel To America, You Can Be Arrested!": news explainer from documents on a red painted backdrop, 179 s; dr3 "FlyDubai Hijacking Incident 3D | Capt Smit Machchhar": a documentary reconstruction in a real room, 179 s; dr4 "Petrol Prices SCAM in 2026!": a data-card explainer on a gold texture, 130 s), **elevated ten times** by the best motion designer and creative director in the world: the same grammar, every element animated with intent, a case that visibly builds, captions on every word, and a dense, dark, cinematic sound score. All the references' measured layouts, recipes and rules are kept here. Where a reference reel and a direction disagree, **the directions win**.

**Measured sizes are floors, not targets.** The receipt is the hero: it fills its box, and the camera pushes into the marked line instead of leaving it small. A receipt you can't read on a phone is unfinished.

**Full screen or a box, never any footage or picture over its own blur** (Studio Standard): the creator's video either fills the whole frame edge to edge, or sits in a designed box (rounded corners, a rim or soft shadow, on this style's own ground) that it fills completely, cropped around the face. The same for every other medium: screen recordings, landscape clips, photos, receipts, diagrams and other people's footage either fill the frame (cover-cropped to the part that matters, or a push-in) or sit in a designed box on the style's own ground or scene (its colour, gradient, texture, grid or drawn set). Never shrink anything over a blurred or darkened copy of itself, never pad a band or box with blurred side bars, never set a card on the creator's blurred frame: a hard rectangle against its own blur looks cheap. A tight selfie stays full screen: move or shrink the text above the head, not the video; the hair may touch the top edge, eyes to chin are always in. When the whole head must be seen, use a box layout where it fits.

### The feel (read this twice)
This is the edit of **a prosecutor laying out a case to a jury of millions**. He's fast, indignant and completely sure, and the screen behaves like a courtroom exhibit table: every time he names something, the proof slams onto the table in its original form, a highlighter runs under the exact line as he reads it, and a red verdict stamp lands on the lie. The rhythm is **claim, proof, mark, verdict, flash, next**. Between exhibits the frame burns like old film and resets. His face is never far: in its own box under the evidence, in a ringed circle in the corner, or full frame, staring into the lens for the verdict. It should feel like **a Netflix true-crime documentary cut for a phone**: dark, warm, contrasty, urgent, with every cut doing work. It is never a meme edit, never a slideshow, never gentle.

**Who the reference creator is:** a fast, indignant, highly credible Indian explainer who talks to camera in **Hindi with English nouns** about news, politics, media, money and public events. He sounds like someone laying out a case in court: "ye dekho" ("look at this"), a name, a number, a document. His punchlines are verdicts ("asli mein aisa hua hi nahi": "in reality this never happened").

**Audience (ICP):** Hindi and Hinglish-speaking Indians, 18–45, who follow the news, use YouTube and Instagram as their news source and argue about it with family. They don't trust claims; they trust **a screenshot with the line highlighted**, a headline with its outlet's name, a graph, a date.

**Style lineage:** a TV-documentary explainer rebuilt for the phone, built from three layers:
1. **Presenter layer:** one tripod setup, medium close-up, the speaker either **keyed onto a themed texture** whose colour matches the topic (fire orange for war, red paint for danger, gold for money) or **filmed in a real room** for a story. Jump cuts re-frame him; pauses are cut. He is full frame, in his own box of a split, or in a framed picture-in-picture.
2. **Receipt layer:** the proof of every claim in its original form (thumbnail, post, headline, document, phone video, graph, map, price card), each with one mark: a sweep, a red circle, a stamp, a count-up.
3. **Punctuation layer:** a **burn flash** (yellow, orange and red light, about 0.4–0.5 s, the cut hidden inside the brightest frame) with a whoosh and a thump between exhibits, a blur cut inside a block, a stamp or red tint on the emotional word.
- **This style is not one layout.** One creator and one temperament carry **three formats** (§3.0): the receipts reel, the reconstruction story and the data-card explainer. They share six layouts (L-1, L-2, L-2i, L-3, L-4, L-5; §3.2–3.7), and the speaker moves between them every few seconds.
- **The divided layouts stay divided.** The evidence and the speaker live in **two separate boxes** with a coloured divider between them (evidence on top, speaker below; or the inverse in the data format). Never collapse them into one box, never float the speaker over the evidence.

## §1 Procedure (follow in order)

1. **Inventory.** Probe the footage; identify the setup (§12.1: keyable studio or real room); conform variable frame rate first.
2. **Transcribe** with word timings (Hindi comes back in Devanagari: keep it for timing). Check every name, outlet, place and number against the script.
3. **Make the claims list:** every **named thing** (outlet, person, place, date, number, document, post, event) with its onset; each gets a receipt within ~1 s (M3). Mark the **verdict words** ("nahi", "fake", "scam") and **emotional words** ("bachche": "children"): they carry stamps and tint beats.
4. **Choose the format** (§3.0) from the shape of the script:
   - a claim to expose or a pattern of events proved with documents → `FMT-RECEIPTS`;
   - one event told in story order → `FMT-STORY`;
   - an argument made from numbers → `FMT-DATA`.
   Write one line on why. One format per reel; it may borrow a single device from another, never its set or PIP shape.
5. **Choose the set and theme colour** (§4.2); the divider, PIP border and stamp follow it.
6. **Segment** the script into: `HOOK` (0–3 s), `PROOF-n` exhibits / `BEAT-n` story steps / `STEP-n` numbers, `TWIST` or `PAYOFF` (the moment the case closes), `CTA`, `END`.
7. **Draw the speaker map** (§3.0a): a layout for every sentence.
8. **Captions** (§5.2): every spoken word, in the creator's caption choice (romanised Hinglish by default), unless their profile says no captions.
9. **Ideate 3 hook variants** (§6) and recommend one.
10. **Fill the beat sheet** (§13): one beat per named thing, verdict word or layout change, each with its face check, **its hero and its sounds**.
10b. **The 10× pass** (§8.0): for every beat, the hero, how it anticipates, lands and follows through, its secondary action, where it came from and where it goes; plan the case spine (EV-1 counter, EV-2 board). Then **attack the plan**: where is a receipt unreadable or small? Where is a frame still? Where does a receipt appear without a beat before it? Where is the verdict soft? Where would a viewer swipe? Fix it in the plan.
10c. **Score it** (§11): every event gets its sound, logged in the sound ledger.
11. **Plan the transition map** (§9.3) against the budget (§9.4).
12. **Produce the assets** (§8.6) from real sources; list anything missing. **Never invent a receipt.**
13. **Show the creator** the plan and stills (§13.3).
14. **Build,** look at a still of every layout and stamp, check the face in each (§3.8), look at your frames (§15), score, render (§16).

## §2 Craft that never bends
- **The whole face is always visible,** hair to chin, in every layout, box and PIP, at every re-frame (the measured limits are in M1 below). A caption may cross the face; a graphic, receipt, stamp or PIP may not.
- **Text never sits on text;** nothing sits on the caption; a receipt's marked line is never under a label, the PIP or the caption.
- **Sound in sync:** every SFX on the frame its picture lands, never on a consonant of a name or number (slide ±2 f).
- **True and exact:** every headline, quote, number and name exactly as published; a verdict stamp only on content a cited source shows false (M8).
- **Readable at phone size:** the editor's own text ≥ 24 px (labels ≥ 26 px); the marked line of a receipt readable on a phone, or the camera pushes in until it is.
- **Loudness:** −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1.0 dBTP; the last syllable heard in full, then a hard stop.

Everything else is direction. You decide; write your reasons into the plan.

### 2.1 The house grammar (what makes it this style)
Not checks: this is what the four reference reels do every time, and why it works. Break one only with a reason in the plan. (The IDs are kept because other sections cite them.)
- M1. **The whole face is always visible, hair to chin, in every layout:** full frame, every jump-cut re-frame and punch-in, the speaker's box of a split, every circle, rectangle or card PIP. The face decides how far a crop goes: in full frame the head top stays **≥ 80 px** from the top edge; in a box or PIP it stays **≥ 6% of the box height** below the box's top edge (≥ 30 px in a circle) and the chin stays inside. Scale a tight source down rather than cut the hair. Fixes: dr4's speaker-top split (hair ~40 px from the top), dr3's circle PIP (hair on the ring), dr2's tightest push (50–80 px). **Captions may cross the face** (a caption is allowed over the face); a graphic, receipt, stamp or PIP may not.
- M2. **One format per reel** (§3.0), chosen from the script with a written reason; one set theme (§4.2) whose colour also drives the divider, the PIP border and the stamp.
- M3. **Receipt on the word.** Every named outlet, person, place, number, date and document gets its real receipt on screen **within ~1 s** of the name (it may lead the word by ≤ 0.3 s). Evidence-box swaps land on the noun (±3 f).
- M4. **The speaker moves.** He changes layout every few seconds: full frame ↔ split ↔ full receipt with PIP ↔ chest object. Shares in §3.0a. Never a stretch of more than **~12 s** in one layout without a change inside it (a swap, a sweep, a re-frame).
- M5. **Divided layouts stay divided.** In L-2 the evidence box and the speaker box are two boxes of equal height with a coloured divider between them (y ≈ 948–967); in L-2i the speaker box is on top. Never merge them, never float the speaker over the evidence, never put the evidence behind the speaker.
- M6. **Pauses are cut.** No gap over **0.3 s** in `FMT-RECEIPTS` / `FMT-DATA` (dr4 had none over 0.25 s). Exceptions: a raw clip's own sound, one section change, and in `FMT-STORY` up to **two suspense pauses of 0.5–0.9 s** before a reveal (dr3).
- M7. **Every receipt gets one mark** that says why it is on screen, landing on the spoken words: a highlight sweep behind the exact phrase, a red circle, an outline box, a stamp, a count-up, a bracket.
- M8. **Facts only from the script or supplied sources.** Every headline, quote, number and name is exactly as published. A **verdict stamp is a claim**: "FAKE" (or any verdict) lands only on content a cited source has shown to be false, and that source appears in the same block.
- M9. **Recreated visuals are tagged.** Any 3D re-enactment, AI-generated or rebuilt scene carries a **REPRESENTATIONAL** tag for as long as it is on screen (dr3), at a readable size (§5.6, fix).
- M10. **Bystanders are protected.** Faces of private people in raw clips are pixelated or blurred (dr2); public figures appear as themselves, named.
- M11. **Raw clips speak for themselves.** A shocking phone video plays 3–7 s with its own audio and its own burned-in subtitles, with no voice-over (dr2). Keep the source's subtitles; never add a second caption layer over them.
- M12. **Name every person shown** with a label the first time (§5.3), on the spoken name (±3 f).
- M13. **Audio:** **−14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1.0 dBTP.** dr1 sat at −9.7 LUFS with +0.2 dBTP (clipping), dr2 at −0.3, dr3 at −0.5: fix. dr4's −14.0 / −1.4 is the reference.
- M14. **End clean.** The last word is heard to its last syllable, then a hard stop within 6 f; an end card is ≤ 1.0 s. Fixes: dr1 cut the last word mid-syllable ("dekh sakte pr-"); dr4 left 1.5 s of black.
- M15. **Spelling:** English words, names, outlets and places spelled exactly as in the source (SMIT MACHCHHAR, HAMAM AL-HAMMAMI). ₹ with Indian grouping.
- M16. **Hold before you cut.** A label or chip holds ≥ 1.5 s (observed 1.5–2 s); a stamp holds ≥ 0.5 s before the flash takes it; a highlighted line holds ≥ 0.6 s after its sweep completes.

**Never in this style:**
- N1. **Copyrighted film clips.** dr3 cut to movie scenes for "hijacking": fix. Use real footage, a document or a tagged re-enactment.
- N2. **Third-party plugs and watermarks.** Crop or cover app watermarks burned into downloaded clips (dr2 showed an editing app's watermark): fix. The CTA promotes only the creator's own videos.
- N3. Graphics over the face (M1): chest objects sit **below the chin** (≥ chin + 40 px); stamps sit on the receipt, never on the speaker or the PIP.
- N4. A PIP that hides the action of the receipt under it. Move the PIP to the empty corner, shot by shot (dr3).
- N5. A PIP flush with or past the frame edge (dr4's gold card sat at x 3 and at x 1079): fix. Keep **≥ 30 px** from every edge.
- N6. Name labels in the platform's bottom band (dr1 set them at y ≈ 1640–1660, under the platform UI): fix. Labels live inside y ≤ 1540 (§3.9).
- N7. Text smaller than **24 px** on screen as the editor's own text (dr3's REPRESENTATIONAL tag was ~18 px): fix.
- N8. Bouncy, cartoon or comic sounds, joke voices, any music track or bed (§11).
- N9. Transitions outside the house set `T-01…T-12` (§9).
- N10. A receipt on screen without its source identity: every article shows its outlet's name or logo; every post shows its account; every graph shows what it measures.
- N11. Fake comments, invented posts, invented quotes, edited headlines or numbers that don't match the source.
- N12. A true peak above −1.0 dBTP, a clipped master, a last word cut short, or a file whose duration doesn't match the edit.

## §3 Formats and screen layouts

### 3.0 Choosing the format
| The script… | Format | Why |
|---|---|---|
| …accuses someone or exposes a pattern, and the proof is posts, headlines, documents and clips | `FMT-RECEIPTS` | The exhibit chain (§7.1) and the stamp turn the reel into a case |
| …tells one event in story order (date → what happened → who → how it ended → open questions) | `FMT-STORY` | A dateline, a map and re-enactments make the story followable with the sound off |
| …argues from numbers that change over time or between two things | `FMT-DATA` | The same price card reused for each year makes the comparison like for like |

**The set follows the format** (keyed texture or real room); if the matte is poor (§12.3), any format may use the real room.

### 3.0a The speaker map (every reel)
| Beat | Layout | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Frame 0** | **L-1 full frame**, mid-word (§3.1) | The face opens the case |
| **The first named thing** (~1.5–3.5 s) | **L-2 split** slides open (evidence top, speaker bottom) or **L-2i** rises (speaker top, evidence bottom) | The receipt arrives while he is still talking to you |
| **A receipt that must be read** | **L-3 full receipt + PIP** | The text needs the whole width; the PIP keeps him presenting |
| **A raw clip with its own sound, a montage, an emotional image** | **L-4 face absent**, 1–12 s | The footage is the argument |
| **A verdict, an opinion, a turn, a question to the viewer** | **L-1 full frame**, entered with a burn flash | He talks to you, not to the evidence |
| **A number or keyword to remember while he talks** | **L-5 chest object** over L-1 | No cut away needed for one object |
| **Ending / CTA** | L-1, or L-2/L-2i with his own video card in the evidence box | The last thing seen is him or his next video |

- The speaker is **never cut out over the B-roll** and **never side by side** with it: his box, his PIP, or full frame.

### 3.1 `HOOK` (0–3 s)
- **Frame 0 is the speaker, mid-word** (all 4 reels). Never black, never a title card, never an empty receipt.
- **3–4 visual changes in the first 3 s** (measured 3, 3, 3, 4): the blur clears, the split opens with the first emblem or receipt, a label or stamp lands, the first swap.
- The first receipt is on screen by **1.5–3.5 s** (§6.3).

### 3.2 `L-1` full-frame talking head
- The speaker on the set (keyed texture or real room) filling the frame: head top 120–260, eye line 400–560, face centre x 420–700, head ~380–430 px wide.
- **Jump-cut re-frames:** consecutive takes alternate between a close framing (A), a medium one (B) and a medium shifted sideways (B'): the face moves ±70–100 px laterally and the scale changes ~115–120% between takes. Re-frames happen only on cuts.

### 4.4 Meaning of colour
- **The theme hue** = the topic's mood (fire = war, red = danger, gold = money, the real room = a story).
- **Red** = false, danger, costlier, the incriminating line: stamps, red circles, red sweeps, red tint beats, up-chevrons.
- **Green** = cheaper, better, the good number.
- **Yellow** = look here: a marker on a headline, a price label, a topic label, a route.
- **Orange** (fire set) = the highlighted words of a post.
- **Cyan** = measured data (a graph line).
- **Black box + white text** = a fact the speaker states (date, year, keyword).
- **Blue tint** = a reveal that the footage was something else.
**Rules:** one mark colour per receipt; red never decorates; green and red only for direction in data.

### 4.5 Footage look (don't fight it)
- **Keyed sets:** warm, contrasty, saturated skin against the warm texture, rich blacks (a dark crew-neck tee: navy or black), a soft drop shadow behind the speaker toward one side.
- **Real room:** natural, warm, soft daylight, gentle contrast; keep it as shot.
- **Receipts:** as grabbed. Raw phone clips stay ungraded (cold snowy blues are fine); archive photos may go B&W; B-roll may be desaturated or B&W with a red tint for a negative beat (dr4).
- **Data cards:** near-black with faint film grain and scratches (dr4).

## §6 Hook system

### 6.0 The 10× hook
- **Frame 0 is the speaker mid-word, already accusing,** with something already moving: the blur clearing, the dateline chip rising, the split beginning to open. Never a still first frame.
- **The first receipt lands like a slap by 1.5–2.5 s** (earlier than the references' 3.5 s): anticipation (the frame dims 15% for 3 f), the slam with a camera-shutter snap, the mark on the very next words.
- **The case is announced visually:** the `EXHIBIT 01` counter appears with the first receipt, so the viewer knows a list of proof is coming (EV-1).
- **The first verdict or the first number lands before 5 s** with its sound (stamp hit or count tick + thump).

### 6.1 What the hook must do in about 3 seconds
1. **Accuse or alarm in the first sentence**, while the speaker's face is on screen (frame 0 is always him).
2. **Show the first receipt or the subject by 1.5–3.5 s** with "ye dekho" energy: the subject's emblem, the headline, the date, the map.
3. **Open a loop the receipts will pay:** "phir se" ("once again") implies a list of offences; "sabse khatarnaak faisla" ("the most dangerous decision") is withheld; "why did it fall?"; "why is petrol dearer when crude is cheaper?".

Voice, receipt and mark say **the same thing three ways**: the voice accuses, the receipt proves, the mark (stamp, sweep, label) judges.

### 6.2 Hook formulas (`HF`)
| ID | Formula | Spoken pattern (romanised, gloss) | Seen in |
|---|---|---|---|
| HF-1 | **Accusation + absurd proof** | "…ne phir se apni aukaat dikha di hai. Ye dekho, …ne Burj Khalifa ko hi gira daala." ("…have once again shown their true level. Look at this: they brought down the Burj Khalifa itself.") | dr1 |
| HF-2 | **Shared knowledge → hidden danger** | "…jo nautanki kar rahe hain use to aap dekh hi rahe honge, lekin aap ye nahi jaante honge ki…" ("You've surely seen the drama…, but what you probably don't know is that…") | dr2 |
| HF-3 | **Dateline cold open** | "30 September ki subah … ki flight … achaanak se bina kisi warning ke neeche girne lagti hai." ("On the morning of 30 September, flight … suddenly starts falling without any warning.") | dr3 |
| HF-4 | **"Scam" + promised shock + hard numbers** | "… ko lekar jo scam … kar rahi hai na, aap bhi hairaan ho jaoge… 2014 mein … 105 dollar … aur … 72 rupees." ("The scam … is pulling over …, you'll be shocked too… In 2014 it was $105 and ₹72.") | dr4 |

Add-ons that stack with any formula:
- **"Ye dekho" ("look at this")** pointing straight at the first receipt (dr1).
- **A cold proof:** a raw clip before the subject is named (dr2).
- **A question with a visual question mark** ("aaj rate kya hai?": "what's the rate today?") on the chest (dr4).

Write **3 variants** per video using different formulas, and recommend one.

### 7.2 The exhibit ritual (one recipe per exhibit, identical every time)
1. **Enter:** T-01 burn flash (or a blur cut inside a block), timed so the peak sits **just before** the noun that names the exhibit.
2. **Show:** the receipt in L-2 (evidence box) or L-3 (full + PIP), with its source identity visible (outlet, account, agency).
3. **Name:** `LBL-NAME` or the outlet's own logo on the name (±3 f); a logo may pop with overshoot (dr1's logo disc).
4. **Mark:** the highlight sweep runs behind the exact words **while he reads them**, or the red circle draws on the number, or the stamp slams on the verdict word.
5. **Hold:** ≥ 0.6 s after the mark completes; the PIP or the speaker box keeps him alive.
6. **Exit:** a hard swap to the next receipt in the same block, or a burn flash back to L-1 for his reaction.
- **Pick one return style per reel** (burn flash in dr1, dr2, dr4; flash or blur in dr3) and keep it.

### 7.3 Open loops (retention)
- **The list implied by "phir se" ("once again"):** each exhibit pays one off (dr1).
- **The withheld danger:** "the most dangerous decision" named only after ~7–13 s (dr2).
- **The question on screen:** a lightning "?" on the chest over "aaj rate kya hai?" ("what's today's rate?"), answered by the table (dr4).
- **The twist:** "why did it fall?" → "hijacking" at 35 s → "the pilot himself" at 41 s (dr3).
- Every loop is paid off **on screen** with a receipt, not only in words.

## §8 Graphics system

### 8.0 The 10× layer (what the best motion designer adds to every exhibit)
**Animation principles, applied to this style:**
| Principle | How it shows up here |
|---|---|
| **Anticipation** | Before a receipt lands, the current frame dims 10–15% for 3 f and the PIP or speaker box nudges 6 px aside to make room; before a stamp, the receipt darkens slightly and holds still for 4 f |
| **Squash & weight** | Receipts land with a 2-frame 1.03 → 1.00 settle and a contact shadow that tightens; the stamp lands at 2.5× → 1.0 with a 1-frame flatten (scale y 0.96) on impact |
| **Follow-through & overlap** | The stamp's ink bleeds outward 2 f after impact; a sweep's glowing leading edge trails 2 f; a label settles 2 f after its receipt; chevrons cascade 3 f apart |
| **Arcs** | Article cards rotate in on an arc (the rotate-in recipe), photos drop in on a slight curve with a tilt that settles to 0° |
| **Slow in / slow out** | Strong ease-out on every arrival, ease-in-out on pushes and pans; only typewriters and counts are linear |
| **Staging** | One hero per frame: when the receipt lands, everything else (the speaker box, the set texture) drops 10% in brightness; when he delivers the verdict, the receipts are gone |
| **Secondary action** | While a receipt holds: a slow 2–3% push toward the marked line, the set texture's embers/smoke drift, the PIP ring breathes ±2%, film grain on archive material |
| **Timing** | Receipts fast (6–8 f), stamps faster (6 f), counts to the voice (0.4–1.5 s), the verdict beat slowest (a 0.3–0.5 s hold of stillness before it) |
| **Exaggeration** | One verdict per reel gets the big treatment: the screen tints red for 2 f, the stamp lands bigger, the low boom under it |
| **Appeal** | Real paper, real screenshots with real shadows, warm contrasty light; the evidence looks touchable, the speaker looks lit like TV |

**Design hierarchy (every frame):**
1. **The marked words** of the receipt (the sweep, the circle, the number): what the viewer must read.
2. **The receipt's source identity** (the outlet logo, the account, the date).
3. **His face** (in its box, PIP or full frame), never covered, never competing.
4. **Labels, chips, the exhibit counter, the caption.**
If the eye doesn't land on the marked words first, the frame is wrong.

**Effects toolkit (one or two per element, never all):**
- **Burn flash** (the signature, T-01), with the cut inside its peak.
- **Push-to-line:** the camera pushes into the marked line (1.0 → 1.25) as he reads it (EV-3).
- **2.5D photo:** an archive photo split into foreground/background with a slow parallax (a 3% drift difference) for emotional beats.
- **Paper and grain:** documents get a paper texture and a soft shadow; archive photos and data cards get film grain and scratches.
- **Red tint and red glow:** on the lie, the stamp, the emotional word (T-08), never decorative.
- **Depth of field:** the set texture behind a receipt blurs slightly more when the receipt lands.
- **Motion blur** on anything moving > 40 px per frame.
Never: memes, stickers (other than the subject's sticker punchline), cartoon pops, shakes on his face, RGB splits, glitch transitions.

### 8.3a Frame-by-frame recipes (30 fps; source frames in Appendix A)
- **Stamp slam** (dr1): f0 the stamp appears huge (~2.5×), partly off frame; f2–3 it shrinks fast to ~1.2×; f6 it lands at 1.0 with ~−6° rotation and a tiny settle; ease-out; red glow on. Land f6 on the stressed syllable of the denial word ("nahi"). Hold until the burn flash takes it (≥ 15 f).
- **Highlight sweep** (dr1 orange; dr2, dr3 red): bars the height of the text line grow left to right **behind** the words, one line at a time, timed to his reading: ~10–12 f for a short phrase (dr2), ~12–20 f per line on a post (dr1: 24 f and 40 f at 59.94 fps). The first word's bar starts on that word's onset. Variants: a red underline, a white outline box, a yellow marker, a red ellipse.
- **Article rotate-in** (dr2, dr3): the headline block appears rotated ~90° (reading vertically, from the left) and swings flat in **6–7 f**, ease-out, slight motion blur, a whoosh on it. The PIP is already in place.
- **Circle PIP pop** (dr2): ~85% → 100% over 1–2 f, no bounce. **Blur-in variant** (dr3): the receipt and the PIP arrive blurred together and sharpen over ~5 f.
- **Dateline fade-rise** (dr3): opacity 0 → 100%, 60–80 px up, **6 f**, ease-out.
- **Date typewriter** (dr2): the box appears empty with the red cursor; characters type ~1 per 1.5–2.5 f; the box widens with the text, the cursor riding its right edge; complete in ~0.85 s.
- **Year typewriter** (dr4): empty box + cursor 2 f, then "2" (f4), "20" (f12), "201" (f14), "2014" (~f22); the underline draws beneath; ~0.8 s.
- **Count-up / count-down** (dr4): "$56" → "$105" in 0.6 s ease-out; "₹62" → "₹72" in 0.4 s, fading up from 0% with a blur; "$104" → "$96" over 1.5 s with yellow then green down-chevrons; "₹76" → "₹105" → "₹111" over ~1 s, red up-chevrons at the end. Land on the number's last syllable. Elements enter in order: year, barrel, price, nozzle, price.
- **Graph bracket + count-down** (dr3): a thin white line grows down from the top-right of the segment, turns the corner and closes a rectangle in **0.7 s** (21 f), linear; holds 0.6 s; then the view zooms to the drop and a white callout rides down the cyan line counting 34,000 → 31,227 → 28,568 → 20,633 → 17,966 → 17,000 ft over ~3 s, ease-in-out, in sync with the spoken numbers.
- **Route draw** (dr3): the yellow line extends for ~2 s while the map pans and zooms toward the end; the end dot scales 0 → 100% in **3 f**; the end label box pops.

| Stamp | 2.5× → 1.2× → 1.0, 6 f, ~−6° to −10°, glow |
| Sweep | 10–20 f per line, behind the text, from the word's onset |
| Typewriter | 1 char / 1.5–4 f with a red cursor; box widens with the text |
| Count | 0.4–1.5 s ease-out, landing on the last syllable; chevrons after |
| PIP | Pop 85 → 100% in 1–2 f, or sharpen with the receipt in ~5 f |
| Split slide / rise | 12 f / 8 f, ease-out |
| Holds | Labels ≥ 1.5 s, stamps ≥ 15 f, marked lines ≥ 18 f after the mark |
| Exits | With the next cut or inside a flash; never a separate fade-out |
| Anticipation | 3 f dim (10–15%) before a receipt; 4 f stillness before a stamp |
| Receipt settle | 1.03 → 1.00 in 2 f with a tightening contact shadow |
| Stamp follow-through | Ink bleed 2 f after impact; 1-frame flatten on impact |
| Push-to-line | 1.0 → 1.25 over 18–24 f, ease-in-out, on the marked line |
| Exhibit tick | The counter digit rolls in 4 f |

### 10.2 Camera
- **Jump-cut re-frames** between takes (A close / B medium / B' shifted ±70–100 px, ~115–120% scale steps); no zoom inside a shot, no Ken Burns on the speaker.
- Receipts move (pushes, pans, zooms); every re-frame is limited by the face (M1).

### 10.3 Layer order (back to front)
1. Set texture (keyed) or the real room · 2. Speaker (with its soft drop shadow on a keyed set) · 3. Evidence box / full receipt · 4. Divider · 5. PIP · 6. Marks (sweeps sit **behind** the receipt text, circles and boxes over it) · 7. Labels, chips, chest objects · 8. Stamp · 9. Captions (every word) · 10. Light passes (T-01, T-02, T-08).

### 10.4 Finishing
- Warm, contrasty, saturated on keyed sets; natural on the real room. No vignette.
- Data cards carry faint grain and scratches; the rest is clean.
- Spell-check every label against the source (M15).

## §11 Sound design (D4)

**No music bed, ever.** Sound is SFX only (the creator's direction, 12 Oct 2026): never a music track, bed, drone or loop under the voice.

The references measured ~4–8 hits per 60 s (dr1 ~8, dr2 ~5, dr3 ~5, dr4 ~5), almost all on flashes, card entries and stamps, with nothing on sweeps, typing or counts (dr1 ran a low dark drone, dr3 a suspense pad, dr4 a music tail: we use none). This edition **scores every exhibit event**, dark and cinematic: the viewer should feel each receipt land like evidence on a table.

**Never in this style:** bouncy, cartoon or comic sounds, joke voices, meme sounds, chimes and dings that feel cute, any music track or bed.

### 11.2 The SFX ledger (Studio Standard Rule, unchanged)
- **Any file at most 2× in a reel,** except the one **list cue** (here the receipt snap family or `paper-turn-page`, once per exhibit, rotating the shutter variants inside the 2× limit; or the `YEAR` typing in `FMT-DATA`).
- **No file on two consecutive cues;** rotate within each pool.
- **Density:** about one cue every 1.2–1.8 s on average, each tied to a picture; plain hard cuts and jump cuts get nothing.
- **Never more than two cues overlapping;** never on a consonant of a name or number (shift ±2 f).
- Snaps, marks, typing and ticks are texture; the flash, the stamp and the verdict boom are what the viewer should feel.

### 11.3 Silence and raw clips
- **Go silent (no new SFX) for 0.3–0.6 s before the verdict** or the twist, then land the stamp or the hit.
- **Raw clips** play their own audio at speech level (dr2: −2 to −5 dB vs speech), with no SFX over them; news clips under the voice are ducked to ~−15 dB.

### 11.4 Mix
High-pass 80 Hz, de-ess, firm compression (a dense, loud voice is the house sound); **−14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1.0 dBTP** (M13). The voice is always the loudest thing.

## §15 Look at your frames
Not a checklist; the questions the best editor asks while scrubbing:
- **Frame 0, sound off:** is he mid-word, accusing, with something already moving?
- **Their video and every picture:** full screen, or in a designed box it fills on the style's own ground? Never a sharp rectangle (footage, recording, landscape clip, photo) over its own blur, never blurred side bars, never a card on their blurred frame?
- **Every receipt:** did it land like evidence (a beat before, a snap, a settle)? Can I read its source and its marked line on a phone? Did the camera go to the line?
- **Every frame:** what does my eye land on first? It should be the marked words. Is his whole head in, in his box, PIP or full frame?
- **The case:** can I see it building (the exhibit counter, the board)? Does the verdict land like a gavel?
- **Sound:** does every event have its sound, dark and on the frame, no file overused, no music?
- **Captions:** every word, just under his chin, never on a marked line?
- **Vibe:** urgent, warm, documentary. Anything cute, comic or gentle comes out.
