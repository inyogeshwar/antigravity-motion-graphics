# Warm Craft Explainer Playbook (v2, "Hands, Face, One Gold Word, Elevated" edition, for Claude Opus 5.5)

> **In this plugin:** this file is the whole playbook: every observed value and recipe from the 2 reference reels is in it, and there is no other playbook file to open. Both references run at **30 fps**, so every frame count below is at 30 fps (`f` = 1/30 s). Positions are on 1080×1920 (both sources are 720×1280, scaled ×1.5). The per-reel notes this file cites (`pm1`, `pm2`) are not shipped. Sounds are picked by id from SOUNDS.md; commands are in the edit skill's REFERENCE.md. The page gives you `FACE(n)` and `FACE0` (with `head_top` and `chin`) for every speaker frame. "Warm Craft Explainer" is a working name for the style.

**Purpose:** you (Claude) receive a talking-head video plus its script (and, for the step format, footage of the real thing being done). Use this document to **ideate and build** every cut, caption, keyword, step marker, transition and sound, then edit the Reel so it looks and sounds like a calm, warm, filmic craft explainer: **the real thing is done on camera by real hands, the face comes back for the opinion, and one word per phrase glows gold**, cut with such warmth, weight and craft that the viewer can almost feel the paper under their own fingers and stays to the last step.

**What v2 is:** a frame-by-frame analysis of the only two finished reels available from the reference creator (Peter McKinnon, a Canadian photographer and filmmaker), **elevated ten times** by the best editor, cinematographer-minded motion designer and creative director in the world. The hands-face-gold-word grammar is kept whole. This edition makes every angle change one continuous action, lets the gold word catch the light, gives the STEP chip a count you can feel, turns the time skip into a living time-lapse, takes the proof like a photograph, lets the speaker dock under the work while the hands keep going, and scores it all with a whisper-quiet, tactile foley of paper, pen, focus rings and shutters. All measured layouts, recipes and rules are kept. Where a reference reel and a direction disagree, **the directions win**. The two reels:
- **pm1** "my notebook system": a 110 s hands-on tutorial. A pocket notebook is set up in five steps on a patterned rug, filmed overhead, close and low, with his continuous voice-over; a desk talking head cuts in for asides. A black mono "STEP n" chip numbers the steps; a 13 s single-take recap ticks the chip 1→5.
- **pm2** "does printing make you a photographer?": a 47 s podcast clip. Two people, tight alternating singles, cut on whoever speaks or reacts, no music, no graphics but the captions.

The analysis covered cut lists, framing and caption measurements, caption reveal timing, the transition frame recipe, colour sampling and audio (loudness, gaps). Contact sheets of both reels were checked by eye for this file.
- Every rule below is either **observed** (seen in the reels, the most consistent value chosen as the house standard), a **fix** (something a reel got wrong that this playbook replaces; marked **(fix)**), or this edition's **elevation** (marked as such; §6.0, §8.0, §8.7).
- **This style is not one layout.** One caption system, one grade and one temperament carry **two formats** (§3): the hands-on step tutorial and the face-to-face conversation clip. Pick the format from the script and footage.
- **The speaker moves.** Face full frame ↔ the real thing full frame, by a hard cut on the action word; back on the face for every opinion, source and step name; the closer punch at the end. This edition adds one designed move, the **bench card** (`L-7`, EV-7): when a step is named while the hands keep working, the face eases into a wide card under the work, then grows back to full frame.
- Only two reels exist. Where a value was seen once it is still the house value, but it is marked "(1 reel)" when that matters. Guesses are marked as guesses.

**Measured sizes are floors, not targets.** The references' captions (46–66 px, a 362 px column, long lines shrunk to 22–48 px) and STEP chip (300×98 at 48 px) were small on a phone. Here:
- **Full screen or a box, never any footage or picture over its own blur** (Naman, 13 Oct): the creator's video, a screen recording, a landscape clip, a photo or a diagram either fills the whole frame edge to edge (cover-crop to the part that matters, or a push-in), or sits in a designed box (rounded, rimmed or softly shadowed, on this style's own ground, colour, gradient, texture, grid or drawn set) that it fills completely. Never shrink anything inside the frame over a blurred or darkened copy of itself, never pad a box, band or panel with blurred side bars, never set a card on the creator's own blurred frame. A tight selfie stays full screen as shot: move the text, not the video (eyes to chin always in; the hair may touch the top edge). When the whole head must show, use a box sized so the footage fills it with the head inside. (A matte cut-out over a plate is fine: it has no box edge.)
- **Captions:** `CAP-WORD` **72–80 px** (`FMT-STEPS`) / **76–84 px** (`FMT-TALK`), `CAP-GOLD` ≈ 1.35× (**100–110 / 104–116 px**), every spoken word, a column up to **900 px**; never below **64 px**: break the phrase instead.
- **The STEP chip:** **≥ 420×130 px** with 64 px mono, a 5-dot progress row under it (EV-3).
- **The real thing is the hero:** the object (or the screen) fills the frame; the one detail that proves the step reads at **≥ 48 px** on a phone: get the close shot (L-4), rack to it (EV-5) or crop the recording until it does.
- **The bench card** (`L-7`): the work full bleed on top, the face card **≥ 940 px wide** below.
- **No dead frame:** a frame that is more than half plain or blurred background for longer than ~0.7 s means the thing is too small or the next element is late. The one exception is the deliberate empty-surface reset (T-04, ~1 s): it is the style's breath, and the hand always comes back into it.
- **The selfie stays big:** a tight selfie stays full screen as shot (eyes to chin always in; the hair may touch the top edge): move the chip and the text, never shrink the video; in the bench card the footage fills the card (≥ 940 px wide), never a narrow pillar.

**Never pale.** The reference look is warm and filmic, and it drifts muted. Here it is **warm and rich**: deep blacks, warm glowing mids, skin with life in it, the object's own colour (a red string, a coral note, a kraft cover) at full strength, highlights rolled off softly but never grey. Never desaturate, fade or lift the blacks as a look; a blurred fill behind scaled footage keeps its colour (≈ 85–90 % brightness). The film burn (T-03) is a short, warm event, never a grade (§4.4).

**How to use this file:**
- Follow §1 (procedure) in order.
- Look up visuals by ID: formats `FMT-…`, layouts `L-…`, hook formulas `HF-…`, hook visuals `H-…`, structure devices `SD-…`, B-roll families `B-…`, patterns `P-…`, transitions `T-…`, elevated devices `EV-…`.
- §2 is the craft that never bends; §2.1 is the house grammar (what makes this style, with reasons). Everything else is a default you adapt like the best editor in the world, with a one-line reason in the plan.

### The feel (read this twice)
This is **a friend at his workbench at golden hour, showing you exactly how he does the thing, close enough to smell the paper.** Every step is real: his hands, his pen, the page, a string pulled tight. He names the step to your face, then the hands do it, and the cut between angles is so in step with the motion that it feels like one shot. One word in every line glows gold, as if it caught the window light. The STEP chip clicks over like a typewriter key and a little row of dots fills as you go; the time skip blurs weeks of pages into two seconds; the finished thing is raised to the lens, the focus pulls, and the shutter takes it. It feels **calm, tactile and expert**: unhurried but never idle, warm but never soft. If it looks like a slick ad, it's wrong; if it looks like a static desk vlog, it's wrong too.

**Who the reference creator is:** a photographer-filmmaker who talks to camera like a friend at his desk: calm and warm in tone, quick in words (3.6–3.9 words/s), a few fillers left in. His tattooed hands are an identity mark, so pm1 reads as him even when only his hands are on screen. The step is *named* on his face and *done* by his hands.

**Audience (ICP):** creators, photographers, stationery and productivity people, English-speaking. They want the actual sequence of steps, close enough to copy, and the opinion of someone who has done it for years. The calm, tactile pace is part of the appeal.

**Style lineage:** a filmic "maker" edit in three layers:
1. **Picture:** everything filmed, nothing designed. The real object in real hands (overhead, close, low angle) on a textured surface, graded warm with crushed blacks. The face is full frame or absent; this edition adds one designed exception, the wide bench card (`L-7`).
2. **Words:** white tight grotesk words that fade in place as they are heard, **one keyword per phrase in a glowing gold italic serif**, bigger than the rest; in the step format a black mono **STEP n** chip at the top.
3. **Punctuation:** hard cuts on word boundaries; a film-burn flash (teal → white → warm, 8 f) at most twice; empty-surface resets done in camera. This edition adds the light on the gold word, the counting chip, the living time-lapse, the rack, the shutter freeze and a whisper-quiet tactile score (§8.7, §11).

## §1 Procedure (follow in order)

1. **Inventory.** Probe the footage (resolution, fps, duration, audio). Conform to a constant 30 fps if it is phone VFR.
   - Identify every **setup** (§12.1): desk talking head (medium), a tighter version of it, overhead hands, handheld close / object to the lens, low-angle table level, a seated conversation single, a handheld selfie.
   - Note how much room is above the head in every talking shot. The whole head (hair to chin) must fit at every framing this plan will use (§3.5). Footage tighter than that gets the scale-down fix (§12.3), decided now, not after the stills.
2. **Transcribe** with word timestamps. Captions are the spoken words, sentence case, punctuation kept. Check every name and product against the script. Mark fillers ("uh", "um") and slips ("Those are the four. Five things") for removal from the captions, and slips for removal from the audio (§2 M9).
3. **Choose the format** (§3.0): a sequence of actions → `FMT-STEPS`; an opinion or conversation → `FMT-TALK`; a solo speaker with no B-roll → `FMT-TALK` with the one-person adaptation (§3.7). One line on why.
4. **Segment** the script: `FMT-STEPS` → `HOOK` (result + claim), `STEP-n` (each opened where the step is named), `RECAP`, `CLOSE`. `FMT-TALK` → `HOOK` (the debatable line), `COUNTER`, `TURN` ("I agree… what's interesting…"), `STAKES`, `PEAK`, `CLOSE`.
5. **Mark every action and every keyword** with its onset: each spoken action ("write the dates", "peel this off") needs its hands shot while it is said; each caption phrase gets **one gold word** (§5.3).
6. **Plan the speaker map** (§3.3): for each beat, face (L-1 / L-2 / L-6), hands (L-3 / L-4 / L-5), or the bench card (`L-7`, 1–2 per 60 s) when a step is named while the hands keep working. Rule of thumb: **name it on the face, do it with the hands, opinion on the face, close on the face.** In talk: whoever speaks; whoever reacts on a one-word interjection.
7. **Ideate 3 hooks** (§6) and recommend one. Frame 0 shows the finished result sharp, or the face already speaking. Never an empty or blurred frame 0.
8. **Fill the beat sheet** (§13): one beat per shot, each with layout, pattern, caption phrases with the gold word, chip state, transition, elevated devices, sounds and a face check.
9. **Plan the transition map** (§9.3): every cut, both flashes (if any), the chip on/off frames, the recap ticks. Check the budget (§9.4).
10. **Produce the assets** (§8.6): the user's own B-roll of the real thing (hands, object, screen, photos). Nothing is designed except the captions and the chip. List anything missing.
11. **The 10× pass and the score** (§8.0, §8.7, §11): for every beat, its hero (the hand's action point, the proof detail, the gold word) and how it lands (relay, light, chip roll, rack, freeze, time-lapse, bench card); then score every real-thing event and log the sound ledger. **Attack the plan:** where is a proof detail too small to read? Where does an angle change drop the hand's motion? Where is the face away for too long? Where is a frame mostly empty outside the reset? Where is a sound on a plain face cut or under an opinion line? Fix it in the plan.
12. **Show the creator** the plan and stills (§13.3).
13. **Build, look at your frames (§15), score, render (§16).**

## §2 Craft that never bends
- **The whole face is always visible:** hair to chin, at every framing, punch-in, jump cut and in the bench card; tight footage stays full screen as shot (eyes to chin in; the hair may touch the top edge), never scaled down over its own blurred copy, never a narrow pillar. Captions may cross the face.
- **Text never sits on text:** a caption never over the STEP chip or a word written on the page that the line names.
- **Sound in sync:** every SFX on the frame its picture lands, never on a consonant (slide ±2 f); nothing louder than the voice.
- **True and real:** every object, number and result is the user's own; nothing redrawn or invented.
- **Readable at phone size:** captions 72–84 px (never below 64 px); anything meant to be read ≥ 48 px; the real thing big.
- **Loudness:** −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP; end hard on a complete sentence.

Everything else is direction. You decide; write your reasons into the plan.

### 2.1 The house grammar (what makes it this style)
Not checks: this is what the two reference reels do every time, and why it works. Break one only with a reason in the plan. (The IDs are kept because other sections cite them.)
- M1. **The face is whole whenever it is on screen.** Hair to chin inside the frame at every framing, punch-in, crop and jump cut: head top ≥ 60 px in a tight single, ≥ 170 px in the desk medium. How far a punch-in goes is decided by the face, never by the frame. **(fix: pm2 cut the host's crown off at the top edge at 8–11, 16–21 and 24–35 s, and the guest's beanie at 21–24 and 41–47 s.)** When footage is tighter than the face allows (a tight seated crop, a handheld selfie where hair and chin leave the frame), **keep it full screen as shot and skip the punch-ins** (eyes to chin always in; the hair may touch the top edge); never shrink it over a blurred copy of itself; in the bench card the footage fills the card with the head inside (§12.3). Captions may cross the face or chin.
- M2. **Full frame or absent is home.** Face ↔ hands by hard cut (pm1), person ↔ person by hard cut (pm2). No PiP, split or cut-out of the face (2/2 reels). This edition's one designed exception is the **bench card** (`L-7`, EV-7): a wide card under the work, 1–2 times per 60 s, the face whole inside it.
- M3. **Show the real thing, done by real hands, while it is said.** The voice runs continuously and the B-roll is laid over it (pm1: 391 words, no pause for the picture).
- M4. **Frame 0 is never empty, never a blur:** the finished result sharp, or the face already mid-sentence. **(fix: pm1 opens on 3 motion-blurred frames, f0–2.)** First caption word by **f6**.
- M5. **One gold word per caption phrase** (two only in a same-kind list), the concrete noun or verb of the action, or the idea or feeling in talk; always the biggest thing in the caption. **Never a function word (fix: pm1 highlighted "the").**
- M6. **Readable on a phone.** Body 72–80 px (`FMT-STEPS`) / 76–84 px (`FMT-TALK`), gold ≈ 1.35× (100–116 px), **floor 64 px for any line**, a column up to 900 px: break the phrase instead of shrinking it. The STEP chip ≥ 420×130 px with 64 px mono; the detail that proves a step ≥ 48 px. **(fix: pm1 shrinks long lines to 22–32 px, pm2 to 40–48 px.)**
- M7. **Caption block bottom ≤ y 1500. (fix: pm1's line 2 sits at y 1575–1640, inside Instagram's bottom UI band.)** On the face the block sits just under the chin so the eye meets it right after the face (§5.2); on the hands it keeps its fixed band.
- M8. **Cuts land on word boundaries.** B-roll cuts may fall mid-sentence, never mid-word. Speaker cuts land on the change or 0.2–0.4 s before the next person's first word (pre-lap).
- M9. **Tight speech, natural voice:** 3.6–3.9 words/s, pauses ≤ 0.5 s (pm1 max 0.56 s; pm2 max 0.71 s). Natural fillers may stay in the audio, but **never caption a filler (fix: pm1 captioned "uh,")** and **cut a slip (fix: pm1 kept "Those are the four. Five things")**.
- M10. **The STEP chip lands on every step, on the cut into its first action shot**, never over the face. **(fix: pm1's STEP 5 pops 3.1 s into its shot.)**
- M11. **Shots ≥ 12 f. (fix: pm2's jump cut at 9.67 / 9.80 leaves a 4-frame sliver.)**
- M12. **Audio:** −14 LUFS integrated, **true peak ≤ −1 dBTP (fix: pm1 −0.4, pm2 −0.6 dBTP).** Scored with a whisper-quiet tactile palette (§11); no music.
- M13. **End hard:** picture out ≤ 0.5 s after the last word, no end card, no fade; a 3 f audio fade so a mid-laugh cut doesn't click **(fix: pm2 cuts the laugh at full level).**
- M14. **Facts are real.** Every object, number and result on screen is the user's own.

**Never in this style:**
- N1. A PiP, split, corner box or cut-out of the speaker; the bench card (`L-7`) is the only card, wide, 1–2 times per 60 s (M2).
- N2. Graphics over the face. The only graphics are the captions (which may cross it) and the STEP chip (top band, hands shots only).
- N3. Designed B-roll: icons, stock, screenshots as cards, logos, emoji, diagrams. Everything is filmed (2/2 reels).
- N4. Fast zooms, punch-ins or Ken Burns sweeps inside B-roll; the hands make the movement. This edition's only post moves on the hands are the slow filmic drift (≤ 3 % per shot), the 101.5 % relay settle (EV-2), the rack (EV-5) and the time-lapse ramp (EV-4). The only punch-in on the face is the closer (L-2).
- N5. Flashes, whips, glitches, dissolves, spins or wipes, except the film-burn flash (T-03): ≤ 2 per reel, never on the face. The shutter freeze (EV-6) is an exposure blink (≤ 12 %, 2 f), not a flash.
- N6. A second caption colour; more than one gold hue per reel.
- N7. Bounce, pop, overshoot or exit animations on captions. The gold word's light sweep (EV-1) is light, not motion.
- N8. Sound on a plain face cut, under an opinion line on the face, or on a white caption word. Every SFX belongs to a picture event on the real thing (§11).
- N9. A store name, logo, link or brand graphic on screen. Where-to-buy lines stay spoken asides on the face. The object's own label may show because it is the object.
- N10. Music, ever: no bed, track, drone or loop under the voice, in any format (the creator's direction, 12 Oct 2026).
- N11. A static object with no hands and no action for more than ~1.2 s outside a deliberate reset (T-04).
- N12. A pale picture: desaturated, faded, milky or lifted-black footage (§4.4).
- N13. A narrow pillar, or the footage shrunk over its own blur: a tight selfie stays full screen or fills a card ≥ 940 px wide (§12.3).
- N14. Comedy, cartoon or crowd sounds, voice memes, risers, cash registers, glitches, whips or digital beeps. The warmth is the voice and the hands (§11).

### 3.7 ADAPTATION: one person talking to their own camera
> **This section is an adaptation, not an observation.** pm1 is a single creator with a second camera setup for his hands, and pm2 is two people at a podcast table with two cameras. Most users of this style film themselves alone, often with one camera. The grammar below uses **only devices seen in pm1 and pm2**, re-assigned to one person.

**A. `FMT-STEPS`, solo (the closest fit; it is already one person).**
- The face slot is the user's talking-head take (desk, chair or selfie), full frame, L-1 rules.
- **The hands slot is taken by the user's own real thing:** their hands on the object (overhead or close, as in L-3 / L-4), their phone or laptop screen filmed or recorded, their printed or on-screen photos, their notebook, their desk. Whatever it is, it fills the frame, full bleed, the way pm1's notebook does. Never a card, never a box.
- A screen recording takes the L-4 slot: crop it to the region where the action happens so that it fills 9:16 and its text reads on a phone (the same job as pm1 holding the page to the lens). If the action region is wider than 9:16 allows at a readable size, push in to the part that matters (a slow drift across it if both ends matter), or set it full width in a designed box on the warm ground (a soft shadow, a 2 px gold hairline, the wood or paper texture around it), never over a blurred copy of itself. No zoom moves inside it beyond the slow drift (N4): if two regions matter, cut between two crops on a word boundary, like pm1 cuts between overhead and close, or rack between them (EV-5).
- The STEP chip, the recap tick, the name-on-face / do-with-hands rule, the empty-surface reset (for a screen: the empty desktop or empty page held ~1 s before the next state) and the time-skip flash all apply unchanged.
- **The bench card** (`L-7`) fits screen work well: the recording on top, the face below, when the user explains while the recording plays. A screen's typing may take a keyboard sound from the hands pool (§11.1) if the recording is silent.
- If the user has **no B-roll at all**, do not fake one: use `FMT-TALK` solo below.

**B. `FMT-TALK`, solo (the conversation grammar for one speaker).**
- **The two cameras become two framings of the one take:** L-1 (the as-shot medium) and L-2 (the ~120% punch, the closer framing from pm1). Cut between them where pm2 cuts between people: **on a breath, on a sentence change, or on a turn of thought** ("I agree… what's interesting…", "but…"). Each framing keeps the whole head (L-2 punches only as far as the face allows).
- If the footage is already a tight handheld selfie (no room to punch), keep one framing and cut on breaths only, the way pm2 jump-cuts inside one shot at 9.67 to remove a stumble. Shots ≥ 12 f (M11).
- **Reaction beats:** pm2 holds the listener for 1.1–1.5 s on "mm-hmm" and "I know". Alone, the reaction is the speaker's own: a nod, a laugh, a hand to the chin, a pause before the strong line (pm1 jump-cuts to a hand-to-chin beat at 60.90 inside the face shot). Keep one or two of these silent or one-word beats, 1.0–1.5 s, as their own cut, instead of trimming them away with the pauses.
- **Rhythm:** shot length follows the emotion: 4–6 s per framing while the argument is laid out, 1.2–2.5 s per framing at the peak.
- **Gold words carry the escalation:** topic nouns early ("printing", "photos", "photographer"), emotion words at the peak ("lost", "awful", "scary", "buried", "drowning").
- **The hook** is the most debatable line, spoken at frame 0, face already mid-thought (HF-2).
- **The end** is a hard cut on the last line or on the speaker's own laugh (§6.5).
- Captions: the `FMT-TALK` sizes (76–84 px body, 104–116 px gold, just under the chin, ending ≤ y 1500).
- No music (N10); a nearly dry score (§11: a soft air on the framing change at the turn, one sub under the stakes line); no graphics but the captions.

### 4.3 Meaning of colour
- **Gold italic** = "this is the word": the action, the object, the feeling. One per phrase.
- **White** = everything he is saying.
- **Black chip** = "where we are in the steps".
- **Teal → white → warm flash** = time has passed or a chapter turned.
- **The object's own colour** (a red string, a coral note) = the only saturated colour; it pulls the eye to the thing.

**Rules:** one gold hue per reel (amber for `FMT-STEPS`, lemon for `FMT-TALK`); never a second accent colour in the captions; no colour-coding of speakers (pm2 uses the same caption for both people).

### 4.4 Footage look: warm and rich, never pale
- **Warm and rich.** Deep blacks (crushed, a hint of cool in the shadows), warm glowing mids, skin with life in it, highlights rolled off softly (≈ 230/255), shallow depth of field everywhere. The references measured fairly muted (mean chroma ~21 on the face shot, ~45 on the rug in pm1; ~32 in pm2): **keep their warmth, not their mutedness.** Saturation natural or up to +10 %; never lowered.
- The talking head is a dark room with warm practicals (bookshelves) behind; the top third is almost black.
- **For the user's footage:** keep it as shot, or a touch richer. Match exposure and white balance between the face and the hands shots so they read as one room. The gentle filmic grade (warm mids, rolled highlights, deep blacks) goes equally on every shot; it never desaturates, fades or lifts the blacks.
- **Never pale:** no faded, milky or desaturated look on any shot; no blurred fill behind his footage; the film burn (T-03) is a short warm event, never a grade. The object's own colour (a red string, a coral note, a kraft cover) stays at full strength: it is the eye's target.
- **This edition's filmic finish:** fine grain ≤ 3 %, a soft halation on bright practicals, a vignette ≤ 10 % on hands shots only (§10.4).

### 5.4 Caption animation (both formats, measured)
- **Block in:** hard swap; the old block disappears on the frame the new block's first word appears (no clear frames).

- **Exit:** none; the block stays until the next block's first word.
- **Across cuts:** a block **continues across a cut** if the phrase continues (pm2 21.70, 35.30; pm1 91.13). Captions follow the voice, not the picture.
- **Across the flash:** captions stay on top through T-03, visible through the white (pm1 f1035–1042).

### 5.5 Phrase breaking
- Break at commas, full stops and before a new clause. 2–7 words per block (pm1), 2–6 (pm2); house **3–6**.
- A block holds 1–2 lines. Line 1 is often the short lead-in ("This is my *system*"), line 2 the follow-on ("and how I am productive.") — **but line 2 is never shrunk** (fix, M6): if it doesn't fit at ≥ 64 px within 900 px, it becomes the next block.
- Never caption a filler ("uh", "um") and never caption a slip that was cut from the audio (M9).
- Spell names and products as written in the script; keep the speaker's casual grammar ("Now you got a bookmark").

## §6 Hook system

### 6.0 The 10× hook (this edition)
- **Frame 0:** the finished result, sharp, in his hands and already moving with a slow 2 % drift (`FMT-STEPS`), or his whole face mid-sentence (`FMT-TALK`); the first word on by f6. Never a blur, never black.
- **The first gold word catches the light by ~1.1 s** (EV-1), with `short-shine-2025-08-27-06-45-01-utc-short-shine-4` very low under it.
- **`FMT-STEPS`: three shots of the finished thing in the first ~4.5 s,** each joined by a match-on-action relay (EV-2) on a word of the triple claim; the third raised to the lens with a rack to the detail (EV-5). Then the hard cut to the face for the constraint.
- **The shutter freeze on the claim** (EV-6, optional): the finished page held to the lens freezes 8–10 f on the claim's last gold word, then plays on. It says "this is the result" like a photograph being taken.
- **≥ 6 changes in the first 3 s** (pm1 had 2 cuts + 9 word reveals): relays, gold-word light, the drift, the rack, the words.

### 6.1 What the hook must do in about 3 seconds
1. **Show the result or the face, sharp, on frame 0.** pm1's first readable image is the *finished*, filled-in tracker (f3, fix: f0); pm2's is the host's face already mid-sentence.

3. **Open a loop:** *how* the finished thing got made (answered step by step under the chip), or a debate the viewer wants to take a side in (answered never; the comments do it).

Picture, caption and voice say the same thing: the result on screen, the claim in the voice, the gold word on the noun that matters ("*system*", "*printing*").

## §7 Structure devices

### 7.0 The shape of each format
**`FMT-STEPS` (pm1, 110 s):**
HOOK, result + claim (0.00, H-1) → constraint on the face (5.87) → STEP 1, the main build, 27 s (7.73, chip 1, reset at 30.60) → time skip to the filled result (34.63, T-03) → STEP 2 (37.65, T-03, chip on the first revealed frame) → STEP 3 named on the face, then done (40.43 → chip 46.80) → opinion on the face + payoff macro (49.90) → STEP 4 named and justified on the face (57.00, 9.9 s → chip 66.90) → time frame on the face + reset ritual (69.03) → STEP 5 named on the face (77.40 → low angle, chip) → finished in hand at the desk (88.63) → RECAP, one 13 s take, chip 1→5 (91.13) → CLOSE on L-2 (104.17). Full shot list in §14.1.

**`FMT-TALK` (pm2, 47 s):** HOOK question (0) → COUNTER (5.03) → "that's a great point" (≈15) → TURN "I agree… what's interesting…" (15.77) → STAKES "is your work gone?" (21.70) → the fear (24.47) → rapid back-and-forth (35.30) → PEAK "buried alive / drowning" (41.30) → CLOSE on the laugh (46.13).

### 7.2 The step ritual (one recipe per step, identical every time)
1. **Name the step on the face** (L-1), unless it follows straight from the previous action (steps 1 and 2 began on the hands).
   - Steps 3–5: "I found these sheets…", "But I cover this with the fourth thing I do…", "Something that can be very useful… is adding a bookmark."
2. **Cut to the hands on the first action word.** STEP chip on the same frame (SD-1).
3. **Process shots**, 1.5–7 s each, alternating overhead (L-3), close (L-4) and low (L-5) by match-on-action cuts on word boundaries. One gold word per phrase on each action ("*peel*", "*thread*", "*Double* knot it").
4. **Reset** (SD-4) between sub-steps if the object changes state.
5. **Back to the face** for the opinion or the reason (pm1 49.90 "I mostly use it for *photos*"; 69.03 "If it's been two weeks").
6. **Payoff close:** a macro of the finished part (pm1 52.5–57.0: the photo sitting in the new pocket).

### 7.3 Open loops (retention)
- **The finished-result loop** (`FMT-STEPS`): the viewer sees the filled tracker at f0 and the blank page at 7.73; the loop is closed by the time-skip flash (34.63) and again by the recap.
- **The count loop:** the chip counts 1→5 and its dot row fills (EV-3); the recap repeats the count in one take; the closer says "Those are the five things".
- **The debate loop** (`FMT-TALK`): the hook question is never answered; the stakes keep rising ("is your work gone?"). Leave it open.
- Every loop is paid on screen with the real thing (M14).

## §8 B-roll system

### 8.0 The 10× layer (what the best motion designer adds to every real thing)
**Animation principles, applied to this style:**
| Principle | How it shows up here |
|---|---|
| **Anticipation** | The cut waits for the hand to begin its move (cut on the motion's peak, never before it); the chip's numeral blurs 1 f before it rolls; the room tone dips 2 f before the shutter freeze |
| **Follow-through & overlap** | The relay's incoming angle settles 101.5 → 100 % over 4 f; the gold word's glow trails its glyphs by 2 f; the bench card trails the face by 2 f and settles |
| **Squash & weight** | Paper, pen and string have weight: the cut lands on the press, the knot, the stick; the chip's numeral thumps 1.0 → 1.04 → 1.0; the finished object at the lens lands with a 1-frame 1 % compression |
| **Arcs** | The bench card travels on an arc; the hand's arc carries across the relay; the gold light sweep runs on a 20° diagonal |
| **Slow in / slow out** | The drift, the time-lapse ramp and the dock ease in and out; the cuts stay hard (they are the rhythm) |
| **Staging** | One hero per frame: the hand's action point (the pen tip, the knot, the sticker edge). The rack (EV-5) softens everything else; the caption keeps clear of it |
| **Secondary action** | The slow drift (≤ 3 %, alternating direction shot to shot), a page lifting in a breath of air, dust in the window light, the gold glow breathing once |
| **Timing** | Process shots 1.5–7 s while the hands work; the face 1.5–10 s; the reset holds ~1 s (the breath); the recap one 13 s take; 0.5 s of quiet before the payoff |
| **Exaggeration** | Once per reel the reveal goes all the way: weeks of pages blurring into two seconds, or the finished thing at the lens with the focus pull and the shutter |
| **Appeal** | Warm light, real paper, real ink, real hands; filmic, rich and tactile; nothing glossy, nothing cartoon |

**Design hierarchy (every frame):** 1. the hand's action point (or the face, on talking beats) · 2. the gold word · 3. the white caption · 4. the STEP chip and its dots · 5. everything else, softened by depth.

**Effects toolkit:** shallow depth of field and the rack (EV-5); the warm light sweep on the gold word (EV-1); the film burn (T-03); the shutter freeze's exposure blink (EV-6); motion blur only inside the time-lapse ramp (EV-4); fine grain ≤ 3 %, soft halation on practicals, a vignette ≤ 10 % on hands shots; a soft shadow under the bench card. Never: glossy flares, light-leak overlays, glitches, RGB splits, whips, spins, stickers, emoji, arrows, icons.

3. Hand-off: a new angle on the same action (match-on-action), or the object leaves and returns in its next state (SD-4).

### 8.5 Density and variety

### 8.6 Asset rules

## §9 Transition system (`T`)

A **hard cut** is the default and the voice carries the continuity. A **flash** marks time passing. A **reset** is done by the hands. Every transition must be one of these IDs.

### 9.1 Transition library (30 fps)
| ID | Transition | Frames | Recipe | SFX |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **T-01** | **Hard cut over the voice** (default) | 0 | A cut between hands shots, or hands ↔ face, on a word boundary; mid-sentence is fine because the voice continues. Often match-on-action (overhead → close). Captions continue across it. This edition: every match-on-action cut is a relay (EV-2) | the action's own sound (§11.1); a plain face cut stays dry |
| **T-02** | **Cut to the face** (aside) | 0 | Hard cut to L-1 on the first word of an aside, opinion, source or step name; back to the hands on the first action word | none (face cuts stay dry) |
| **T-03** | **Film-burn flash** (signature, rare) | 8 | Runs on 2s (a 15 fps burn): **f1–2** the outgoing shot gets a teal-blue wash (mid `#738C96`, shadows `#26485D`). **f3** a white burn rolls down from the top: top `#FDFDFA` grading to teal `#2A738F` at the bottom. **f4–5** full white `#FDFDFA`; **the cut sits inside the white.** **f6–7** the incoming shot is revealed from the top with a warm red-orange tint (`#65362F` → `#B36F51`) while the bottom is still white. **f8** clean incoming shot. ### 9.4 Transition budget
- **T-03 film-burn flash: ≤ 2 per reel**, both around one boundary (a time skip and the chapter right after it), never on the face, never within the hook. pm1 uses exactly 2 in 110 s, 3.0 s apart.

- **T-08 closer:** 1, at the end.

- **T-10 / T-11 bench card:** 1–2 per 60 s, never in the hook or the closer.

## §10 Motion tokens, camera, layers, finishing

### 10.1 Motion tokens (30 fps)
- Block in: hard swap on the new block's first word. White word: 2 f fade in place. Gold word: 3 f fade + sharpen + glow (optional settle ≤ 2%, guess). Timing: the audible word. Exit: none.
- Chip: hard on with the cut, off 4–5 f before the next cut; recap numeral hard-swaps.
- T-03: 8 f on 2s. Reset hold: ~1 s. Closer punch: a hard cut to ~120%, face-limited.
- Minimum shot 12 f (fix). Hook first word by f6. End ≤ 0.5 s after the last word, 3 f audio fade.

This edition's motion, on top: the slow filmic drift (≤ 3 % per hands shot, ease in-out, alternating direction), the relay settle (101.5 → 100 %, 4 f), the gold light sweep (10–12 f) and glow breath (14 f), the chip roll (6 f) and dot bloom (2 f), the rack (8–10 f), the shutter freeze (8–12 f hold, 2 f blink), the time-lapse ramp (1.5–2.5 s), the dock (10–12 f) and the return (8–10 f). Everything else is the hands, the page and the people.

### 10.2 Camera
- **Desk talking head (L-1):** tripod, eye level, ~50–85 mm feel, shallow focus, dark room with warm shelves. Static; no Ken Burns, no punch alternation. Jump cuts keep the framing (T-07).
- **Closer (L-2):** the same take punched ~120%, once, at the end; the face decides how far.
- **Overhead (L-3):** locked or near-locked, top-down; both hands in frame.
- **Handheld close (L-4):** natural drift; the object is moved toward the lens, the lens doesn't chase it.
- **Low angle (L-5):** lens near the surface, very shallow.
- **Conversation single (L-6):** tripod, eye level, a tight crop from a wider podcast camera; a slight operator reframe between takes is fine. Head top ≥ 60 (fix).
- **No fast moves in post** anywhere (N4); the slow drift, the relay settle, the rack and the ramp are the only ones.

### 10.3 Layer order (back to front)
1. The warm ground (wood or paper texture, a soft vignette) behind a boxed screen recording (§3.7); never a blurred copy of any footage or recording
2. Footage (face or hands), full frame; in `L-7`, the work on top and the face card below (soft shadow, 2 px gold hairline)
3. STEP chip and its dot row (hands shots only)
4. `CAP-WORD` + `CAP-GOLD` with the gold light sweep (EV-1) (always on top, through cuts and flashes)
5. T-03 flash overlay (the captions sit above it in the reference: they stay visible through the white)

### 10.4 Finishing
- **The references used no grain, vignette or bloom.** This edition's filmic finish: fine grain ≤ 3 %, a soft halation on bright practicals, a vignette ≤ 10 % on hands shots only, the glow behind the gold word. The face gets the grain only.
- **Match the face and hands shots** in exposure and white balance; the gentle filmic grade (§4.4) equally on all shots: warm and rich, never pale.
- **Typos:** spell-check every on-screen word against the script; names and products exact.
- **Delivery:** 1080×1920, 30 fps constant.

## §11 Sound design (D4)

**No music bed, ever.** Sound is SFX only (the creator's direction, 12 Oct 2026): never a music track, bed, drone or loop under the voice.

### 11.0 What the references do (measured)

|---|---|---|---|
| `FMT-STEPS` (pm1) | Continuous, dry, close; speech median −21.7 dBFS RMS (40 ms windows) | None designed. One paper riffle at 0.08–0.24 s (−15.6 dBFS in 4–10 kHz) on the opening flip, probably the footage's own sound | −14.8 LUFS, LRA 5.1 LU, true peak **−0.4 dBTP (too hot)** |

### 11.2 The SFX ledger (Naman's rule)
- **Any file at most 2× in a reel,** except the one **list cue** (here the single keystroke `keyboard-typing-2025-02-18-23-33-14-utc-mountain-audio-typing-sound-4` on every STEP chip and recap tick).
- **No file on two consecutive cues;** rotate within each pool. The footage's own sound doesn't count.
- **Density:** `FMT-STEPS` about one cue every 1.6–2.4 s on hands beats, each tied to a picture event (an action, a chip, a rack, a freeze, a burn); the face's asides and opinions stay dry. `FMT-TALK` at most one cue every 6–10 s.
- **Never more than two cues overlapping;** never on a trigger word's consonant (shift ±2 f).
- The paper and pen are texture; the chip key, the shutter and the reveal are what the viewer feels.

### 11.3 Silence and the mix
- **Go quiet (no new SFX) for 0.4–0.6 s before the reveal after the time skip and before the closer's shutter,** then land it. The face's opinion lines play dry.
- **Laughter is kept** at its natural level, limited only by the true-peak ceiling.
- Voice: high-pass 80 Hz, gentle de-ess, light compression; two-pass loudnorm to **−14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP** (fixes both references). LRA up to ~10 LU is fine for talk.
- **End:** the audio stops with the picture, with a 3 f fade (M13). The voice is always the loudest thing; every SFX sits under it.

### 12.2 Crop table (starting values)
Face framings are in §3.5. In addition:
- **L-2 closer:** scale ≈ 1.20 about the face centre; if 1.20 would push the hair above y 120, use the largest scale that doesn't.
- **Landscape source for a 9:16 single:** crop around the face so the head top lands at 60–120 and the face is 40–48% of the width.
- **Hands shots:** as shot, full frame, no added movement.
- **Any face shot too tight** (hair or chin outside the frame): the tight-footage rule (§12.3).

### 12.3 Tight footage (tighter than the face allows): full screen or the card, never its own blur
This is the fix for the case that keeps failing: **a tight handheld selfie close-up (or a tight podcast crop) where the hair and the chin leave the frame.**
1. **Keep it full screen as shot.** Eyes to chin always in; the hair may touch the top edge. Move the chip, the gold word and the text clear of the face; never move or shrink the video for them.
2. **When the whole head must show,** use the bench card (`L-7`): the footage fills the card (cover, cropped on the face, ≥ 940 px wide) on the warm work shot, with the head inside.
3. **Never** shrink the footage inside the frame over a blurred copy of itself, and never pad it with blurred borders (Naman, 13 Oct: "either full screen or enclosed in a box").
4. **If the hair left the frame while recording,** keep it full screen (eyes to chin in), keep that moment short, and prefer another part of the take for long beats.
5. **Skip punch-ins (L-2) on such footage**; cut on breaths instead (§3.7). Captions may cross the face; nothing else is checked.

### 12.4 Frame rate
- Deliver at **30 fps** (both references are 30 fps). Conform phone VFR footage to constant 30 fps first. Conform any 24/25 fps B-roll to 30 fps before cutting.

### 12.5 Audio
- One clean voice track per speaker (a lav or a podcast mic). Keep room tone between lines in `FMT-TALK`; don't gate it to silence.
- Keep the hands footage's own sound under the voice at a low level if it's clean (paper, pen).

## §15 Look at your frames
Not a checklist; the questions the best editor asks while scrubbing:
- **The footage frame:** is every video, recording and picture full screen or in a designed box it fills, never a sharp rectangle over its own blur, never padded with blurred bars?
- **Frame 0, sound off:** is the finished thing sharp and moving (or his whole face mid-sentence), with the first word on? Does the first gold word catch the light by ~1 s?
- **Every step:** was it named on the face (or in the bench card) and done by the hands on its first action word, with the chip counting and its dot lighting? Does every angle change carry the hand's motion through the cut?
- **Every proof:** can I read the detail on a phone? Did it get the rack, and the first finished result its shutter?
- **The speaker:** the whole head in every face frame, the caption just under the chin, back at least once per step and never away for more than ~20 s? If the bench card is used, is it wide and the work above it big?
- **Scale:** shrink the still to thumbnail size. Are the gold word and the action point the first things I see? Is any frame mostly empty or blurred background (outside the deliberate reset), or a narrow pillar?
- **Colour:** warm and rich, deep blacks, the object's colour strong? Anything pale comes out.
- **Sound:** paper, pen, keys, focus, shutter, soft air, each on its picture; no file overused; the face's lines dry; quiet before the reveal and the closer; no music?
- **Vibe:** calm, tactile, expert, warm. Anything slick, glossy, cartoon or busy comes out.

## §16 Build + render
- **Pipeline:** the plugin's edit skill: plan, draw every frame in `reel.js` (`frame(t)`), look at stills, score (`plan/sounds.json`, then `veos sfx`), storyboard, render.
- **Fonts (bundled):** Inter Tight (600, captions), Instrument Serif Italic (gold keyword), JetBrains Mono (700, STEP chip; fallback Courier Prime Bold), Plus Jakarta Sans (fallback body).
- **Helpers you'll want** (pure functions of t): `Footage(t)` (hard-cut list, per-shot scale/offset, and `ScaleDownFill(shot, scale)` for §12.3); `Captions(blocks, words)` (pre-laid blocks, ≤ 900 px, 64 px floor, under the chin on the face, 2 f / 3 f fades on the audible onset, independent of the cuts); `StepChip(n, onF, offF)` + `RecapTick(ticks)`; `FilmBurn(cutF)` (the 8 f T-03 overlay, under the captions); `Grade(shot)` (warm and rich, identical on every shot, never pale); `Drift(shot)` (≤ 3 %), `Relay(cutF)` (EV-2), `GoldLight(word)` (EV-1), `ChipRoll(n)` + `StepDots(n, total)` (EV-3), `TimeLapse(range, ramp)` (EV-4), `Rack(rect)` (EV-5), `ShutterFreeze(f)` (EV-6), `BenchCard(t)` (`L-7`, T-10 / T-11), `Grain`, `Halation`. All pure functions of t.
- **Final:** 1080×1920, 30 fps, H.264, AAC, −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP; check a contact sheet of the final file: the whole head in every face frame, every caption line ≥ 64 px, the chip on every step.
